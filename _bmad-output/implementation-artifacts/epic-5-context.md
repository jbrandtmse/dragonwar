# Epic 5 Context: Art Passes — the Dragon and the Table

<!-- Generated from planning artifacts. Regenerate with compile-epic-context if planning docs change. -->

## Goal

Replace the bare placeholder scene with a table a player can actually see and read: first flat-shaded placeholder meshes for every visible collision body and moving mechanism, then phased art passes. These cover the sculpted Dragon, rustic hand-painted playfield art under saturated insert lenses, the cabinet and backbox at real scale for the Walk-up silhouette, mechanisms, plastics, ramp and guides that look like hardware, and dot-matrix Backglass art that sets up the dragon-and-knight fiction. Art is the project's largest unbudgeted risk, so this epic may trail epics 1–4 and never blocks them. Each pass swaps visuals behind the fixed node contract and lands with its attribution entries.

## Stories

- Story 5.0a: Visible placeholder geometry
- Story 5.1: The Dragon
- Story 5.2: Playfield art and materials
- Story 5.3: Cabinet, backbox and glass
- Story 5.4: Mechanisms, plastics, ramp and guides
- Story 5.5: Backglass art and DMD frames

## Requirements & Constraints

- **Stylized at real dimensions.** Simplified geometry and hand-painted textures, with real proportions and real lighting behaviour. Photoreal detail, triangle count and texture resolution are explicitly not goals. Realism lives in proportions and light.
- **Triangle budget:** single LOD, 500–2,000 triangles per object (the Dragon ≤ 2,000). No LOD chains.
- **Legibility:** from the one fixed view, both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp and the DRAGON bank must be legible. The Backglass stays visible as a strip at the top.
- **The Dragon** is the protagonist. It crouches off-centre with the Lock lane between its legs and the Mouth as the Lock's eject. It has mouth open/close plus one hit reaction, and no idle animation. The mouth is fully open before the first ball of a Mouth eject spawns.
- **Inserts** are lights beneath a translucent playfield, seen through a lens and cup, never decals. They are saturated. Muted or earth-tone inserts are rejected. Every insert must read clearly against the art at the fixed camera distance.
- **Walk-up:** the cabinet, backbox, glass, legs and lockdown bar have real-machine silhouette and scale. The camera frames the backbox, then descends past the glass.
- **Backglass:** animated, pixelated dot-matrix frames at the Backglass resolution. The war-fiction Attract sequence must convey a dragon and a knight with no text instructions, judged by the stranger link test. The score must stay legible during and after sequences.
- **Performance and size:** stable 60 FPS on a 2022 mid-range laptop GPU. The compressed initial payload is ≤ 20 MB, enforced in CI, and first playable Walk-up is within 10 s at 50 Mbps. Texture memory and lighting are the real costs, not geometry.
- **Provenance is a hard gate:** every asset gets an `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` entry *before* the file lands.
  - Record generated assets with the tool and date. Record author-made ones as author-made.
  - Verify licences at source, including fonts.
  - `pinball-parts` is CC BY-SA. Exclude its one NC-SA node group.
  - No NC, unlicensed or GPL-2.0-only material, and nothing from commercial machines.
  - New source files carry the GPL-3.0 header.
- **Feel must not regress:** the Dragon's Lawlor miss-test entry in `docs/feel-test.md` is re-run after the Dragon replaces its placeholder.

## Technical Decisions

- **The Blender source owns all geometry.** `assets/src/dragonwar.blend` is the sole owner of every position, mesh and switch zone. `tools/export.py` validates it and writes `public/assets/dragonwar.glb` (presentation) and `dragonwar.collision.json` (physics).
  - The validator checks node names (`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`, unique, one material each) and every `lightgroup`, `surface` and `phys_material` against a dump of `TABLE`.
  - Both loaders fail fast on a missing node or an unknown property.
  - The Story 5.0a placeholder geometry is generated by `tools/make-placeholder-blend.py`, from the same authoring source as collision so the two cannot drift.
- **Node contract** (art replaces visuals behind the same names):
  - `col_` is invisible collision and the only thing the ball hits. `sw_` is switch zones. `vis_` is non-collidable visuals. `l_` is inserts, with lens and cup below the surface. Mechanisms are named as their device.
  - Exactly two top-level nodes, `playfield_root` and `cabinet_root`, plus `pivot_pitch`.
  - Static meshes carry `TEXCOORD_1` (lightmap UV) and a `lightgroup`.
  - The playfield material carries a translucency mask that matches every `l_` position.
- **Frames:** geometry is authored unpitched in the table frame (mm, right-handed, origin bottom-left, Y up the playfield, Z toward the glass). Pitch is a presentation rotation of `playfield_root` about `pivot_pitch`; `cabinet_root` stays level. Only the three conversions in `frames.ts` may convert units or axes. The Babylon scene is right-handed.
- **Moving visuals follow the snapshot.** Flipper angle, target up/down, spinner angle and plunger travel come from the latest physics snapshot, with no interpolation. The Dragon rig animates only from `show_dragon_mouth_open` and the hit-reaction shows, so there is one truth.
- **Lighting:** inserts are driven as emissive plus a dynamic light beneath a transmissive lens behind `LampDriver`. The insert intensity is tuned so the role colour is preserved and not clipped to white. The `gi_backbox`, `gi_cabinet` and `gi_arch` architectural channels light the cabinet and backbox art. The per-group bake is a later swap that must not require renaming meshes.
- **Backglass** is a DMD renderer in `presentation/backglass`. Sequences are selected and timed in presentation from payload-complete events. Rules never format text, and English literals live only there.
- **Boundaries:**
  - This epic writes assets and presentation (`presentation/scene`, `presentation/mechanisms`, `presentation/backglass`), never `src/sim/`.
  - `presentation/**` imports only `sim/contracts` and `sim/table`.
  - Device-name string literals outside `sim/table/dragonwar.ts` and `test/**` are lint errors.
- **Hashes:** visual-only changes must not move any physics or rules state hash. A header-only re-record of the goldens' asset hash is expected when the model changes. `NullEngine` rasterises nothing, so visibility claims need a real browser capture (Story 5.0a's integration AC).
- **Conventions:** `assets/src/` holds editable sources and `public/assets/` holds exported glb, json and textures. Files use `kebab-case.ts`. Only `host/` logs.

## Cross-Story Dependencies

- **5.0a comes first.** It creates the `vis_` placeholders that 5.1–5.4 replace. It also covers the visible half of the dropped-target state and makes flippers and plunger visible.
- **5.1 needs Story 3.3 (Epic 3)** for the Dragon mouth and hit-reaction rig and shows. It must keep `col_dragon_body`, `sw_lock_lane` and `bd_lock` names. A ledger note says `col_dragon_body` does not exist in the committed geometry, so reconcile the names.
- **5.2 overlaps Story 4.2 (Epic 4)**, inserts as lights through cups. Lens positions and the mask must agree.
- **5.3 needs Story 4.6 (Epic 4)**, the Walk-up camera, to frame the backbox and glass.
- **5.5 builds on the Story 2.6 DMD renderer.** Its cues sit alongside Story 4.5's Backglass cues.
- **Epic 3 runs in parallel.** Both epics re-record the same golden replay headers (`test/replays/**`), so coordinate those edits.
- **Routed geometry defects may need collision edits** despite the "scaffolding unchanged" intent. Changing `col_`/`sw_` moves physics hashes and needs goldens re-recorded deliberately:
  - DW-161: pop-bumper skirt zones overlap.
  - DW-159: the post-protrusion gate.
  - DW-142: a bare shooter-lane divider end.
  - DW-258: a ball sinks at the Ramp entrance.
