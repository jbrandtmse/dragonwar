## Intent

**Problem:** Nothing in `GameState` ever moves. `phase` is written once as the literal `'attract'` (`src/sim/loop/index.ts:230`) and never transitions; `PlayerState` is a fully-declared type with **zero constructions anywhere in `src/`**; `players` is always `[]`, `rng` always `0`, `modes` always `[]`, and `hardwareEnabled` is an inert field with **zero readers**. A ball reaches the playfield only through the dev-only `pulseCoil('c_trough_eject')` escape hatch (`src/sim/loop/index.ts:374-376`, whose own header names this story as its replacement) or a golden's declared coil prologue. Separately, `GameState.machine.deviceSlots` is written by `sim/loop` **after** `rules.step()` returns (`src/sim/loop/index.ts:351-355`), a live AD-7 violation (`DW-70`) that `pnpm check:ad7` has named in red for the whole epic.

**Approach:** Introduce the ball controller as the owner of the real ball lifecycle — Start creates a game and a player from `GameStart`, further Start presses add players up to four before ball 1 ends, the controller alone pulses `c_trough_eject` to serve, a drain ends the ball and rotates to the next player or the next ball, and the last player's last ball ends the game — and, in the same change, make `machine.deviceSlots` derived **inside** `rules.step()` so AD-7 finally holds, flipping `pnpm check:ad7` from its deliberate red to green while rewriting the in-suite wrapper into a gate whose red is still demonstrable.

**Why the new AD-7 gate is identity-based — and why it must now import `sim/loop`.** After the fix, comparing rules-derived slots against physics's own view is a *fidelity* check, not an *ownership* check: it passes just as well if someone re-adds the loop's copy. Reference identity does not — `physics/machine.ts:478-484` allocates a fresh object and fresh arrays on **every read**, so the moment `deviceSlots: machine.deviceSlots` returns, identity differs on every tick. **The correction this pass found: the existing harness states at `:55` that it deliberately avoids importing `sim/loop`, driving `machine.step` + `rules.step` by hand instead. A harness that never runs the loop cannot observe the loop's copy line at all**, so the identity assertion would have been green under its own named mutation — a gate whose red cannot be demonstrated. Task 8 therefore requires the rewrite to drive `createLoop().advance()` and assert on `snapshot.game.machine.deviceSlots`. Two further constraints follow and are stated deliberately rather than left as traps: the gate must target the **per-tick** copy, not the boot seed (`initialMachineState` reads the getter once and then carries that object by reference, so a boot-only regression would slip past an across-ticks identity check); and the fix must **preserve structural sharing** — returning the same `machine` when nothing changed, as `ball-controller.ts:39-41` and `rules/index.ts:97` already do — because a derivation that reallocates `deviceSlots` every tick makes the identity gate red *without* the mutation and therefore worthless.

**Why `bd_shooter` needs a closing-edge event at all.** DW-70 cannot be closed without one: `machine.deviceSlots` is a **total** `Record` over all three devices (`state.ts:72`, bound at `names.ts:65`, so a partial derivation is a type error), and the rules layer today has *zero* signal for the shooter lane's occupied state — the closing edge was traced through all six stages of `devices/index.ts` and is observationally silent. Reusing `device_ball_entered/_left` needs no AD-19 amendment (the spine enumerates them at `:228`), but it **falsifies `applyDeviceEvents`'s stated premise** that every `device_ball_entered` means a ball left play — hence the parking guard in task 3(a). The alternative (a new `device_ball_arrived` event) *would* need an AD-19 amendment for a strictly worse outcome.

**How the narrowed mode-teardown clause is made falsifiable without pre-empting Story 3.1.** `epics.md:1383-1392` narrows this story's obligation to what it can falsify: a stub mode present in `modes[]` before the drain is gone after it, **its teardown observed strictly before `ball_ended`**, with a mutation that reorders the two. The measured premises hold at this tree — `src/sim/rules/modes/` does not exist, `modes: []` is the only value ever assigned anywhere, nothing reads `state.modes`, and `_will_stop` appears nowhere under `src/` or `test/`. The design problem is that `RulesStepResult` returns **one** end-of-tick `state` and one `events[]` per tick, so `expect(drain.state.modes).toEqual([])` is satisfied identically whether teardown ran before or after `ball_ended` was pushed — **a reorder mutation leaves it green**, which is exactly the vacuity the amendment exists to kill. Intra-tick *event* order is the codebase's established discriminator (`test/rules-devices.test.ts:250-268`, `test/loop.test.ts:103-127`), but using it here would require authoring a mode-stop member of the closed `SemanticEvent` union — and **AD-8's Rule already reserves that vocabulary** (`mode_<name>_will_start / _starting / _started` and `_will_stop / _stopping / _stopped`), with `epics.md:1618-1644` giving Story 3.1 the convention and the identical assertion. Authoring a competing generic event here would be a Rule 20 spine decision this stage may not take. **AC 5 therefore pins the ordering through state attribution instead:** teardown credits the stub to the **ending** player's `modesPlayed` — an existing declared `PlayerState` field (`state.ts:105`) with an unambiguous meaning — and `ball_ended` carries `player`, so a teardown that runs after the rotation mis-attributes the stub to `players[1]`. That is a real defect class, it is two-sided, and it reddens under precisely "a mutation that reorders the two". The **event-level** `_will_stop` broadcast lands in full at Story 3.1, where modes are real and AD-8's naming applies — nothing is dropped; it is asserted where it is real.

**`phase` is `'game'`, not `'playing'`.** `epics.md:1372` says "phase becomes `playing`"; the shipped union (`state.ts:27`) is `'attract' | 'game' | 'highscore_entry' | 'game_over'`, `'game'` is already exercised at `test/contracts.test.ts:246`, and AD-7 names no phase members. Inventing a fifth member where `'game'` already means exactly this would give two names for one state. This is a Rule 5 tier-1 correction (a wrong name the AC cites), recorded here so a reviewer can challenge the reading rather than discover it.

**`ball_started` must be authored.** The same AC names `ball_will_start` → `ball_starting` → `ball_started`; only the first two exist (`events.ts:96-105`). Adding the third is additive and consistent with the existing ball vocabulary, and it deliberately trips `test/contracts.test.ts:186`'s `never` guard at `pnpm typecheck` — add the `case` arm, never widen the guard.

**Why `hardwareEnabled` must start `false`, and what it costs.** `loop/index.ts:177` hardcodes `hardwareEnabled: true` at boot, while `phase` is `'attract'` and the field has **zero readers anywhere**. AD-7's Rule says "`ball_starting` enables hardware". If the boot value stays `true`, AC 2's assertion that hardware is enabled on `ball_starting` is **vacuously true on arrival** — precisely the "green depends on the defect it claims to guard against" class Rule 19 names, and there is no alternative formulation: a field that is already `true` cannot be observed becoming `true`. It must start `false`. Because `hardwareEnabled` is inside `MachineState` and both `stateHash()` and `gameStateHash()` hash `GameState`, this moves `expectedHash` **and** `expectedGameStateHash` on all five goldens.

**Reconciling that with the frozen `Never` and `Block If` clauses — read the scoping carefully, this is not a licence to re-record freely.** The `Never` list forbids touching `expectedHash` / `expectedGameStateHash` **"in a header-only refresh"**, and the `Block If` halts when the trace shows **"a trajectory change (any ball position differing before/after at 25-tick sampling)"**. This story's golden work is a **third category** the wording did not anticipate: *positions identical, `GameState` deliberately different*. Task 17 is therefore explicitly **not** a header-only refresh, and it is **not** a trajectory re-record either. The operative test is unambiguous and governs: **if any ball position differs at 25-tick sampling, HALT** — that is the grant-bound re-record the Block If exists for. If every position is identical and only the two hashes moved, the change is accounted for field by field and proceeds. DW-113's own contribution (task 16) remains header-only exactly as the `Always` clause states; the hash movement comes from task 6, a different cause, and the two must be attributed separately in the golden `notes`.

**The boot seed is construction, not mutation.** Tasks 5 and 6 have `sim/loop` build the *initial* `GameState` at `:228-236` and seed it from `GameStart`. That is not the thing AD-7 forbids and not the thing DW-70 is: the initial construction already lives there today, and AD-7's Rule constrains **mutation** ("mutated only inside `rules.step`"). DW-70 is specifically the **per-tick overwrite after `rules.step()` returns** (`:352-354`). After this story, `sim/loop` constructs `GameState` once at boot and never writes a field of it again — that is the invariant AC 1's `grep` checks, and the distinction is why the boot seed is not itself a violation.
