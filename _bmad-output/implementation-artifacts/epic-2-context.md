# Epic 2 Context: A Complete Game on the Real Shot Map

## Goal

Epic 2 turns Epic 1's placeholder vertical slice into a machine a stranger can play with no instructions. The real playfield geometry is drawn for the first time — the drain triangle, both Loops, the Ramp, the off-centre Dragon with the Lock lane between its legs, the DRAGON drop bank, Top lanes, slings and pops — and every device on it is wired into the table registry with a switch that registers reliably at the fastest speed the physics core can produce. On that geometry sits the whole standard game: Start and Hot seat for one to four players, the ball lifecycle, plunge and Skill shot, lane change, ball save, end-of-ball bonus and multiplier, tilt warnings, Tilt and Slam tilt, ball search, Match, game over and a return to a minimal Attract — all readable from a DMD Backglass with the inserts lit in the table's fixed colour grammar. Geometry is the first story and iterates with the rules that land on it.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **The shot map is the epic's foundation and is drawn from the reference dimensions alone** — no Bally template. **It is now drawn**: both Loops with entries and exits, one Ramp with an authored height and a decided return inlane, the Dragon off-centre with the Lock lane between its legs and a Mouth eject pose, the six-target DRAGON bank, **three** Top lanes, two slingshots, **three** pop bumpers, two inlanes, two outlanes and the plunger lane, all as `col_` and `sw_` nodes under the prefix contract — with **at least one** `sw_` zone per zone-requiring switch, more than one where a device presents separate faces (`s_dragon_body` carries a zone per Dragon leg). The plunge path is separated from the Loop channel: a plunged ball clears the Loop entrance, crosses the top and reaches the left flipper unaided — measured 3.66 mm closest approach and 438 ticks in the flipper band, against 249.40 mm and zero before. Figures that cannot be measured ship marked `unverified`. **Three clauses of that foundation are still open and owned elsewhere:** the Loops' exit paths feeding toward the flippers is 2.1c's; "guides end at rubber posts, never bare metal" is 2.1d's — the shot map shipped with **zero** `rubber_post` nodes, so that rule is unmet and its named mutation is unperformable until then; and the Ramp, though drawn with its authored height and its return rail built and verified, **has never been reachable by any shot in committed geometry** — that is 2.1f's, and it is a measured fact rather than a regression anything introduced.
- **The routing story's FIRST obligation is to repair the test that will judge it — before any geometry work in that story.** `test/shot-routing.test.ts`'s `assertReachesFlipperBandOrLeavesPlay` is `reachedFlipperBand || leftPlay`, and `leftPlay` is set when the ball **drained**, so the two outcomes the criterion exists to distinguish are today indistinguishable; four of its ten cases also close their primary switch only because `driveShot()`'s teleport lands the ball *inside* the zone under test. The pin must assert `reachedFlipperBand` per shot where the criterion requires it, keep `leftPlay` only as a terminal-outcome guard, stop the teleport landing inside the zone, and be demonstrated **red against the current geometry** before it judges any new geometry. **This ordering is binding and survives a runner change:** with the pin unrepaired the routing story inherits a green test over the exact geometry it is chartered to fix — the `DW-119` failure a third time.
- **A completed Loop must return the ball to an inlane, and that routing is its own story.** A ball driven into either Loop at a plausible flipper-shot speed has to arrive playable at the flipper on the corresponding side rather than descending an outlane — the Loops are combo shots, not one-way trips. This is design work, not map-drawing, and it is the one place in the epic where **the drain triangle's dimensional bounds and the Ramp's position are explicitly negotiable**, provided every change is recorded with its measurement and the drain triangle's own behavioural gates still pass. Two prior attempts inside the map-drawing story established the shape of the problem: pure diverter deflection was measured at under 5 mm of net lateral drift where roughly 50 mm is needed, diverters solid enough to respond firmly cap the ball's own ascent instead, and the only other corridor on the table is a 5.4 mm gap against a 13.495 mm ball radius. The likely answer is a multi-surface funnel proven out empirically — the way the funnel under the drain triangle was — or a Ramp position that opens a genuinely passable corridor. The plunge-path separation above must survive the change, the drain switch must be re-verified against the routing that finally lands, and every behavioural gate the shot-map story shipped must still pass, with any golden whose trajectory the routing genuinely changes re-recorded on a trace shown correct rather than merely green. The **Left Loop's north end is also free** and belongs to the same story: shortening `col_loop_top` at its left end for the plunge fix left `col_loop_l` no longer meeting the top connector, so no ball closes both Loops' switches on one orbit and nothing tests that it can.
- **A shot case that starts from a teleported ball proves nothing about whether the shot exists.** The routing harness repositions the ball at each case's release point, so every case proves only what happens *after* that placement, never that the placement is reachable. Two full code-review passes and the original implementation all missed a shot the table cannot deliver at all, because nothing in the suite asked the question. The harness must therefore prove each release point is **reachable** — a ball originating at a plunge or at either bat, under the real physics pipeline, arriving within a stated tolerance — as a property of the harness rather than of each case, so a case added later inherits it without opting in. A case proved unreachable **fails, naming the case and the closest approach achieved**, and is marked against its owning ledger entry rather than deleted, weakened or quietly re-pointed at a reachable start. The check carries its own demonstrated mutation and **cannot pass vacuously**: a run in which zero cases were evaluated fails loudly rather than reporting success. The same blind spot has a dimensional twin — an inlane feed guide can be shifted 20 mm or deleted outright with every routing case green, because the behavioural observable cannot distinguish "delivered onto the bat" from "still on the rail above it".
- **The bottom-right approach corridor is too narrow to admit a ball to two of the table's named shots, and both defects share one cause and one fix.** Measured on the committed collision document: from `col_guide_outer_r`'s east face (279.525) to `col_sling_r`'s west face (314.0) is **34.475 mm**, i.e. **7.485 mm of ball-centre freedom**. Entering the Ramp channel needs a ball centre ≥ 351.495, so the Ramp is **50.990 mm out of reach** (`DW-137`, demonstrated by 256 swept releases closing `s_ramp_enter` zero times); only bank targets overlapping x ≈ 280.5–314 can be struck directly, so **two of six** DRAGON targets are reachable (`DW-136`). Neither is a regression the routing story introduced — the Ramp was already 21.990 mm short at that story's baseline (`43a9c37`) and was deepened to 50.990 mm as the explicitly-granted consequence of widening the Loop lanes for the orbit. The corridor must be **re-solved as a whole** — the slingshot span, both Ramp walls, the DRAGON bank and the Loop lane budget together, not one body at a time — because moving `col_sling_r` back east re-narrows the very lane the orbit needs. **Buying the Ramp by breaking the delivered orbit is a hard stop, not a trade to make silently**, and a dimensional gate must pin the corridor width against its tunable so a later change cannot silently re-narrow it.
- **The pop-bumper count is three.** No artifact ever stated it — the requirement says only "pop bumpers" while tagging Top lanes at three and slingshots at two — so the author fixed it at three (`s_pop_1..3` / `c_pop_1..3`), matching the Top-lane count and the standard arrangement for this shot density. A wildcard cannot be declared in the registry. Like every other unverified figure it is recorded in `TABLE` with a `source` and a `confidence`, so the provenance is visible rather than looking like a fact somebody found.
- **The switch set completed in `TABLE.switches`** covers `s_loop_l_in/out`, `s_loop_r_in/out`, `s_spinner`, `s_ramp_enter/made`, `s_dragon_[d,r,a,g,o,n]`, `s_dragon_body`, `s_lock_lane`, `s_lock_1..3`, `s_top_1..3`, `s_inlane_l/r`, `s_outlane_l/r`, `s_sling_l/r`, `s_pop_1..3` and `s_drain`, each with its per-device `settleTicks` class — plus the ball device `bd_lock` (capacity 3, ejecting through `c_mouth`, with a `ballSearchOrder`) and the coils `c_sling_l/r`, `c_pop_1..3`, `c_dragon_bank_reset` and `c_mouth`. The export script validates the Blender source against the registry dump and both loaders must load the result. **Switch-edge semantics are settled:** the tracker latches a make on the tick it observes the zone, and `settleTicks` gates the **break**, never the make. A residual off-by-one remains on that break side — both trackers break after `settleTicks`+1 outside ticks rather than `settleTicks` — routed to the device story; it moves no golden.
- **`bd_lock` is declared but does not yet behave, and that is the device story's charter.** Boot occupancy is a property of the device, not a constant: slots are filled unconditionally at construction, so the machine boots **seven** balls and a Lock-lane shot raises measured `device_overflow` events. The Mouth eject pose sits **inside** `sw_lock_2`, so the entry detector re-parks the ejected ball on the same tick and no ball ever leaves — "one ball per pulse" is unmet. The slot zones sit in open field, swallowing any passing ball, instead of being bounded by the Lock lane's own geometry. Fixing the boot state moves **all five** goldens together, because the state hash covers `machine.deviceSlots`; each golden's `notes` must be corrected in the same pass, since all five claim `bd_lock` contributes an *"empty"*-slots entry when the real boot value is `[true, true, true]` — wrong in the one detail the note exists to record. The `rubber_post` guide terminations belong to the same story and need a gate with a demonstrated mutation (flip one guide-end post's `surface` to `metal`, re-export, the gate goes red naming that guide); so does the export's non-convex-footprint rejection, which fires today but has no automated regression pin — and adding a Blender-gated case means deliberately updating the test that pins the exact case count and its skip formula.
- **Two distinct open questions are answered and recorded in the decisions document. Do not conflate them.**
  - **OQ-6 is the playfield geometry itself** — flipper tip gap, outlane widths, post positions, loop entries, **Ramp height and its return side**, Dragon placement, and now the **spinner model** as well. The Ramp's return-inlane choice is the **OQ-6**/FR-27 decision; it is decided (right inlane) but **not yet delivered**, and its delivery was **moved by amendment out of the routing story and into the corridor story** — the routing story could satisfy it only through the harness's teleport, so the clause went where the reachability it depends on is actually bought.
  - **OQ-5 is whether the Lock lane carries both lock and mode start.** If it does not, a separate `sw_scoop` and `bd_scoop` are added with the Mouth as eject only — a table-and-geometry change, not an architecture change, because the Lock arbiter decides from state.
- **Every shot's miss must come back playable (Lawlor's test), verified per shot.** A feel-test entry lands for each of the seven shots — Left Loop, Right Loop, Ramp, Dragon, Lock lane, bank, Top lanes — recording where the most common miss goes; none may be a centre drain, and any shot that fails is re-drawn before its story closes. Running the ritual is author-owned work at the Reference machine — the pipeline cannot close it, and the miss-destination judgement itself stands explicitly **`pending-author` and unwritten**, so no stage may record it as satisfied.
- **Switches register at maximum ball speed or the geometry is wrong.** Zone tests use the ball's per-tick swept segment, never its end position, and a test asserts that the fastest ball a full-strength plunge and flipper hit can produce misses no pass through a rollover, the bank, the Lock lane or the drain — each switch closing exactly once per pass.
- **Per-player facts stay per player in Hot seat** — score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus and multiplier, extra balls, lit lanes.
- **Ball save has a precise shape**: enabled at launch with the timer starting on the launch event, not on enable; a hurry-up display state before expiry; a grace window past the *displayed* expiry during which a drain still saves; saved balls auto-launch; multiple arming sources stack with the longest live window winning; Tilt disarms all.
- **Tilt keeps the score and forfeits the bonus.** Warnings are spaced by a semantic window (default count 1) and the bob is never reset by command — its physical decay plus a settle window is the settle. On Tilt, flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch are disabled together and the ball ends when the last ball in play drains. Slam tilt ends every player's game, returns the machine to Attract, and lives inside the replay.
- **Ball search never releases locked balls while a mode timer runs**, escalates through each device's declared order, and only at its final stage issues the one command that lets physics despawn balls outside a device.
- **Match draws a multiple of ten from the seeded rules PRNG** at the configured probability (default 8%) and is display-only under free play.
- **English literals live in the Backglass and nowhere else** in the simulation. Rules never format text.
- **`pnpm check:ad7` is deliberately red** (`DW-70`), and stays red through Story 2.4. It is a ledger entry routed to **Story 2.5** as that story's own deliverable — device-slot state must come to be derived inside the ball controller rather than written after the rules step, and the test that currently asserts the failure is updated in the same change. No earlier story should touch it; it is not a defect to repair on sight.
- **A tunable's `source` string is embedded verbatim in every golden replay's `header.gameStart.tuning`.** Editing one — even to correct prose — re-records all five golden headers in the same pass, which is why the tip-gap provenance wording is routed to Story 2.5 rather than fixed where it was found.
- **Provenance is a hard gate** and the attribution entry lands before the file does, with the licence verified at its source.
- **A golden is re-recorded only under an explicit author grant, never to make a suite green and never by switching a parity check off.** Three were re-recorded under a narrow grant while the shot map landed, each traced correct *before* recording with the reasoning written into its own `notes` — that is the binding condition for any future re-record. The all-five re-record now rides the `bd_lock` boot fix in the device story. The `col_spinner_l` → `vis_spinner_l` rename is batched into that same re-export for the same reason: either change alone moves `assetHash` and breaks all five, so batching costs one re-record rather than two.
- A tick-rate or solver-constant change is a physics-version bump that re-records every golden.

## Technical Decisions

- **The devices-and-shots layer is the only consumer of switch events**; the build fails if anything else under the rules tree imports one. It owns the drop bank (letters and the reset coil, pulsed on ball-will-start and on bank completion), the spinner count, ball-device slots, the shooter lane and the declared shots, and emits only payload-complete device events — shot made and broken, ramp made, target down with its letter, bank completed, dragon hit, lock lane entered, spinner spins, lane entered, lane change pressed, ball launched, device ball entered and left, button pressed. Lane *state* belongs to the base mode; the layer reports entries and presses only.
- **Shots are declared data, not code** — an ordered switch sequence in the registry with a tick window converted from a millisecond tunable. In order inside the window emits made; a broken sequence or an expired window emits broken; a Loop taken the wrong way emits nothing.
- **Hardware rules run inside the physics step.** Slingshots and pop bumpers join the flippers and plunger: switch closes at tick *t*, coil energises inside tick *t*'s own physics step with no rules round trip, gated only by coil enable/disable. Disabled, they act as passive rubber and emit no actuation. Every actuation emits a contact event to presentation so each mechanical sound has exactly one source; rules never receive a contact event.
- **Physics owns ball bodies and mechanical state; rules own accounting.** The Lock is a parking device (capacity 3, filling the lowest empty slot, ejecting the highest filled slot at the Mouth pose, one ball per pulse); a slightly-off Lock-lane shot hits the Dragon body instead. The shooter lane is non-parking with the served ball resting simulated on the plunger tip, and the *opening* of its switch is the one event that means "plunged". Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else; a slot beyond capacity surfaces as an overflow event that rules answer with an immediate eject. A dropped target is non-collidable until the bank reset coil raises it. The spinner is a pass-through gate rather than a body the ball strikes — see the next bullet.
- **The spinner is a pass-through gate, not a collision body.** A ball crossing its switch zone imparts rotation proportional to entry speed, and the spinner then closes its switch **once per revolution until it decays**, because the requirement awards per rotation — that clause is deliberately preserved. Thirteen-plus rigid-body variants were measured across both candidate ball paths and every one that genuinely contacted the ball produced a permanent stall, because a static rigid body cannot graze a 13.495 mm-radius ball with the precision those paths allow; the analytic swept-segment zone test is therefore the physically correct model here, not a workaround for a failure. A node nothing collides with is not `col_` under the prefix contract, so the spinner's collision node is renamed to a visual node in the device story. **The physics-devices story owns the spin and decay mechanism**, driven off the zone crossing.
- **Game state is one plain-data tree with fixed ownership scopes** — JSON-serializable, no class instances or closures, mutated only inside the rules step, with player-scoped, machine-scoped and mode-local facts strictly separated. The mode stack is empty between balls: every active mode receives its will-stop before ball-ended; ball-will-start resets ball save, tilt and multiball; ball-starting enables hardware.
- **Single arbiters for the overloaded seams.** The ball controller alone pulses the trough and autolaunch coils and alone mutates balls-in-play; ball save is one machine device it owns, armed and disarmed by source through a rules-internal interface; the Lock arbiter is the only consumer of lock-lane entries. "A multiball is running" is a flag set and cleared in mode lifecycle phases, never derived from balls in play.
- **The command union is closed.** Rules to physics: coil pulse/enable/disable, plus the recover command for ball search alone. Rules to presentation: lamp, GI, flasher and show commands, nothing else.
- **Lamp state is a pure projection** recomputed from state every step; the loop emits only the diff, and no mode issues a lamp command. Roles are a closed union and steps are 0 to 3; no RGB, hex or colour name may appear anywhere in the simulation, and one presentation-side table is the sole role-to-colour mapping.
- **One clock, no wall-clock or unseeded randomness in the simulation.** Timers are authored in milliseconds in the tuning file, converted to ticks once at load, and pace presentation by emitting step events presentation animates to — presentation never reports completion. All rules randomness draws from the seeded PRNG in game state, so the Skill-shot lane and the Match number replay identically.
- **Modes have unique numeric priorities** — base 100, skill shot 200 — and speak the four-phase start and stop convention. Epic 2 runs that minimal stack only.
- **Blender owns placement, the registry owns wiring, the export script enforces the contract.** Node prefixes are load-bearing: collision scaffolding, switch zones, visuals, insert lens-and-cup geometry, mechanisms named as their device. The export validates the source against a dump of the registry and writes both the render asset and a collision file in table-frame millimetres; the simulation never parses the render asset, and both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property.
- **Layering is law and is linted.** Device-name string literals outside the table file and the test tree are errors, so every new switch, coil, lamp and device name enters through the registry. Three complementary provenance gates exist and none may be retired in favour of another: a per-file **presence** check across tracked files, a **structural** provenance test (upstream copyright block intact, provenance sets disjoint, the solver-constant pin, the port-body freeze), and the **import** linter. Presence cannot detect a stripped copyright block; Story 2.0 is the structural gate's documented owner.
- **Tunables carry a source and a confidence.** Material parameters are named entries with scatter at zero. Scoring values stay adjustable through Epic 2 and freeze only after Epic 3's first full playtest.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera, no camera controls.** Both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp and the DRAGON bank must all be legible from it. The backbox strip that carries the Backglass is **not** part of that framing obligation in the shot-map story — no backglass or backbox geometry exists anywhere yet (`cabinet_root` is authored childless), so Story 2.6 authors the quad and owns any camera consequence.
- **The Backglass is a low-resolution dot-matrix display** — a visible dot grid, no smoothing — showing every player's score with the current player highlighted, the ball number, and the highest-priority mode's name and whichever fields it publishes, converted to display units in presentation.
- **Presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.** An end-of-ball screen names the player from the event payload, never from the snapshot that has already advanced.
- **The colour grammar is fixed and never varies**: white for a lit or qualifying shot, orange for DRAGON letters and the Lock, red for Hurry-up, green for Quick multiball, blue for Joust, purple for extra ball and special. Progression within a colour is a brightness and blink ladder expressed as the step value, with cadence timed by presentation.
- **Flipper buttons move the lit insert** one position per press across the Top lanes and across the inlane/outlane set, wrapping.
- **Attract in this epic is minimal**: the Backglass cycles the last game's scores with a start prompt and shows the flipper, plunge and Start keys once from the view configuration. The camera walk-up belongs to Epic 4.

## Cross-Story Dependencies
