## Goal

Epic 2 turns Epic 1's placeholder vertical slice into a machine a stranger can play with no instructions. The real playfield geometry is drawn for the first time — the drain triangle, both Loops, the Ramp, the off-centre Dragon with the Lock lane between its legs, the DRAGON drop bank, Top lanes, slings and pops — and every device on it is wired into the table registry with a switch that registers reliably at the fastest speed the physics core can produce. On that geometry sits the whole standard game: Start and Hot seat for one to four players, the ball lifecycle, plunge and Skill shot, lane change, ball save, end-of-ball bonus and multiplier, tilt warnings, Tilt and Slam tilt, ball search, Match, game over and a return to a minimal Attract — all readable from a DMD Backglass with the inserts lit in the table's fixed colour grammar. Geometry is the first story and iterates with the rules that land on it.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **The shot map is the epic's foundation and is drawn from the reference dimensions alone** — no Bally template. **It is now drawn, and every clause of it is delivered**: both Loops with entries and exits, one Ramp with an authored height and a decided return inlane, the Dragon off-centre with the Lock lane between its legs and a Mouth eject pose, the six-target DRAGON bank, **three** Top lanes, two slingshots, **three** pop bumpers, two inlanes, two outlanes and the plunger lane, all as `col_` and `sw_` nodes under the prefix contract — with at least one `sw_` zone per zone-requiring switch, more than one where a device presents separate faces (`s_dragon_body` is served by `sw_dragon_body_l` / `sw_dragon_body_r`, one per Dragon leg). The plunge path is separated from the Loop channel: a plunged ball clears the Loop entrance, crosses the top and reaches the left flipper unaided — measured 3.66 mm closest approach and 438 ticks in the flipper band, against 249.40 mm and zero before. Figures that cannot be measured ship marked `unverified`. The Loops' exit paths feeding toward the flippers landed with 2.1c; "guides end at rubber posts, never bare metal" landed with 2.1d, which took the map from zero `rubber_post` nodes to 48; and the Ramp — drawn with its authored height and its return rail but never reachable by any shot in committed geometry, a measured fact rather than a regression — was made reachable by 2.1f, by a real driven shot rather than a teleport.
- **The routing harness's pin was repaired before it judged any new geometry, and no later story may weaken it.** `test/shot-routing.test.ts`'s `assertReachesFlipperBandOrLeavesPlay` was `reachedFlipperBand || leftPlay` with `leftPlay` set when the ball **drained**, so the two outcomes the criterion exists to distinguish were indistinguishable; four of its cases also closed their primary switch only because the harness's teleport landed the ball *inside* the zone under test. Both defects are fixed: the pin asserts `reachedFlipperBand` per shot where the criterion requires it, `leftPlay` survives only as a terminal-outcome guard, and a release point is proved clear of every body and zone before it is driven. The repair was demonstrated **red against the geometry it then went on to judge** — the ordering that stopped the `DW-119` failure recurring a third time. **The standing rule is the durable part:** a story that relaxes an assertion here rather than fixing the geometry underneath it is repeating that defect, not resolving it.
- **A shot case that starts from a teleported ball proves nothing about whether the shot exists** — so the harness proves each start point is reachable, and any case added later inherits that without opting in. Every release point lives in one manifest, a single `driveCase(id)` entry point is the only way to drive one — so a coordinate cannot be driven without a declaration — and non-teleporting witness trajectories (a ball originating at a plunge or at a bat, under the real physics pipeline) measure each release point's closest approach. It carries an anti-vacuity floor so a run that evaluates zero cases fails loudly rather than reporting success, and is backed by an opt-in dense out-of-process sweep that proves the negative. **Its measured baseline is a fact to preserve, not a number to edit:** `check:reachability` exits 0 over **52 driven cases — 32 proven reachable, 20 recorded unreachable**, measured from **644 swept releases in ~106 s**; Story 2.3 left that baseline unchanged. The release count is the sweep's own recipe count and moves independently of the case manifest — re-measure both rather than deriving one from the other. **The single-origin limitation that once qualified this baseline is closed:** every witness was a plunge or a plunge-then-LEFT-bat until 2.1f added a **right-bat witness family**, which is why the Ramp could not be proved reachable at any corridor width; the dense sweep now drives both bat sides and asserts that it does, and the witness-corpus floor is a derived origin-family check — plunge-only, plunge-then-bat-l and plunge-then-bat-r must each be represented. The remaining unreachable set is a `DW-138` cohort the lead is harvesting, several of which may still reflect the two axes the 2026-09-04 adjudication left with `burndown` (a SECOND flip, and a `bd_lock`/`c_mouth` origin) rather than the geometry. A story that moves that split must move it by changing geometry, not by re-declaring a case. A case proved unreachable **fails, naming the case and the closest approach achieved**, and is marked against its owning ledger entry rather than deleted, weakened or quietly re-pointed at a reachable start. The same blind spot has a dimensional twin — an inlane feed guide can be shifted 20 mm or deleted outright with every routing case green, because the behavioural observable cannot distinguish "delivered onto the bat" from "still on the rail above it". A proximity record now falsifies that one; the dimensional gates remain the primary catch.

On Tilt, flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch are disabled together and the ball ends when the last ball in play drains. ## Technical Decisions

- **AD-19 governs Story 2.4 directly and is the decision to read first.** `src/sim/rules/devices/` is the **only** consumer of `SwitchEvent`; the build fails if anything else under the rules tree imports one. It owns the drop bank (letters and the reset coil, which it **alone** pulses — on `ball_will_start` and on `bank_completed`), the spinner count, ball-device slots, the shooter lane, and the declared shots. It emits only payload-complete device events: `shot_<name>_made` / `_broken`, `ramp_made`, `bank_target_down { letter }`, `bank_completed`, `dragon_hit`, `lock_lane_entered`, `spinner_spin { count }`, `lane_entered { lane }`, `lane_change_pressed { side }`, `ball_launched`, `device_ball_entered { device, slot }`, `device_ball_left { device, slot }`, `button_pressed { button }`. **Modes, scoring and the ball controller consume device and shot events and never a raw switch.** Lane *state* (lit flags, completed sets) belongs to the base mode; this layer reports lane entries and lane-change presses only. An Epic 1 minimum of this module already exists and handles only `ball_launched` plus internal slot enter/leave bookkeeping, resolving device and slot from `TABLE.ballDevices` rather than any literal — Story 2.4 grows it into the full layer.
- **Shots are declared data, not code** — an ordered switch sequence in `TABLE.shots` with a tick window converted from a millisecond tunable (`shot_left_loop = [s_loop_l_in, s_loop_l_out]` within `loopWindowMs`). In order inside the window emits made; a broken sequence or an expired window emits broken; a Loop taken the wrong way emits nothing. **This sequence-based detection is load-bearing, not stylistic**: a bare `s_loop_*_in` edge is genuinely ambiguous in the shipped geometry — both Loops' `_in` zones span their whole lane mouth, which also spans that side's outlane column, so **every outlane drain closes `s_loop_l_in` / `s_loop_r_in`, and so does a made Ramp** (measured `firstMakes` on a made Ramp: `s_ramp_enter, s_ramp_made, s_loop_r_in, s_inlane_r` — the return crosses into the Loop lane). **This layer must never treat a bare `s_loop_*_in` as a Loop entry.** The ordered pair is what makes it safe. Story 2.4's inbox carries this as `DW-133`.
- **`lock_lane_entered` over-reports and the fix is an arbiter question, not a geometry one** (`DW-166`, Story 2.4's other inbox entry). An **under-powered but on-axis** Lock shot closes `s_lock_lane` without ever being captured — measured threshold **550–600 mm/s**: the ball enters the lane and closes the switch but never reaches a slot. The wider failure this narrows (`DW-134`, balls wandering into the lane from open field) was **closed by Story 2.1d** and is pinned by Story 2.3's absence-pin over 56 driven columns; this residual is what survives. The fix is either a discriminating condition on the event or an arbiter that tolerates a non-capturing entry. **A geometry change is the wrong answer** — it would undo the wedge fix 2.1c's bevel reversal exists for, which is precisely why 2.1d declined to chase it.
- **Hardware rules run inside the physics step.** Slingshots and pop bumpers join the flippers and plunger: switch closes at tick *t*, coil energises inside tick *t*'s own physics step with no rules round trip, gated only by coil enable/disable. Disabled, they act as passive rubber and emit no actuation. Every actuation emits a contact event to presentation so each mechanical sound has exactly one source; rules never receive a contact event. **Story 2.2 delivered both, and they sit at different points in the tick — copy that shape rather than re-deriving it.** The slingshot *is* the ported model, registered per coil through a thin subclass that never edits the frozen port (`DW-79` — wire from outside: construct, subclass, inject), so its parabolic kick fires **inside the collision solve** at contact time. The pop bumper is an authored skirt device that needs a switch *edge*, which exists only after the switch tracker steps, so it fires **after** that call and before the step returns, as an impulse directed radially away from the bumper's own footprint centroid derived from the committed document. Three consequences worth inheriting: kick **magnitude alone cannot break a reflection symmetry** — a ball descending exactly onto a bumper's apex vertex needs a fixed, deterministic lateral floor, never randomness; reporting that a coil *branch ran* is not the same as reporting that an impulse was *delivered*; and two `sw_pop_*` skirt zones overlap in committed geometry, so one ball can make two pop switches on a tick and fire two near-cancelling coils — escalated rather than patched, because the root fix is `sw_` geometry and therefore another five-golden re-record, while the code-side mitigation changes *which* coil fires and so is **a product call for the devices-and-shots layer**.
- **`popKickMmPerS` ships at 200 and is protected rather than safe.** The provenance prose was corrected at Story 2.3 (riding that story's golden re-record). The corrected facts: there is **no floor near 180** — virtually any positive kick clears the stranding case, down to 0.5 mm/s — and the landscape is **chaotic in both directions**, with the nearest re-strand at **198 mm/s, two mm/s *below* the shipped value** (reproduced at 1.12–1.86 mm trailing progress near the (93, 840) equilibrium), and a further cliff at 221. Measured neighbours: 198 → re-strand, 199 → 126.79, 200 → 126.84, 202 → 309.72. No nearby value is better — 215–220 trades a 2 mm/s lower margin for a 1 mm/s upper one. Sentinels sit on the clearing pocket's ends so a future physics change that lands on a re-strand reddens loudly rather than shipping silently; deliberately **not** a pin on 198's re-strand, which would go red if a future change *widened* the margin. Widening that margin is a separate real question needing its own sweep.
- **Physics owns ball bodies and mechanical state; rules own accounting.** The Lock is a parking device (capacity 3, filling the lowest empty slot, ejecting the highest filled slot at the Mouth pose, one ball per pulse). **A ball device's boot occupancy is a declared property of that device, never a global constant** — the trough boots full, the Lock boots empty, physics reads the declaration, and the machine's four-ball total is asserted by name at construction. This is a registry contract, not a one-story detail: **every future ball device declares its own boot occupancy**, and the device, shots, lifecycle, ball-save and ball-search stories are all named consumers. The shooter lane is non-parking with the served ball resting simulated on the plunger tip, and **the *opening* of `s_shooter_lane` is the one event that means "plunged"** — the devices layer emits it as `ball_launched`, on which the ball controller increments `ballsInPlay`, starts the ball-save timer and arms the skill shot (which closes on the next playfield closure that is not a Top lane). Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else; a slot beyond capacity surfaces as an overflow event that rules answer with an immediate eject. A dropped target is non-collidable until the bank reset coil raises it. **The spinner is a pass-through gate, not a collision body** — thirteen-plus rigid-body variants were measured across both candidate ball paths and every one that genuinely contacted the ball produced a permanent stall, because a static rigid body cannot graze a 13.495 mm-radius ball with the precision those paths allow; the analytic swept-segment zone test is the physically correct model here, not a workaround for a failure. A node nothing collides with is not `col_` under the prefix contract, so the spinner's node is `vis_spinner_l` and is a presentation mesh.
- **`GameState` is one plain-data tree with fixed ownership scopes** — JSON-serializable, no class instances or closures, mutated only inside the rules step. **Player-scoped** (only under `players[i]`): score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus by category and multiplier, extra balls, lanes (lit flags and completed sets, owned by the base mode; the skill-shot mode writes the lit Top lane once on `ball_starting` from the PRNG and never again), Jackpot seed and Wars started. **Machine-scoped** (only under `machine`): device slot states and `ballsInPlay`, `hardwareEnabled`, `ballSave`, `tilt`, `multiball`, read-only high scores. **Mode-local**: the mode's own timers and counters, published to presentation only as its typed view. The mode stack is empty between balls: every active mode receives its will-stop before ball-ended; ball-will-start resets ball save, tilt and multiball; ball-starting enables hardware.
- **Single arbiters for the overloaded seams.** The **Lock arbiter** in the ball controller is the only consumer of `lock_lane_entered` and emits exactly one of locked, mode-start with candidates, strike, or spit (device full of another player's balls; the credit still counts). It alone pulses `c_mouth` and serves from the trough after a lock entry; when lock and mode start both apply it credits the lock first; the mode-select window is its timer, the ball stays parked meanwhile, flipper edges move the selection and Start or either flipper confirms; every Mouth eject is preceded by a mouth-open show and follows it by a converted lead time. **Ball save** is one machine device the ball controller owns, armed and disarmed by source through a rules-internal interface, sources stacking with the longest live window winning, Tilt disarming all; only the ball controller pulses the trough-eject and autolaunch coils and mutates `ballsInPlay`. **"A multiball is running"** is a flag set in a mode's starting phase and cleared in its stopped phase, never derived from balls in play.
- **The command union is closed.** Rules to physics: coil pulse/enable/disable, plus the recover command for ball search alone. Rules to presentation: lamp, GI, flasher and show commands, nothing else.
- **Lamp state is a pure projection** recomputed from state every step; the loop emits only the diff, and no mode issues a lamp command. Roles are a closed union and steps are 0 to 3; no RGB, hex or colour name may appear anywhere in the simulation, and one presentation-side table is the sole role-to-colour mapping.
- **One clock, no wall-clock or unseeded randomness in the simulation.** Timers are authored in milliseconds in the tuning file, converted to ticks once at load, and pace presentation by emitting step events presentation animates to — presentation never reports completion. All rules randomness draws from the seeded PRNG in game state, so the Skill-shot lane and the Match number replay identically.
- **Modes have unique numeric priorities** — base 100, skill shot 200 — and speak the four-phase start and stop convention. Epic 2 runs that minimal stack only.
- **Rules tests run headless in Node with no physics or rendering loaded**, driven by a switch-script DSL typed by `SwitchName` (`close('s_loop_l_in').at(100).open().at(120)…`). Every device and shot event this layer emits needs at least one scripted test. Integration tests that reach `FrameOutput` through the loop's `advance` are a separate obligation — Story 2.3 found both of its modules had **none at all**, a real gap its own review subagents never flagged.
- **Blender owns placement, the registry owns wiring, the export script enforces the contract.** Node prefixes are load-bearing: collision scaffolding, switch zones, visuals, insert lens-and-cup geometry, mechanisms named as their device. The export validates the source against a dump of the registry and writes both the render asset and a collision file in table-frame millimetres; the simulation never parses the render asset, and both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property.
- **Layering is law and is linted.** Device-name string literals outside the table file and the test tree are errors, so every new switch, coil, lamp and device name enters through the registry. Three complementary provenance gates exist and none may be retired in favour of another: a per-file **presence** check across tracked files, a **structural** provenance test (upstream copyright block intact, provenance sets disjoint, the solver-constant pin, the port-body freeze), and the **import** linter. Presence cannot detect a stripped copyright block; Story 2.0 is the structural gate's documented owner.
- **Tunables carry a source and a confidence.** Material parameters are named entries with scatter at zero. Scoring values stay adjustable through Epic 2 and freeze only after Epic 3's first full playtest.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera, no camera controls.** Both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp and the DRAGON bank must all be legible from it. The backbox strip that carries the Backglass is **not** yet part of that framing obligation — no backglass or backbox geometry exists anywhere (`cabinet_root` is authored childless), so Story 2.6 authors the quad and owns any camera consequence.
- **The Backglass is a low-resolution dot-matrix display** — a visible dot grid, no smoothing — showing every player's score with the current player highlighted, the ball number, and the highest-priority mode's name and whichever fields it publishes, converted to display units in presentation.
- **Presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.** An end-of-ball screen names the player from the event payload, never from the snapshot that has already advanced.
- **The colour grammar is fixed and never varies**: white for a lit or qualifying shot, orange for DRAGON letters and the Lock, red for Hurry-up, green for Quick multiball, blue for Joust, purple for extra ball and special. Progression within a colour is a brightness and blink ladder expressed as the step value, with cadence timed by presentation.
- **Flipper buttons move the lit insert** one position per press across the Top lanes and across the inlane/outlane set, wrapping.
- **Attract in this epic is minimal**: the Backglass cycles the last game's scores with a start prompt and shows the flipper, plunge and Start keys once from the view configuration. The camera walk-up belongs to Epic 4.
