## Goal

Epic 2 turns Epic 1's placeholder vertical slice into a machine a stranger can play with no instructions. The real playfield geometry is drawn for the first time — the drain triangle, both Loops, the Ramp, the off-centre Dragon with the Lock lane between its legs, the DRAGON drop bank, Top lanes, slings and pops — and every device on it is wired into the table registry with a switch that registers reliably at the fastest speed the physics core can produce. On that geometry sits the whole standard game: Start and Hot seat for one to four players, the ball lifecycle, plunge and Skill shot, lane change, ball save, end-of-ball bonus and multiplier, tilt warnings, Tilt and Slam tilt, ball search, Match, game over and a return to a minimal Attract — all readable from a DMD Backglass with the inserts lit in the table's fixed colour grammar. Geometry was the first story and iterated with the rules that land on it; **the geometry, physics, device-vocabulary, lifecycle, display, lamp and ball-save halves are all now delivered**, and what remains is the last band of rules stories — bonus, tilt, ball search, Match and game over — plus one chartered correction to the skill shot's lit lane.

Assume your instruments are vacuous until you have watched them go red.** Every acceptance criterion this epic has found pinned by a test that could not fail was caught by a deliberate falsification attempt — **not one by a passing run.** The running count is **49** (Story 2.9 alone added four). Repeatedly the vacuity sits *inside a test written specifically to prevent an earlier vacuity*. The shapes worth carrying: an expectation **computed from the value under test**, so the assertion can never fail (a tunable settable to 0, or to 1, with the suite fully green — pin a tunable against an independently authored literal or an observable consequence, never against a re-import of itself); a duration pin that constrains only that a span is *non-empty*, so it could collapse to one tick; a boundary whose probes straddle it without sitting on it; a signature fix with no executing assertion; an assertion on a display row the rasteriser silently drops; player scoping never exercised at index ≠ 0; inputs scripted on separate ticks so the named mutation is a no-op; a spinner test so symmetric it could not tell closes from opens; an AC whose gate was **narrower than its own wording**; a kick-direction test that dotted a physics-frame velocity against a table-frame offset, so a full sign inversion made every case pass *more strongly*; an assertion comparing a hand-built literal **against itself**; a harness case satisfied by its own printed **test title**; and a story about a light that changed no pixel, verified by assertions that would pass whether or not the light changed anything.
- **Two new shapes from Story 2.9 that any story writing anti-vacuity guards must know.** (a) **A non-vacuity guard the spec itself demanded was itself vacuous.** The task required "a derived count assertion that every zone-bearing subject was actually checked, so the loop cannot go silently vacuous" — and the guard as written compared the count against **a second call of the very helper the loop's `continue` consults**, so a regression returning `null` for a genuinely zone-bearing subject lowered both sides together and still passed. The self-referential shape reproduced *inside the test written to prevent it*. The fix derives the count from an independent registry (`Object.keys(TABLE.lamps).length - 1`). Assume the same of your own guard: name what it is derived from, and check that source is not the thing under test. (b) **A mutation is a property of a *tree*, not of a test.** A mutation genuinely observed red in one iteration was **silently invalidated** when a later edit added a second conjunct to the same line — the guard could then be deleted with 29/29 ball-save and 52/52 golden tests green, and nothing re-checked it. Found only because a fresh review pass **re-walked the block the rework had edited** rather than trusting the record that the mutation had been observed. **If you edit a line a recorded mutation targets, that mutation is void until re-run** — and a recorded mutation is not evidence that the guard holds *now*.
- **A mutation harness can itself be vacuous, so state the expected red before running the mutation.** At Story 2.7's gate the runner's extractor grepped `[0-9]+ failed` and took `head -1`, matching vitest's **test-files** counter before its **tests** counter and under-reporting two mutations' red. It was caught only because the spec had *predicted* the expected red and the prediction and the measurement disagreed. The same discipline caught a regression guard whose exact-tick arrays encoded harness setup rather than the property under test: the lead and the reviewer got different tick values from the *same* fixed code, and the assertions were replaced with structural invariants.

Three separate defects have passed straight through a green suite for exactly this reason (a never-set `emissiveColor` that would have rendered a panel black; a UV ghost sliver read as a clipped text line; and fourteen insert lights that changed no pixel). **A presentation claim that only a rendered frame can settle must be measured in a real browser, and the measurement recorded with its numbers.** Story 2.9's smoke ran in the real product with a real held Enter key rather than the `c_autolaunch` hatch every automated test uses, **with the post-expiry drain as its control** — that control is what made the first half evidence.
- **Read a reporter contract, never scrape rendered output.** `test/ad7-device-slots.test.ts` spawns its nested harness with `--reporter=json --outputFile` and reads structured fields. **This shape is load-bearing and must not be undone.** A fix that scraped vitest's human summary line went red on Ubuntu while green on Windows — the runner colourises that line, so ANSI escapes land between `Tests` and the digit. That gate is pinned against both anti-vacuity mutations: an **exact** passing count of 3 (never a `>=` form) catches a deleted `it()` block, and publishing the computed device-slot views on `task.meta` catches gutted bodies that keep their titles.
- **Ball save is delivered (Story 2.9), and its arming rule is narrower than it reads.** `machine.ballSave` is one machine device the ball controller owns, with `arm({ ticks, source })` / `disarm(source)` through a rules-internal interface; enabled at `ball_starting` with the timer **not** started (`ball_save_enabled`), started on `ball_launched` (`ball_save_timer_started`); a hurry-up display state for the last `ballSaveHurryUpMs`; a grace window past the *displayed* expiry during which a drain still saves; the ball controller **alone** pulses `c_trough_eject` then `c_autolaunch`; sources stack with the longest live window winning; Tilt disarms all. Its tunables are `ballSaveMs` **8000**, `ballSaveHurryUpMs` **2000**, `ballSaveGraceMs` **2000**, all top-level scalars converted at load through `shotWindowTicks()` against `tickHz` **1000** (so the derived `…Ticks` siblings are numerically equal). **The timer is started only by a *player* plunge inside a game, never by a save's own re-serve** — see Technical Decisions for why, and for what actually enforces it. Exactly one source ships today; Story 3.7 is the first second one.
- **AC 5's drain probes are now FOUR, and the fourth is the point.** Drains at `expiry − 1`, `expiry + grace − 1`, **exactly `expiry + grace`** and `expiry + grace + 1`; the first three save and the fourth ends the ball. The three original probes could not catch the off-by-one they existed for — `expiry + grace − 1` passes under an inclusive `<=` **and** an exclusive `<`, and `expiry + grace + 1` fails under both, so the set stayed green whichever comparison shipped. Only a drain at *exactly* `expiry + grace` separates them. The project's only tick windows are all inclusive `<=`, which is the reading this criterion now pins. **The generalisation is the reusable part: a boundary criterion whose probes straddle a bound without sitting ON it pins nothing.** The probe set was strengthened, not the promise changed.
- **Bonus is per category, multiplied, and forfeited on tilt** (Story 2.10): letters, Loops and Strikes each increment from device events (`bank_target_down`, `shot_left_loop_made` / `shot_right_loop_made`); the multiplier starts each ball at 1× and advances 2× → 3× → 5× on each `lanes_completed { set: 'top' }`, capped at 5×; the `ball_ended` payload carries `bonusByCategory`, `multiplier` and `total = Σ category × value × multiplier`, the total is added to the score, and rules emit `bonus_count_step` events **paced by `bonusCountMs`** that the Backglass animates to; a tilted ball pays `total` 0 with the score unchanged; categories and multiplier reset at `ball_will_start` while letters persist.
- **Tilt keeps the score and forfeits the bonus** (Story 2.11). Warnings are spaced by a semantic window with a default count of 1, and the bob is **never reset by command** — its physical decay plus a settle window is the settle. On Tilt, flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch are disabled together via `CoilCommand disable`, ball save is disarmed, and the ball ends when the last ball in play drains. Warnings are per player. Slam tilt ends every player's game without bonus and returns the machine to Attract.
- **Ball search never releases locked balls while a mode timer runs** (Story 2.12), escalates through each device's declared `ballSearchOrder`, cancels and restarts on any switch closure, and only at its final stage issues the single `RecoverCommand` that lets physics despawn balls outside a device — after which `ballsInPlay` is corrected **from slot switches**. The device failure vocabulary (`eject_failed`, `ball_missing`, `broken`, `device_overflow`) must be handled without throwing, and `device_overflow` answered with an immediate eject.
- **Match draws a multiple of ten from the seeded rules PRNG** at the configured probability (default 8%) and is display-only under free play (Story 2.13). Game over shows final scores by player; Attract returns after `attractMs` or on Start, with hardware disabled, `modes[]` empty and `players[]` cleared on the next Start.
- **The lit Top lane rotates; it is not drawn** (Story 2.14, chartered 2026-09-07 out of Story 2.7). PRD FR-18 describes the lit Top lane as *"rotating each plunge"* — a deterministic advance, not a uniform draw. The shipped uniform draw repeats one lane on all three balls in **11.20%** of games and on a consecutive pair in **55.51%** (measured over 200,000 seeds), against Story 2.7's own criterion that the lit lane differs across balls. **The author has decided the form in full: a seeded starting position drawn once per game from `GameState.rng`, then a deterministic one-lane advance per plunge.** That satisfies every word of the rotation decision (a three-ball repeat is impossible **by construction**), satisfies Story 2.7 AC 5 verbatim with **no rewording**, and keeps alive the only mutation-proven pin anywhere that `GameStart.seed` reaches `GameState.rng` and changes something observable. **A fixed start is the wrong answer and was explicitly reconsidered and rejected.** The story also owes the amendment of two ratified artifacts (Story 2.7 AC 5 in `epics.md` and its spec's AC 6) in the same commit, with the reasoning recorded.
- **Every shot's miss must come back playable (Lawlor's test), verified per shot.** A feel-test entry lands for each of the seven shots — Left Loop, Right Loop, Ramp, Dragon, Lock lane, bank, Top lanes — recording where the most common miss goes; none may be a centre drain. Running the ritual is author-owned work at the Reference machine — the pipeline cannot close it, and the miss-destination judgement stands explicitly **`pending-author` and unwritten**, so no stage may record it as satisfied.
- **Per-player facts stay per player in Hot seat** — score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus and multiplier, extra balls, lit lanes. Delivered by 2.5, populated for the lane set by 2.7, projected to lamps by 2.8; the remaining stories consume it.
- **English literals live in the Backglass and nowhere else** in the simulation; rules never format text. A source-literal scan over `src/sim/` is one of Story 2.6's own acceptance assertions. **Its residual is live:** the scan's comment-stripping character scanner still desyncs on regex literals, recorded as an occurrence rather than re-filed, because the durable fix touches a CI gate.
- **Provenance is a hard gate** and the attribution entry lands before the file does, with the licence verified at its source. Two named traps stay closed around insert and lighting art: `vbousquet/pinball-parts`' "Thin Film Interaction" node group is **CC BY-NC-SA** and is exactly what one reaches for to make a lens look right — it cannot be used; and `vbousquet/vpx_lightmapper` has **no licence verified at source** (a research digest claiming GPL-3.0 is not verification) and no attribution row, so nothing may be taken from it.
- **`toPhysics()` negates y** (`y → playfieldHeightMm - y`), so a table-frame vector and a physics-frame vector point opposite ways on that axis. **Frame-mixing is a live hazard with a proven history on this epic.** Name the frame; assert in one frame, per axis.

## Technical Decisions

- **Story 2.9's defining output is not the feature but a correction to the architecture record itself, and a 2.10 planner should read it first.** AD-6's clause "the opening of `s_shooter_lane` … starts the ball-save timer" was **unqualified**, and read literally it describes an unplayable game: a save re-serves through `c_autolaunch`, which opens `s_shooter_lane` and so emits `ball_launched` — the very event the clause arms on — so **every save re-armed a fresh full window**. Measured twice independently at production tuning over 120,000 ticks, seed 0, no input: **28 `ball_saved`, zero `ball_ended`**, because the 8,000-tick window exceeds the shipped table's ~4,274-tick natural drain interval. The **control** — `ballSaveMs` 500 / grace 100, giving zero saves and `ball_ended` at the same tick 4273 — is what makes that null result evidence rather than a blind harness. Ball 2, **the bonus**, lane rotation, game over and Match were all unreachable, i.e. Stories **2.10**, 2.13 and 2.14. The author decided: **a player plunge arms the window; a save's own re-serve does not.** After the fix, the same harness reaches `game_over` with all three balls ending. Two lessons ride with it. **(1)** The implement stage's own review saw this exact shape and *dismissed it as mandated by AD-6's text* — reasoning from the wording without measuring the consequence, the third time this epic that reading an artifact beat measuring it and lost. **(2)** AD-6 clause (1) then had to be **CORRECTED against a premise the runner had written there the day before**: it recorded the `phase === 'game'` gate as load-bearing for the five golden state hashes, and it is not. The `sources` conjunct — the controller's own source must already be in `ballSave.sources`, i.e. `ball_starting` must have enabled it — is what independently forbids arming outside a ball, because the goldens never press `s_start`. Deleting the phase conjunct alone left **29/29 ball-save and 52/52 golden tests green**. Both conjuncts are kept, but **a guard nobody has watched fail is not known to guard anything** — and here the unverified claim was carried by the architecture document itself.
- **The shipped ball-save internals, so 2.10–2.12 do not re-derive them.** `BallSaveState` is **frozen at exactly `{ untilTick, sources }`** and sits inside the hashed `GameState`, so widening it moves `expectedHash`/`expectedGameStateHash` on all five goldens. Consequences that follow from the frozen shape: `armBallSave` only ever moves `untilTick` **forward** (`Math.max`), and `disarm` of the source that set the current maximum does **not** shrink to the next-longest survivor — there is no per-source deadline to recompute from — so AD-18's "the longest **live** window wins" stops holding after any disarm (`DW-219`, routed to Story 3.7, the first story with a second arming source). `isRunning` (`tick <= untilTick`, the *displayed* window the lamp follows) and `isWithinGrace` (`untilTick < tick <= untilTick + graceTicks`, deliberately invisible) are **disjoint**, both inclusive; the drain reads their union. `hasGraceLapsed` resets the **whole device** to `EMPTY_BALL_SAVE`, evaluated at the top of `step()` *before* that tick's device events are read. `disarmBallSave` has **no production caller yet** — Story 2.11 is its first. Two cross-tick discriminators live in the controller **closure, not in `GameState`** (zero state hashes): `awaitingSaveLaunch` and `awaitingSaveRelaunch` — the latter a `{ startTick }` record with a bounded lifetime, which is **a timeout, not a per-pulse causal binding** (`DW-228`, routed to 3.7).
- **Cross-tick state that must not move a hash belongs in the controller closure — and every such latch owes two things.** The pattern is established three times over (`pendingLockLaneClosure`, `awaitingSaveLaunch`, `awaitingSaveRelaunch`). Each latch needs **(i)** a reset at `ball_will_start` and **(ii)** its own **bounded lifetime**, expired at the top of `step()` before device events are read. A plain boolean with neither was `DW-224`/`DW-225`, whose consequence was not a stale flag but a **hung game** — the saved ball resting in the shooter lane at `ballsInPlay: 0`, so no parking entry, no `ball_ended`, no `ball_will_start`, with the sharp corollary that the latch's only reset could never run in the very scenario its own comment cited as justification.
- **`lampsOf`'s signature changed and the reason is a rule, not a detail (AD-9, amended 2026-09-07).** It is now `lampsOf(state, hurryUpTicks): LampState` — in-tree, `lampsOf(state, ballSaveHurryUpTicks = 0)`. A lamp whose step depends on **elapsed time** needs a duration the projection cannot read off `state`, and the duration is threaded in **as a parameter resolved from tuning by `sim/loop`, deliberately NOT as a new `GameState` field**, because `GameState` is hashed under AD-15 and a field there would cost five state hashes for a value that is a constant of the build, not a fact about the game. The projection stays pure — same inputs, same output. **The next timed-lamp story should thread a parameter, not add a field.** There are exactly two production call sites, and `sim/rules` itself never reaches for `TUNING` or `TICK_HZ`.
- **The lamp seam now carries fifteen inserts, and `l_ball_save` is machine-scoped.** Its subject kind is a payload-free `{ kind: 'ball_save' }`, resolved — like `{ kind: 'lock' }` — **above** the "no base mode ⇒ all off" guard, so it lights with no base mode on the stack. It projects role `lit` step **1** while the timer is running and step **3** within the last `hurryUpTicks` of the *displayed* window, `off` from the displayed expiry onward; the **grace period is never shown**, and Tilt makes it inert. It is the one insert with **no `sw_` zone**, so the asset contract carries an explicit zone-less count and a dedicated bottom-centre placement pin. `projectLamp` now ends in an explicit `never` tail, so a fifth `LampSubject` kind fails `pnpm typecheck` there rather than falling through to the `lane` branch's assumptions. The live product question on the projection is unchanged: `l_lock` lights in Attract and between balls whenever `bd_lock` is occupied, contradicting the frozen intent contract in three places — decision-pending, pinned by a test, and whichever side survives needs the other amended.
- **`lampsOf` is a pure projection with a closed vocabulary, and adding a lamp is not free.** Roles are `off · lit · hurryup · quickmb · joust · dragon · special`; steps are 0..3; **no RGB, hex or colour name may appear anywhere under `src/sim/`**, and one presentation-side table is the sole role-to-colour mapping. Subject kinds are constrained with `satisfies` against the wiring keys so a typo is a compile error. Adding a lamp key moves `tableHash` and owes a header-only five-golden re-record — **budget it at planning, not at implementation**; Story 2.9 did exactly that for `l_ball_save`.
- **The golden budget, and the cost correction Story 2.9 had to make TWICE.** `tableHash()` hashes the **whole frozen `TABLE`** in canonical JSON, so any new lamp, switch or device key moves it. `resolveTuning()`'s **entire serialized output** — `source` prose and `confidence` included — is compared as whole-tree canonical JSON, so **a new tunable, or a prose-only provenance correction, moves `header.gameStart.tuning` and invalidates all five goldens before tick 1** even when `TABLE` and `tableHash` never move. A new `…Ms` tunable adds **two** keys (itself plus its derived `…Ticks` sibling) and **must be a top-level scalar** — a nested `…Ms` key throws at load. `assetHash` hashes the **parsed collision document only**, built from three prefix filters (`col_`, `sw_`, `ballDevices`); an `l_` or `vis_` node matches none, which is why Story 2.9 changed the `.blend` and the `.glb` and still moved no `assetHash`. **None of the five goldens presses `s_start`** — all five stay in `phase: 'attract'`, no mode is ever pushed, `rng` never advances, `players[]` stays empty and `lampsOf` projects all-off throughout — which is why a lamps-and-tuning change is header-only by construction. **The cost correction:** a *header-only* refresh is **routine and needs no author grant** (precedent Story 2.4; Story 2.9 performed one). What needs the grant is a **trajectory** re-record — a moved body field, a shifted drain, a physics-version bump. Twice on Story 2.9 a decision was justified by the wrong cost ("a dedicated tunable would force a five-golden refresh"), and twice the story's own record refuted it. **A moved *state* hash means something other than a header change happened: HALT and report, do not re-record around it.**
- **How to re-record, and how to prove you did not move a body.** There is no recording tool — build the harness **in the scratchpad, never under `test/`** (a file matching `test/**/*.test.ts` silently joins `pnpm test`), over the shipped `runReplay` / `buildHeader` / `tableHash()` / `assetHash(doc)` / `PHYSICS_VERSION` surface, with a scratchpad vitest config naming the absolute harness path. Sample full-precision per-ball TSV rows on a tick stride into `trace-BEFORE` / `trace-AFTER` and diff them — **expect no output**; a drained ball emits no row, so a shifted drain shows as a *row-count* change. The measured corpus is **1,132 sampled rows** across the five (80 / 32 / 371 / 384 / 265). **Verify structurally, by parsing JSON per field — never by substring grep**, and **append** to each `notes` rather than rewriting. Note the trap 2.9 hit: an unanchored `git diff | grep` over golden field names now matches the appended `notes` **prose** and emitted 27 lines of false HALT; anchor any such pre-filter to the field's own key position (`^[-+]\s*"<field>"`) and treat the structural comparison as the authority. Preserve `gameStart.tuning`'s **declaration** key order — a past refresh flipped it from alphabetical and the reopen condition is live.
- **`RulesStepResult.modeEvents` still has NO production consumer, and wiring it is Story 2.10's work, not an assumption 2.10 may make.** Verified at this tree: `src/sim/rules/index.ts` produces `modeEvents`, `src/sim/rules/modes/base.ts` emits `lanes_completed { set }`, and the only reader anywhere is the test-side switch-script DSL; `src/sim/loop/index.ts` reads `.state`, `.events`, `.commands` and `.coilCommands` and never `.modeEvents`. So the event 2.10's multiplier advances on **cannot leave `rules.step()` in the shipped product**. The channel is deliberately separate and the event is correct where it is; the loop-side half is missing. This is `DW-208`, the one ledger entry Story 2.10 owns, and it is already written into 2.10's acceptance criteria.
- **What 2.10 will find already built, what is a placeholder, and what is a genuine ownership decision.** `players[i].bonus` exists as `PlayerBonusState { byCategory: Record<string, number>; multiplier: number }`, seeded `{}` / `1` at player creation — the **per-ball reset is 2.10's**, and it belongs in the `ball_will_start` path's `players` map, which today resets only machine-scoped fields. `BallEndedEvent` already declares `player`, `bonusByCategory`, `multiplier`, `total`, `tilted`, `tick`, and the controller emits **`total: 0` hardcoded**, so 2.10 fills a field rather than widening the event or the state — and the tilted case is already literally what ships. Mode teardown runs **strictly before** `ball_ended`, and the ending player is captured *before* rotation can move `currentPlayer`, so the bonus must be computed from that same pre-rotation snapshot. **The ownership decision to make deliberately: the ball controller has never written `score`** — today `score` is written only by a mode. "The total is added to the score" would be its first, and that is a choice, not a detail.
- **A live ball save returns early from the entire drain branch.** On a save the controller emits `ball_saved`, pulses `c_trough_eject` and **returns** — bypassing mode teardown, `ball_ended`, rotation and game over, leaving `currentPlayer`, `ballNumber` and `modes[]` untouched, because it is a re-serve of the *same* ball. Any reasoning of the form "a parking entry taking `ballsInPlay` to 0 ends the ball" is now false: there are two exits and the bonus sits on the second. **2.10's drain tests must therefore run at a tuning that does not save** — four test files already carry a `NO_BALL_SAVE_TUNING` override for exactly this — and a harness that drives a whole ball to its end should assert `ball_ended` **actually arrived**, not merely that nothing failed. That is the precise assertion whose absence hid the re-arm defect behind a green suite.
- **Every new semantic event owes a `describeEvent` case arm, and that arm is a known vacuity shape.** `test/contracts.test.ts` holds an exhaustiveness-gated `describeEvent()`; Story 2.9 added three arms plus three executing assertions with deliberately distinct field values. `bonus_count_step` will need the same. The standing warning is `DW-223`: a dozen-plus pre-existing arms are still unexercised by any assertion, so a wrong field reference type-checks and ships. Note also that **no ball-save event has a presentation consumer yet** — nothing outside the contracts and the controller reads `ball_saved` / `ball_save_enabled` / `ball_save_timer_started` — so 2.10's Backglass-animated `bonus_count_step` is cutting the first rules→Backglass path of its kind rather than following an existing one.
- **AD-19's devices-and-shots layer is the substrate every remaining rules story consumes.** `src/sim/rules/devices/` is the **only** consumer of `SwitchEvent` and the build fails if anything else reaches for one. It owns the drop bank (and **alone** pulses the reset coil), the spinner count, ball-device slots, the shooter lane and the declared shots, emitting only payload-complete events: `shot_<name>_made` / `_broken`, `bank_target_down { letter }`, `bank_completed`, `dragon_hit`, `lock_lane_entered`, `spinner_spin { count }`, `lane_entered { lane }`, `lane_change_pressed { side }`, `ball_launched`, `device_ball_entered/left { device, slot }`, `button_pressed { button }` and `playfield_switch_closed { switch }`. **Modes, scoring and the ball controller consume device and shot events and never a raw switch.** Lane *state* (lit flags, completed sets) belongs to the base mode. **Two by-design gaps, so nobody files them as defects:** `ramp_made` is named in prose but never emitted (a made Ramp surfaces as `shot_ramp_made`), and the two Loops can never emit `_broken`.
- **`playfield_switch_closed` exists because "the first playfield closure" was otherwise undecidable, and its membership is DERIVED, never hand-typed.** Slingshots, pops, the drain and a bare ramp switch emit no device event at all, while AD-19 forbids a mode reading a raw `SwitchEvent`. The event fires on each `closed: true` edge of a set derived **by subtraction from `TABLE`** — every switch key, minus the button, tilt-bob and slam classes, minus every parking device's slots, minus each non-parking device's entry switch: **28 switches** at this tree. Adding a switch to the registry adds it automatically; a hand-typed list would silently miss it. It is Story 2.12's natural answer to "has anything closed?" and Story 2.9's to "did the ball reach the playfield?".
- **The rules→physics coil channel exists and the ball controller owns it.** `RulesStepResult.coilCommands` is queued into `pendingCommands` and consumed on the **next** tick (AD-4). `FrameOutput.commands` deliberately stays presentation-only (`RulesStepResult.commands` is typed `readonly never[]`). This is the channel through which the ball controller pulses `c_trough_eject`, `c_autolaunch` and `c_mouth` — and AD-4's *N+1* delivery is why a rules-issued eject cannot fire before tick 2, a constraint with downstream consequences for any story timing a serve. One ordering fact for 2.10: at game over the hardware `disable` commands go out on the **same tick** as `ball_ended`, so a bonus count-up paced across ticks will straddle a tick on which the machine is already being shut down.
- **`GameState` is one plain-data tree with fixed ownership scopes**, JSON-serializable, no class instances or closures, **mutated only inside the rules step — enforced by a source-level gate over `src/sim/loop/**` covering every field, not only the one that once broke.** Player-scoped (only under `players[i]`): score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus by category and multiplier, extra balls, lanes, Jackpot seed, Wars started — **and `ballNumber`, which already exists, is already player-scoped (correct for Hot seat), is already inside the hashed state and is already correct when the mode stack starts, so Story 2.14 needs no new state field for a per-ball advance.** Machine-scoped (only under `machine`): device slot states and `ballsInPlay`, `hardwareEnabled`, `ballSave`, `tilt`, `multiball`, read-only high scores. Mode-local: the mode's own timers and counters, published to presentation only as a typed view. The mode stack is empty between balls: `ball_will_start` resets ball save, tilt and multiball; `ball_starting` enables hardware.
- **AD-8's four-phase mode convention is PHASED, and an Epic 2 implementation that omits it is conforming, not violating.** Epic 2 ships a **minimal** stack — base 100 plus skill shot 200 — that starts and stops modes directly, with no lifecycle events and no registry. **Story 3.1** generalises it, and from 3.1 onward a direct start/stop path is a defect. The stack's own contract is likewise unsettled and belongs to 3.1: `pendingStartPlayer` lives in a **closure outside `GameState`**, so two states that hash identically step differently; nothing cancels a pending arm if the ball ends inside its one-tick window; and the fan-out is **mode-major** rather than event-major, which differ observably on a tick carrying both a lane-change press and the resolving closure. 2.10 consumes mode events through that same unsettled fan-out.
- **One clock, no wall-clock or unseeded randomness in the simulation.** Timers are authored in milliseconds in the tuning file, converted to ticks once at load (`shotWindowTicks()` is the one sanctioned reader for the `…Ms` window tunables), and pace presentation by emitting step events presentation animates to — **presentation never reports completion**. All rules randomness draws from the seeded PRNG in game state. `src/host/game-seed.ts` derives a per-game seed via Web Crypto and `boot.ts` passes it through the existing `GameStart.seed` contract, so `sim/` still draws only from `GameState.rng` and replays stay reproducible; the dev replay recorder keeps its fixed seed 0, correctly. **The lane draw is currently the ONLY consumer of `GameState.rng` in the shipped product** — Match is Story 2.13's and is unbuilt — so there is no stream-offset hazard today, **but `rng`'s own value is hashed**, so whether Story 2.14's advance still consumes a step is hash-visible on its own. None of the five goldens ever starts a game, so 2.14 cannot redden a golden **today**. One residual: `hostLoop.reset()` does not carry the seed forward, so a tuning-panel hot-apply or dev reset silently restores the zero seed — dev-only paths, escalated to the burn-down sheet.
- **Shots are declared data, not code**, and `entryExclusive` records a **measured geometric fact, never a style choice**: `false` when something other than the shot also closes its own first switch. **Both Loops are `false`** — each `_in` zone spans its whole lane mouth, which also spans that side's outlane column, so **every outlane drain closes `s_loop_l_in` / `s_loop_r_in`, and so does a made Ramp**. A sequence with `entryExclusive: false` is tracked to completion but never emits `_broken` on expiry. The Ramp is `true`. **Never treat a bare `s_loop_*_in` as a Loop entry.** Note also a routing fact Story 2.9 had to correct a reachability claim on: `test/shot-routing.test.ts` pins a **single-ball "sneak-back"** — a near-miss right-Loop shot descends into the shooter lane and rests at the plunger pose with `s_shooter_lane` closed — so **a second, genuine mid-ball `ball_launched` exists today without multiball**. Two separate reachability arguments were built on the false premise that it does not; check yours against this before writing "unreachable". One open question sits here and **nobody should start it**: two consecutive same-side Loop orbits emit a spurious opposite-Loop made, bounded exactly by the Loop window; the fix needs cross-shot state that all of Epic 3 plans against.
- **Hardware rules run inside the physics step.** Slingshots and pop bumpers join the flippers and plunger: switch closes at tick *t*, coil energises inside tick *t*'s own physics step with no rules round trip, gated only by coil enable/disable — which is the channel Tilt uses to disable flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch together. Disabled, they act as passive rubber and emit no actuation. Every actuation emits a contact event to presentation so each mechanical sound has exactly one source; **rules never receive a contact event.** Reporting that a coil *branch ran* is not reporting that an impulse was *delivered*.
- **Physics owns ball bodies and mechanical state; rules own accounting.** Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else. A ball device's boot occupancy is a **declared property of that device**, never a global constant, so the machine boots 4 balls. The shooter lane is non-parking, and **the *opening* of `s_shooter_lane` is the one event that means "plunged"** — the devices layer emits it as `ball_launched`, on which the ball controller increments `ballsInPlay`, arms the skill shot, and starts the ball-save timer **only under the conjuncts above**. A drain is any **parking** device entry taking `ballsInPlay` to 0, deliberately device-agnostic — which is why a `bd_lock` capture of the last ball is currently read as a drain (`DW-221`, routed to Story 3.2). `hardwareEnabled` boots `false` while physics coils still default enabled, so flippers, slings and pops are physically live through the machine's very first Attract; that is `wontfix-accepted` by measurement — **do not "fix" it without the grant a trajectory re-record needs.**
- **Single arbiters for the overloaded seams.** **Ball save** is one machine device the ball controller owns (above); only the ball controller pulses the trough-eject and autolaunch coils and mutates `ballsInPlay`. The **Lock arbiter** is Story 3.2's and does not exist in Epic 2 — which is why the Lock residuals are routed to 3.2 rather than handled here. **"A multiball is running"** is a flag set in a mode's starting phase and cleared in its stopped phase, never derived from balls in play.
- **The command union is closed.** Rules to physics: coil pulse/enable/disable, plus the **recover command for ball search alone**. Rules to presentation: lamp, GI, flasher and show commands, nothing else. **Every semantic event is payload-complete** — presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.
- **Layering is law and is linted.** Device-name string literals outside the table file and the test tree are errors, so every new switch, coil, lamp and device name enters through the registry — the `s_/c_/l_/f_/gi_/bd_/shot_/show_` prefixes are all covered. Device-name literals **are** unrestricted under `test/**`, which is how a test anchors against an authored literal rather than re-deriving one — the fix for one of 2.9's vacuities. `tools/boundary-lint.mjs` also carries the `SwitchEvent` containment check, which is **textual, not a dependency-cruiser module rule**, and the `sim-no-colour` rule. Three complementary provenance gates exist — a per-file **presence** check, a **structural** provenance test, and the **import** linter — and **none may be retired in favour of another**. One known narrowness: `sim-no-colour`'s matchers are narrower than their own AC's words; widening was measured and is not free, so it is `wontfix-accepted` with a named reopen condition.
- **Rules tests run headless in Node with no physics or rendering loaded**, driven by the `SwitchName`-typed switch-script DSL. Every device and shot event needs at least one scripted test. **The headless claim is pinned by a transitive-closure import scan over resolved repo-relative paths, not by a comment** — its first two attempts were themselves vacuous — and the scan's entry list is **ratcheted**: a `test/rules-*.test.ts` file is headless unless named `*-integration.test.ts`, so the next omission reddens by name. Story 2.9's lesson on top of that: **a headless test that *scripts* the consequence it is meant to observe proves nothing about whether the consequence happens.** Its AC 3/AC 4 test hand-scripted the re-served ball's arrival, so a save that suppressed `ball_ended` and then served nothing would have hung the game with the suite green. Pair such a test with one real-`createLoop` integration case.
- **`BLENDER` must be exported by any stage doing Blender work, or the toolchain will report Blender as absent.** The executable is a **portable extract** at `C:/Users/Josh/tools/blender-5.2.1-windows-x64/blender.exe` — no registry entry, no conventional install location. **Four separate agents on this epic have wrongly concluded "Blender is not installed" from this**, and `AGENTS.md` contains zero occurrences of `BLENDER`. **The path is a host-local fact: export it in the environment, and never let it enter `src/`, `tools/` or `test/`.** With `BLENDER` unset the Blender-gated cases skip — a skip, not a failure, so an unset variable looks like success rather than like a gap.
- **Blender owns placement, the registry owns wiring, the export script enforces the contract, and glTF cannot carry material behaviour this project does not register.** Node prefixes are load-bearing; both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property. **A material effect must be authored in the driver, never in the asset**: Blender exports only `KHR_materials_transmission`, Babylon maps that to *refraction* rather than translucency, and this project deliberately registers **no** glTF extensions — so such a block is silently discarded at load and any assertion over it would sit green forever above a dead feature. **A placement pin must bound the node in both axes and from both sides**: 2.9 shipped a half-bound one that a node at `y = −100` would have passed, and the fix bounds it against `vis_playfield`'s own measured footprint rather than against the reference dimensions a second time, so it cannot drift with the calibration.
- **Tunables carry a `source` and a `confidence`, and correcting the prose is never free** — the resolver's entire serialized output is hashed, so a prose-only fix invalidates all five goldens exactly as a numeric change would. Budget a header-only re-record in the same change, or route it to a story already re-recording. A corrected bound can meanwhile be pinned *falsifiably* through the tuning resolver's override seam, which never mutates the production object and so perturbs no hash. **A new tunable must be pinned at its production magnitude, by consequence** — 2.9 shipped `ballSaveGraceMs` and then `ballSaveHurryUpMs` each settable to `1` with the whole suite green, twice, because every behavioural assertion ran under an *override* tuning so the production value was never on the path. Scoring values stay adjustable through Epic 2 and freeze only after Epic 3's first full playtest.
- **Story 2.8's lamp channel and its measured limitation stand unchanged, and Epic 2 must not pre-empt the fix.** In a real browser on the WebGL2 path a lit insert's *entire* visible signal is **7.8 luma out of 255 (~3%)**, and toggling the dynamic light changes the lit pixel by **0.00 luma** — the composited pixel is already at its ceiling well below the shipped emissive level. **The dynamic light is built, positioned, budgeted and transmissive but NOT yet visually load-bearing; the lit colour grammar is carried by the emissive material alone.** The instrument is named and repeatable (`window.__dragonwarBoot.setLightBudget(0)` isolates the light's own contribution). **The falsification condition is the part that matters downstream:** this is a limitation of the *scene*, not of the driver, so **the moment Epic 4 gives the scene headroom that same A/B goes non-zero — and if it does not, the driver has regressed.** Exposure/tonemapping and the driven GI contribution are **Story 4.1**'s; the playfield's alpha dilution over the lens is **Story 4.2**'s. AD-12 carries all of this as an amendment, so no story re-derives it.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera, no camera controls.** Both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp, the DRAGON bank and the backbox strip carrying the Backglass must all be legible from it; the quad was measured to project inside NDC with 0.2206 of headroom, so the camera was not moved.
- **The Backglass is a low-resolution dot-matrix display** — a visible dot grid, no smoothing, **128×32 and 1-bit** — showing every player's score with the current player highlighted, the ball number, and the highest-priority mode's name and whichever fields it publishes, converted to display units in presentation. **Its vertical budget is the live constraint and the author has decided the fix:** with four players the BALL row drops; at three or more the whole mode block drops; at two the mode *name* survives and only its optional fields drop — with the ball number given a shared or shortened line. Separately, `DmdRow.emphasis` is written and asserted but **no renderer reads it**, and the Attract screen emits bare unlabelled numbers, so the panel never identifies *which* player a score belongs to. **Both are routed to Story 2.13 as one problem, with a binding condition on its QA gate: the pinning test must assert the RENDERED DOTS DIFFER between an emphasised and an unemphasised row, never merely that the field is set.** Asserting a field nothing reads is precisely the vacuity that produced the defect.
- **The mode line shows a mode or nothing at all** — the base mode's internal identifier must never leak to the panel, and the fix is pinned on the rasterised dot buffer rather than on a helper's return value. **Its residual is live:** the fix generalises past the decision, so any unlabelled mode now suppresses its published fields too, and from Story 3.1 a higher-priority unlabelled mode would blank a labelled lower-priority one — which contradicts Story 2.6 AC 5, never amended. Harmless at this tree, because base is the only unlabelled mode.
- **The colour grammar is fixed, never varies, and is fully delivered across fifteen inserts:** white for a lit or qualifying shot, orange for DRAGON letters and the Lock, red for Hurry-up, green for Quick multiball, blue for Joust, purple for extra ball and special. Progression within a colour is a brightness and blink ladder expressed as the step value, with cadence timed by presentation and no command traffic from rules per blink. The ball-save insert uses the `lit` role at step 1, stepping to 3 for the hurry-up. **What is deferred is only the dynamic light's *visible* share of it**, and only until Epic 4 gives the scene headroom. A story driving a new lamp gets the full grammar; it does not get a visibly brighter table.
- **Flipper buttons move the lit insert** one position per press across the Top lanes and across the inlane/outlane set, wrapping. **Per the author's decision, lane change matters before the plunge, not during** — and the skill shot closes on the **first** playfield closure of any kind at or after `ball_launched`, Top lane included, with an **unlit** Top lane a MISS rather than a skip; only the lit lane pays. **The shipped code satisfies that operative rule but not yet its rationale**: lane change is applied for the whole ball and the skill shot re-derives the lit lane at the resolving closure, so a flipper press after launch still moves the paying lane. That gap is **decision-pending**, two lead-commissioned composition tests currently encode the live behaviour, and it is explicitly **coupled to Story 2.14**. **Whoever plans 2.14 should carry that answer into the same pass rather than edit those tests twice.** A second, newer composition question now sits beside it: **the skill shot survives a ball save**, so a ball saved from a no-switch centre drain gets a second skill-shot attempt — this time from an *automatic* launch with no player aim. Also decision-pending.
- **Presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.** An end-of-ball screen names the player from the event payload, never from the snapshot that has already advanced.
- **Attract in this epic is minimal**: the Backglass cycles the last game's scores with a start prompt and shows the flipper, plunge and Start keys once from the view configuration. The camera walk-up belongs to Epic 4.
