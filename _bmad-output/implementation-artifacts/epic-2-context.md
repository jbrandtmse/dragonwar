# Epic 2 Context: A Complete Game on the Real Shot Map

## Goal

Epic 2 turns Epic 1's placeholder vertical slice into a machine a stranger can play with no instructions. The real playfield geometry is drawn for the first time — the drain triangle, both Loops, the Ramp, the off-centre Dragon with the Lock lane between its legs, the DRAGON drop bank, Top lanes, slings and pops — and every device on it is wired into the table registry with a switch that registers reliably at the fastest speed the physics core can produce. On that geometry sits the whole standard game: Start and Hot seat for one to four players, the ball lifecycle, plunge and Skill shot, lane change, ball save, end-of-ball bonus and multiplier, tilt warnings, Tilt and Slam tilt, ball search, Match, game over and a return to a minimal Attract — all readable from a DMD Backglass with the inserts lit in the table's fixed colour grammar. Geometry was the first story and iterated with the rules that land on it; **the geometry, physics, device-vocabulary, lifecycle, display and lamp halves are all now delivered**, and what remains is the last band of rules stories plus one chartered correction to the skill shot.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **There is no deliberate red left in this project. Every gate is expected green, and a red one is a defect.** `check:ad7`, `check:corridor` and `check:reachability` all exit 0. The epic's long-standing "check:ad7 is red by design, route around it" framing is **spent** — do not re-derive it from an older artifact. Any stage that finds a red gate has found a regression, not the status quo.
- **Current measured baselines, recorded at Story 2.8's close (2026-09-07).** The suite is **112 files / 1824 tests / 0 failing / 0 skipped**, measured **with `BLENDER` exported** so the Blender-gated cases run rather than skip — with `BLENDER` unset those cases skip instead, which changes the counts, so **state which you did**. `pnpm lint:boundaries` reports `OK -- 104 .ts file(s)`. `check:ad7` exits 0 with **3** passing tests; `check:corridor` exits 0; `check:reachability` exits 0 over its **52** cases (32 reachable / 20 unreachable) from 644 swept releases, taking roughly two minutes. The five goldens share **`tableHash 672573c`** and **`assetHash ab163ff`**. The architecture spine is `final`, updated 2026-09-07, holding **19 ADs** with **`AD-20` the next claimable id**. A story reports these as it found them and explains any movement, and **re-measures at its own tree rather than carrying a figure forward** — Story 2.2 alone needed three separate corrections to stale recorded figures, and Story 2.8's own context pre-warm reported a stale `tableHash` that the planner caught by measuring. A `tableHash` string inside a dated historical sentence in `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` is a record, never the current value.
- **A gate is only evidence if its red was observed for the reason the story claims. Assume your instruments are vacuous until you have watched them go red.** Every acceptance criterion this epic has found pinned by a test that could not fail was caught by a deliberate falsification attempt — **not one by a passing run.** The running count is **42**, and the most instructive is the newest: Story 2.8 was a story about a light that changed no pixel, verified by assertions that would pass whether or not the light changed anything — the original defect reproduced one layer down from the fix for it. Repeatedly the vacuity was *inside a test written specifically to prevent an earlier vacuity*. Other shapes worth knowing: a signature fix with no executing assertion (replacing it one hop downstream left the whole suite green); an assertion on a display row the rasteriser silently drops; player scoping never exercised at index ≠ 0; a test that scripted its inputs on separate ticks, making its own named mutation a no-op; a spinner test so symmetric it could not tell closes from opens; a tunable entirely unenforceable with a green suite; an AC whose gate was **narrower than its own wording**; a kick-direction test that dotted a physics-frame velocity against a table-frame offset, so a full sign inversion made every case pass *more strongly*; and an assertion that compared a hand-built literal **against itself**. **Demonstrate the red through the observable the criterion names**, in a single coordinate frame, asserting per axis so neither can mask the other. Prefer re-deriving a mutation at the **authored source constant**, through the real export and physics pipeline, in an isolated copy rather than in the worktree. A pre-existing observable that already passes before the story ships is a **pre-loaded vacuity**.
- **A mutation harness can itself be vacuous, so state the expected red before running the mutation.** At Story 2.7's gate the runner's extractor grepped `[0-9]+ failed` and took `head -1`, matching vitest's **test-files** counter before its **tests** counter and under-reporting two mutations' red. It was caught only because the spec had *predicted* the expected red and the prediction and the measurement disagreed.
- **A whole class of defect is invisible to the suite, and the browser smoke gate is the only instrument that sees it.** `NullEngine` rasterises nothing, so no headless test can observe a rendered pixel; AD-15 forbids automated rendering assertions. Three separate defects have now passed straight through a green suite of ~1650–1822 tests for exactly this reason (a never-set `emissiveColor` that would have rendered a panel black; a UV ghost sliver read as a clipped text line; and fourteen insert lights that changed no pixel). **A presentation claim that only a rendered frame can settle must be measured in a real browser, and the measurement recorded with its numbers.**
- **Read a reporter contract, never scrape rendered output.** `test/ad7-device-slots.test.ts` spawns its nested harness with `--reporter=json --outputFile` and reads structured fields. **This shape is load-bearing and must not be undone.** A fix that scraped vitest's human summary line went red on Ubuntu while green on Windows — the runner colourises that line, so ANSI escapes land between `Tests` and the digit. A reporter contract cannot drift with a terminal's colour support; a human summary line can, and did. That gate is pinned against both anti-vacuity mutations: an **exact** passing count of 3 (never a `>=` form) catches a deleted `it()` block, and publishing the computed device-slot views on `task.meta` catches gutted bodies that keep their titles.
- **Ball save has a precise shape** (Story 2.9): one machine device owned by the ball controller with `arm({ ticks, source })` / `disarm(source)`; enabled at `ball_starting` with the timer **not** started, and started on `ball_launched`; a hurry-up display state for the last `ballSaveHurryUpMs`; a grace window past the *displayed* expiry during which a drain still saves; the ball controller **alone** pulses `c_trough_eject` then `c_autolaunch`; multiple arming sources stack with the longest live window winning; Tilt disarms all. The boundary test is drains at expiry − 1, expiry + grace − 1 and expiry + grace + 1.
- **Bonus is per category, multiplied, and forfeited on tilt** (Story 2.10): letters, Loops and Strikes each increment from device events; the multiplier starts each ball at 1× and advances 2× → 3× → 5× on each completed Top-lane set, capped at 5×; the `ball_ended` payload carries `bonusByCategory`, `multiplier` and the total, which is added to the score and paced to the Backglass by rules-emitted step events; a tilted ball pays 0 with the score unchanged; categories and multiplier reset at ball start while letters persist.
- **Tilt keeps the score and forfeits the bonus** (Story 2.11). Warnings are spaced by a semantic window with a default count of 1, and the bob is **never reset by command** — its physical decay plus a settle window is the settle. On Tilt, flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch are disabled together via `CoilCommand disable`, ball save is disarmed, and the ball ends when the last ball in play drains. Warnings are per player. Slam tilt ends every player's game without bonus and returns the machine to Attract.
- **Ball search never releases locked balls while a mode timer runs** (Story 2.12), escalates through each device's declared `ballSearchOrder`, cancels and restarts on any switch closure, and only at its final stage issues the single `RecoverCommand` that lets physics despawn balls outside a device — after which `ballsInPlay` is corrected **from slot switches**. The device failure vocabulary (`eject_failed`, `ball_missing`, `broken`, `device_overflow`) must be handled without throwing, and `device_overflow` answered with an immediate eject.
- **Match draws a multiple of ten from the seeded rules PRNG** at the configured probability (default 8%) and is display-only under free play (Story 2.13). Game over shows final scores by player; Attract returns after `attractMs` or on Start, with hardware disabled, `modes[]` empty and `players[]` cleared on the next Start.
- **The lit Top lane rotates; it is not drawn** (Story 2.14, chartered 2026-09-07 out of Story 2.7). PRD FR-18 describes the lit Top lane as *"rotating each plunge"* — a deterministic advance, not a uniform draw. The shipped uniform draw repeats one lane on all three balls in **11.20%** of games and on a consecutive pair in **55.51%** (measured over 200,000 seeds), against Story 2.7's own criterion that the lit lane differs across balls. **The author has decided the form in full: a seeded starting position drawn once per game from `GameState.rng`, then a deterministic one-lane advance per plunge.** That satisfies every word of the rotation decision (the lane advances in sequence each plunge; a three-ball repeat is impossible **by construction**), satisfies Story 2.7 AC 5 verbatim with **no rewording**, and keeps alive the only mutation-proven pin anywhere that `GameStart.seed` reaches `GameState.rng` and changes something observable. **A fixed start is the wrong answer and was explicitly reconsidered and rejected** — it would leave the shipped seed with no observable effect anywhere, reintroducing an already-closed user-visible defect in a new costume. The story also owes the amendment of two ratified artifacts (Story 2.7 AC 5 in `epics.md` and its spec's AC 6) in the same commit, with the reasoning recorded.
- **Every shot's miss must come back playable (Lawlor's test), verified per shot.** A feel-test entry lands for each of the seven shots — Left Loop, Right Loop, Ramp, Dragon, Lock lane, bank, Top lanes — recording where the most common miss goes; none may be a centre drain. Running the ritual is author-owned work at the Reference machine — the pipeline cannot close it, and the miss-destination judgement stands explicitly **`pending-author` and unwritten**, so no stage may record it as satisfied.
- **Per-player facts stay per player in Hot seat** — score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus and multiplier, extra balls, lit lanes. Delivered by 2.5, populated for the lane set by 2.7, projected to lamps by 2.8; the remaining stories consume it.
- **English literals live in the Backglass and nowhere else** in the simulation; rules never format text. A source-literal scan over `src/sim/` is one of Story 2.6's own acceptance assertions. **Its residual is live:** the scan's comment-stripping character scanner still desyncs on regex literals, recorded as an occurrence rather than re-filed, because the durable fix touches a CI gate.
- **Provenance is a hard gate** and the attribution entry lands before the file does, with the licence verified at its source. Two named traps stay closed around insert and lighting art: `vbousquet/pinball-parts`' "Thin Film Interaction" node group is **CC BY-NC-SA** and is exactly what one reaches for to make a lens look right — it cannot be used; and `vbousquet/vpx_lightmapper` has **no licence verified at source** (a research digest claiming GPL-3.0 is not verification) and no attribution row, so nothing may be taken from it.
- **`toPhysics()` negates y** (`y → playfieldHeightMm - y`), so a table-frame vector and a physics-frame vector point opposite ways on that axis. **Frame-mixing is a live hazard with a proven history on this epic.** Name the frame; assert in one frame, per axis.

## Technical Decisions

- **Story 2.8 delivered the lamp channel end to end, and its single most load-bearing output is a *measured limitation* recorded in its own amended acceptance criteria — read the amended text, never an older summary.** Shipped: fourteen `l_` insert lamps in `TABLE.lamps` (three Top lanes, four inlane/outlane, six DRAGON letters, the Lock) with lens-and-cup meshes recessed to table z = −0.3 mm; `lampsOf(state): LampState` as a pure projection in `src/sim/rules/lamps.ts`; the diff into `LampCommand`s in `sim/loop` (never in rules); `presentation/lighting/grammar.ts` as the one `(role, step)` → colour table; `presentation/lighting/lamp-driver.ts` driving each insert's cloned emissive material **and a genuinely transmissive lens over a dynamic light beneath it**; and the `sim-no-colour` boundary-lint rule. **What is measured and recorded:** in a real browser on the WebGL2 path a lit insert's *entire* visible signal is **7.8 luma out of 255 (~3%)** — the top-lane band max reads 194.7 unlit against 202.5 lit — and toggling the dynamic light on and off changes the lit pixel by **0.00 luma**. Dropping the emissive to 0.6 of the role colour to make headroom changes nothing either (the lit band max stays 202.53), because the composited pixel is **already at its ceiling at 0.6**; the real ceiling sits near raw emissive **0.12–0.15**. **The dynamic light is therefore built, positioned, budgeted and transmissive but NOT yet visually load-bearing, and the lit colour grammar is carried by the emissive material alone.** The instrument is named and repeatable: `window.__dragonwarBoot.setLightBudget(0)` disables the insert *lights* while leaving `emissiveColor` untouched, so an A/B against the default budget isolates the light's own contribution; the frame-decoding harness uses Node built-ins only. **The falsification condition is the part that matters downstream:** this is a limitation of the *scene*, not of the driver — `create-engine.ts` creates a single unparameterised `HemisphericLight` at default intensity and the render path configures no exposure or tonemapping at all — so **the moment Epic 4 gives the scene headroom, that same A/B goes non-zero, and whoever runs it should expect it to. If it does not, the driver has regressed.** The three levers and their owners are fixed: exposure/tonemapping and the driven GI contribution are **Story 4.1**'s; the playfield's alpha dilution over the lens is **Story 4.2**'s. **None is Epic 2's, and Epic 2 must not pre-empt them.** AD-12 carries all of this as an amendment, so no story re-derives it.
- **`lampsOf` is a pure projection with a closed vocabulary, and adding a lamp is not free.** Roles are `off · lit · hurryup · quickmb · joust · dragon · special`; steps are 0..3; **no RGB, hex or colour name may appear anywhere under `src/sim/`**, and one presentation-side table is the sole role-to-colour mapping. Lamp `subject` kinds today are `{ kind: 'lane', lane }`, `{ kind: 'letter', letter }` and `{ kind: 'lock' }`, constrained with `satisfies` against the wiring keys so a typo is a compile error. **Story 2.9 must add `l_ball_save` (a new subject kind) to drive role `lit` step 1 → step 3, and therefore owes its own header-only five-golden re-record — budget it at planning, not at implementation.** One live product question sits on the projection: `lampsOf()` resolves the `lock` subject *before* the "no base mode ⇒ all off" guard, so an occupied `bd_lock` lights `l_lock` in Attract and between balls, contradicting the frozen intent contract in three places. That is decision-pending on the burn-down sheet; the current behaviour is pinned by a test, and whichever side survives needs the other amended.
- **The golden budget: `tableHash` moves on any `TABLE` change; `assetHash` does not move on `l_` or `vis_` work.** `tableHash()` hashes the **whole frozen `TABLE`** in canonical JSON, so any new lamp, switch or device key moves it. `resolveTuning()`'s **entire serialized output** — `source` prose and `confidence` included — is compared as whole-tree canonical JSON, so a new tunable *or a prose-only provenance correction* moves `header.gameStart.tuning` and invalidates all five goldens before tick 1. `assetHash` hashes the **parsed collision document only**, which `tools/export.py` builds from three prefix filters (`col_`, `sw_`, `ballDevices`); an `l_` or `vis_` node matches none, so authoring insert meshes and re-exporting is free. **None of the five goldens presses `s_start`** — all five stay in `phase: 'attract'`, no mode is ever pushed, `rng` never advances, `lanes` is never written and `lampsOf` projects all-off throughout — which is why a lamps-and-tuning change is header-only by construction. **A moved state hash means something other than a header change happened: HALT and report, do not re-record around it.**
- **A golden is re-recorded only under an explicit author grant, never to make a suite green and never by switching a parity check off.** There is no recording tool — build the harness **in the scratchpad, never under `test/`** (a file matching `test/**/*.test.ts` silently joins `pnpm test`), over the shipped `runReplay` / `buildHeader` / `tableHash()` / `assetHash(doc)` / `PHYSICS_VERSION` surface, with a scratchpad vitest config naming the absolute harness path. Sample full-precision per-ball TSV rows on a tick stride into `trace-BEFORE` / `trace-AFTER` and diff them — **expect no output**; a drained ball emits no row, so a shifted drain shows as a *row-count* change. The measured corpus is **1,132 sampled rows** across the five (80 / 32 / 371 / 384 / 265); a materially different row count means the instrument, not the change, is wrong. **Append** to each `notes`, never rewrite. One known residual: the last refresh flipped `gameStart.tuning`'s key order from alphabetical to declaration order, producing ~1890 lines of pure reordering noise — functionally inert because the header comparison canonicalises, but the next re-record's throwaway harness decides the order again; **standardise it on `canonicalize()`** rather than flipping it back. A tick-rate or solver-constant change is a physics-version bump that re-records every golden and now fails as a **named** `StaleReplayHeaderError`.
- **AD-19's devices-and-shots layer is the substrate every remaining rules story consumes.** `src/sim/rules/devices/` is the **only** consumer of `SwitchEvent` and the build fails if anything else reaches for one. It owns the drop bank (and **alone** pulses the reset coil), the spinner count, ball-device slots, the shooter lane and the declared shots, emitting only payload-complete events: `shot_<name>_made` / `_broken`, `bank_target_down { letter }`, `bank_completed`, `dragon_hit`, `lock_lane_entered`, `spinner_spin { count }`, `lane_entered { lane }`, `lane_change_pressed { side }`, `ball_launched`, `device_ball_entered/left { device, slot }`, `button_pressed { button }` and `playfield_switch_closed { switch }`. **Modes, scoring and the ball controller consume device and shot events and never a raw switch.** Lane *state* (lit flags, completed sets) belongs to the base mode. **Two by-design gaps, so nobody files them as defects:** `ramp_made` is named in prose but never emitted (a made Ramp surfaces as `shot_ramp_made`), and the two Loops can never emit `_broken`.
- **`playfield_switch_closed` exists because "the first playfield closure" was otherwise undecidable, and its membership is DERIVED, never hand-typed.** Slingshots, pops, the drain and a bare ramp switch emit no device event at all, while AD-19 forbids a mode reading a raw `SwitchEvent`. The event fires on each `closed: true` edge of a set derived **by subtraction from `TABLE`** — every switch key, minus the button, tilt-bob and slam classes, minus every parking device's slots, minus each non-parking device's entry switch: **28 switches** at this tree. Adding a switch to the registry adds it to this set automatically; a hand-typed list would silently miss it. It is Story 2.12's natural answer to "has anything closed?" and Story 2.9's to "did the ball reach the playfield?".
- **`RulesStepResult.modeEvents` has NO production consumer, and wiring it is Story 2.10's work, not an assumption 2.10 may make.** `src/sim/rules/index.ts` produces `modeEvents` and the only reader anywhere is the test-side switch-script DSL; `src/sim/loop/index.ts` reads `.state`, `.events`, `.commands` and `.coilCommands` and never `.modeEvents`. So `lanes_completed { set: 'top' }` — the event Story 2.7 produced and the event 2.10's multiplier advances on — **cannot leave `rules.step()` in the shipped product**. The channel is deliberately separate and the event is correct where it is; the loop-side half is missing, and 2.10 adds it. This is already written into 2.10's acceptance criteria, not left ledger-only.
- **The rules→physics coil channel exists and the ball controller owns it.** `RulesStepResult.coilCommands` is queued into `pendingCommands` and consumed on the **next** tick (AD-4). `FrameOutput.commands` deliberately stays presentation-only (`RulesStepResult.commands` is typed `readonly never[]`). This is the channel through which the ball controller pulses `c_trough_eject`, `c_autolaunch` and `c_mouth` — and AD-4's *N+1* delivery is why a rules-issued eject cannot fire before tick 2, a constraint with downstream consequences for any story timing a serve.
- **`GameState` is one plain-data tree with fixed ownership scopes**, JSON-serializable, no class instances or closures, **mutated only inside the rules step — enforced by a source-level gate over `src/sim/loop/**` covering every field, not only the one that once broke.** Player-scoped (only under `players[i]`): score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus by category and multiplier, extra balls, lanes, Jackpot seed, Wars started — **and `ballNumber`, which already exists, is already player-scoped (correct for Hot seat), is already inside the hashed state and is already correct when the mode stack starts, so Story 2.14 needs no new state field for a per-ball advance.** Machine-scoped (only under `machine`): device slot states and `ballsInPlay`, `hardwareEnabled`, `ballSave`, `tilt`, `multiball`, read-only high scores. Mode-local: the mode's own timers and counters, published to presentation only as a typed view. The mode stack is empty between balls: ball-will-start resets ball save, tilt and multiball; ball-starting enables hardware.
- **AD-8's four-phase mode convention is PHASED, and an Epic 2 implementation that omits it is conforming, not violating.** Epic 2 ships a **minimal** stack — base 100 plus skill shot 200 — that starts and stops modes directly, with no lifecycle events and no registry. **Story 3.1** generalises it, and from 3.1 onward a direct start/stop path is a defect. The stack's own contract is likewise unsettled and belongs to 3.1: `pendingStartPlayer` lives in a **closure outside `GameState`**, so two states that hash identically step differently; nothing cancels a pending arm if the ball ends inside its one-tick window; and the fan-out is **mode-major** rather than event-major, which differ observably on a tick carrying both a lane-change press and the resolving closure.
- **One clock, no wall-clock or unseeded randomness in the simulation.** Timers are authored in milliseconds in the tuning file, converted to ticks once at load (`shotWindowTicks()` is the one sanctioned reader for the `…Ms` window tunables), and pace presentation by emitting step events presentation animates to — **presentation never reports completion**. All rules randomness draws from the seeded PRNG in game state. `src/host/game-seed.ts` derives a per-game seed via Web Crypto and `boot.ts` passes it through the existing `GameStart.seed` contract, so `sim/` still draws only from `GameState.rng` and replays stay reproducible because the header records the seed; the dev replay recorder keeps its fixed seed 0, correctly. **The lane draw is currently the ONLY consumer of `GameState.rng` in the shipped product** — Match is Story 2.13's and is unbuilt — so there is no stream-offset hazard today, **but `rng`'s own value is hashed, so whether Story 2.14's advance still consumes a step is hash-visible on its own.** Measured 2026-09-07: none of the five goldens ever starts a game (all five end at `rng = 0`, `phase = attract`), so 2.14 cannot redden a golden **today** — that stops being true once the goldens are re-recorded through the rules layer in Epic 3, which makes doing 2.14 now strictly cheaper. One residual: `hostLoop.reset()` does not carry the seed forward, so a tuning-panel hot-apply or dev reset silently restores the zero seed — dev-only paths, escalated to the burn-down sheet.
- **Shots are declared data, not code**, and `entryExclusive` records a **measured geometric fact, never a style choice**: `false` when something other than the shot also closes its own first switch. **Both Loops are `false`** — each `_in` zone spans its whole lane mouth, which also spans that side's outlane column, so **every outlane drain closes `s_loop_l_in` / `s_loop_r_in`, and so does a made Ramp**. A sequence with `entryExclusive: false` is tracked to completion but never emits `_broken` on expiry. The Ramp is `true`. **Never treat a bare `s_loop_*_in` as a Loop entry.** One open question sits here and **nobody should start it**: two consecutive same-side Loop orbits emit a spurious opposite-Loop made, bounded exactly by the Loop window; it is unreachable in play today but the inter-orbit margin was never measured, and the fix needs cross-shot state that all of Epic 3 plans against.
- **Hardware rules run inside the physics step.** Slingshots and pop bumpers join the flippers and plunger: switch closes at tick *t*, coil energises inside tick *t*'s own physics step with no rules round trip, gated only by coil enable/disable — which is the channel Tilt uses to disable flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch together. Disabled, they act as passive rubber and emit no actuation. Every actuation emits a contact event to presentation so each mechanical sound has exactly one source; **rules never receive a contact event.** Reporting that a coil *branch ran* is not reporting that an impulse was *delivered*.
- **Physics owns ball bodies and mechanical state; rules own accounting.** Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else. A ball device's boot occupancy is a **declared property of that device**, never a global constant, so the machine boots 4 balls. The shooter lane is non-parking, and **the *opening* of `s_shooter_lane` is the one event that means "plunged"** — the devices layer emits it as `ball_launched`, on which the ball controller increments `ballsInPlay`, starts the ball-save timer and arms the skill shot. `hardwareEnabled` boots `false` while physics coils still default enabled, so flippers, slings and pops are physically live through the machine's very first Attract; that is `wontfix-accepted` by measurement (a boot-disable batch diverged ball positions in two goldens, because Attract-phase actuation is those goldens' own premise) — **do not "fix" it without the grant a trajectory re-record needs.**
- **Single arbiters for the overloaded seams.** **Ball save** is one machine device the ball controller owns, armed and disarmed by source through a rules-internal interface, sources stacking with the longest live window winning, Tilt disarming all; only the ball controller pulses the trough-eject and autolaunch coils and mutates `ballsInPlay`. The **Lock arbiter** is Story 3.2's and does not exist in Epic 2 — which is why two known Lock residuals (a slow shot that parks without emitting `lock_lane_entered`, and the overflow eject that would resolve to a legitimately locked ball) are routed to 3.2 rather than handled here. **"A multiball is running"** is a flag set in a mode's starting phase and cleared in its stopped phase, never derived from balls in play.
- **The command union is closed.** Rules to physics: coil pulse/enable/disable, plus the **recover command for ball search alone**. Rules to presentation: lamp, GI, flasher and show commands, nothing else.
- **Layering is law and is linted.** Device-name string literals outside the table file and the test tree are errors, so every new switch, coil, lamp and device name enters through the registry — the `s_/c_/l_/f_/gi_/bd_/shot_/show_` prefixes are all covered. `tools/boundary-lint.mjs` also carries the `SwitchEvent` containment check, which is **textual, not a dependency-cruiser module rule** (with `tsPreCompilationDeps: false`, a type-only edge is indistinguishable from a value import), and the `sim-no-colour` rule. Three complementary provenance gates exist — a per-file **presence** check, a **structural** provenance test, and the **import** linter — and **none may be retired in favour of another**. One known narrowness: `sim-no-colour`'s matchers are narrower than their own AC's words (camelCase colour identifiers, reordered RGB keys, the numeric-array form and colour terms outside the sixteen-name list all pass today); widening was measured and is not free, so it is `wontfix-accepted` with a named reopen condition rather than an open task.
- **Rules tests run headless in Node with no physics or rendering loaded**, driven by the `SwitchName`-typed switch-script DSL. Every device and shot event needs at least one scripted test. **The headless claim is pinned by a transitive-closure import scan over resolved repo-relative paths, not by a comment** — its first two attempts were themselves vacuous — and the scan's entry list is **ratcheted**: a `test/rules-*.test.ts` file is headless unless named `*-integration.test.ts`, so the next omission reddens by name.
- **`BLENDER` must be exported by any stage doing Blender work, or the toolchain will report Blender as absent.** The executable is a **portable extract** at `C:/Users/Josh/tools/blender-5.2.1-windows-x64/blender.exe` — no registry entry, no conventional install location — which is why the resolver cannot find it unless `BLENDER` names it. **Four separate agents on this epic have wrongly concluded "Blender is not installed" from this**, and `AGENTS.md` contains zero occurrences of `BLENDER`, so nothing a fresh agent reads will tell it. **The path is a host-local fact: export it in the environment, and never let it enter `src/`, `tools/` or `test/`.** With `BLENDER` unset the Blender-gated cases skip — a skip, not a failure, so an unset variable looks like success rather than like a gap.
- **Blender owns placement, the registry owns wiring, the export script enforces the contract, and glTF cannot carry material behaviour this project does not register.** Node prefixes are load-bearing; both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property. **A material effect must be authored in the driver, never in the asset**: Blender exports only `KHR_materials_transmission`, Babylon maps that to *refraction* rather than translucency, and this project deliberately registers **no** glTF extensions — so such a block is silently discarded at load and any assertion over it would sit green forever above a dead feature.
- **Tunables carry a `source` and a `confidence`, and correcting the prose is never free** — the resolver's entire serialized output is hashed, so a prose-only fix invalidates all five goldens exactly as a numeric change would. Budget a header-only re-record in the same change, or route it to a story already re-recording. A corrected bound can meanwhile be pinned *falsifiably* through the tuning resolver's override seam, which never mutates the production object and so perturbs no hash. Scoring values stay adjustable through Epic 2 and freeze only after Epic 3's first full playtest.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera, no camera controls.** Both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp, the DRAGON bank and the backbox strip carrying the Backglass must all be legible from it; the quad was measured to project inside NDC with 0.2206 of headroom, so the camera was not moved.
- **The Backglass is a low-resolution dot-matrix display** — a visible dot grid, no smoothing, **128×32 and 1-bit** — showing every player's score with the current player highlighted, the ball number, and the highest-priority mode's name and whichever fields it publishes, converted to display units in presentation. **Its vertical budget is the live constraint and the author has decided the fix:** with four players the BALL row drops; at three or more the whole mode block drops; at two the mode *name* survives and only its optional fields drop — with the ball number given a shared or shortened line rather than its own. Separately, `DmdRow.emphasis` is written and asserted but **no renderer reads it**, and the Attract screen emits bare unlabelled numbers, so the panel never identifies *which* player a score belongs to. **Both are routed to Story 2.13 as one problem, with a binding condition on its QA gate: the pinning test must assert the RENDERED DOTS DIFFER between an emphasised and an unemphasised row, never merely that the field is set.** Asserting a field nothing reads is precisely the vacuity that produced the defect.
- **The mode line shows a mode or nothing at all** — the base mode's internal identifier must never leak to the panel, and the fix is pinned on the rasterised dot buffer rather than on a helper's return value. **Its residual is live:** the fix generalises past the decision, so any unlabelled mode now suppresses its published fields too, and from Story 3.1 a higher-priority unlabelled mode would blank a labelled lower-priority one — which contradicts Story 2.6 AC 5, never amended. Harmless at this tree, because base is the only unlabelled mode, publishes no fields, and is the lowest priority.
- **The colour grammar is fixed, never varies, and is fully delivered:** white for a lit or qualifying shot, orange for DRAGON letters and the Lock, red for Hurry-up, green for Quick multiball, blue for Joust, purple for extra ball and special. Progression within a colour is a brightness and blink ladder expressed as the step value, with cadence timed by presentation and no command traffic from rules per blink. **What is deferred is only the dynamic light's *visible* share of it** — in the current scene the grammar is carried by the insert's emissive material alone (0.00 luma from the light), and only until Epic 4 gives the scene headroom. A story driving a new lamp gets the full grammar; it does not get a visibly brighter table.
- **Flipper buttons move the lit insert** one position per press across the Top lanes and across the inlane/outlane set, wrapping. **Per the author's decision, lane change matters before the plunge, not during** — and the skill shot closes on the **first** playfield closure of any kind at or after `ball_launched`, Top lane included, with an **unlit** Top lane a MISS rather than a skip; only the lit lane pays. **The shipped code satisfies that operative rule but not yet its rationale**: lane change is applied for the whole ball and the skill shot re-derives the lit lane at the resolving closure, so a flipper press after launch still moves the paying lane. That gap is **decision-pending** (freeze the paying lane at `ball_launched`, or correct the rationale sentence in the spine and epics), two lead-commissioned composition tests currently encode the live behaviour, and it is explicitly **coupled to Story 2.14** — both are about which lane is lit and when it may move, both rest on the same FR-18 sentence, and both are load-bearing for the same two tests. **Whoever plans 2.14 should carry that answer into the same pass rather than edit those tests twice.**
- **Presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.** An end-of-ball screen names the player from the event payload, never from the snapshot that has already advanced.
- **Attract in this epic is minimal**: the Backglass cycles the last game's scores with a start prompt and shows the flipper, plunge and Start keys once from the view configuration. The camera walk-up belongs to Epic 4.
