## Goal

Epic 2 turns Epic 1's placeholder vertical slice into a machine a stranger can play with no instructions. The real playfield geometry is drawn for the first time — the drain triangle, both Loops, the Ramp, the off-centre Dragon with the Lock lane between its legs, the DRAGON drop bank, Top lanes, slings and pops — and every device on it is wired into the table registry with a switch that registers reliably at the fastest speed the physics core can produce. On that geometry sits the whole standard game: Start and Hot seat for one to four players, the ball lifecycle, plunge and Skill shot, lane change, ball save, end-of-ball bonus and multiplier, tilt warnings, Tilt and Slam tilt, ball search, Match, game over and a return to a minimal Attract — all readable from a DMD Backglass with the inserts lit in the table's fixed colour grammar. Geometry is the first story and iterates with the rules that land on it.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **There is no deliberate red left in this project. Every gate is expected green, and a red one is a defect.** `pnpm check:ad7` **exits 0** with **3 passing tests**; `check:corridor` and `check:reachability` also exit 0. `DW-70` was resolved by Story 2.5 and `DW-194` — a genuinely red full suite, where the `wedge.harness.ts` inner `it()` blew vitest's *default* 5000 ms under full-suite contention while the DW-8 guard it serves is a separate and generous 30 s outer timeout — was closed at 2.6 by giving that inner case an explicit 15 s timeout and leaving the outer guard untouched. **The epic's long-standing "check:ad7 is red by design, route around it" framing is spent — do not re-derive it from an older artifact.** Any stage that finds a red gate has found a regression, not the status quo.
- **Read a reporter contract, never scrape rendered output.** `test/ad7-device-slots.test.ts` — the in-suite wrapper over that gate — spawns the nested harness with `--reporter=json --outputFile` and reads structured fields (`numPassedTests`, `numTotalTests`, `assertionResults[].fullName`, `assertionResults[].meta`). **This shape is load-bearing and must not be undone.** Story 2.5's first fix scraped vitest's human summary line and **CI went red on Ubuntu while the file was green on Windows**: the runner colourises that line, so ANSI escapes land between `Tests` and the digit and the regex returned nothing — unreproducible on Windows by construction. `DW-107` had already closed this exact failure class in Epic 1 by adopting the JSON reporter. Generalise it: **a reporter contract cannot drift with a terminal's colour support; a human summary line can, and did.**
- **That gate is pinned against both anti-vacuity mutations, and both pins are deliberate.** Deleting one of the harness's three `it()` blocks is caught by an **exact** passing count of 3 (never a `>=` form). Gutting all three bodies in place while keeping their titles is caught because the harness publishes the device-slot views it actually **computed** on `task.meta`, which the JSON reporter carries verbatim — the second pin exists because the previous one was satisfiable by a test *title*. A third, independent gate in the same file is a **source-level scan of `src/sim/loop/**`** covering *every* `GameState` field, not only `deviceSlots`: AC 1's wording is repo-wide, and re-introducing DW-70's defect class on `players` had left the whole suite green.
- **The shot map is the epic's foundation, is drawn from the reference dimensions alone, and every clause of it is delivered**: both Loops with entries and exits, one Ramp with an authored height and a decided return inlane (right), the Dragon off-centre with the Lock lane between its legs and a Mouth eject pose, the six-target DRAGON bank, **three** Top lanes, two slingshots, **three** pop bumpers, two inlanes, two outlanes and the plunger lane, all as `col_` and `sw_` nodes under the prefix contract — with at least one `sw_` zone per zone-requiring switch, more where a device presents separate faces (`s_dragon_body` is served by `sw_dragon_body_l` / `sw_dragon_body_r`, one per Dragon leg). The plunge path is separated from the Loop channel: a plunged ball clears the Loop entrance, crosses the top and reaches the left flipper unaided — measured 3.66 mm closest approach and 438 ticks in the flipper band, against 249.40 mm and zero before. - **The routing harness's pin was repaired before it judged any new geometry, and no later story may weaken it.** `test/shot-routing.test.ts`'s `assertReachesFlipperBandOrLeavesPlay` was `reachedFlipperBand || leftPlay` with `leftPlay` set when the ball **drained**, so the two outcomes the criterion exists to distinguish were indistinguishable; four cases also closed their primary switch only because the harness's teleport landed the ball *inside* the zone under test. Both are fixed, and the repair was demonstrated **red against the geometry it then went on to judge**. **The standing rule is the durable part:** a story that relaxes an assertion here rather than fixing the geometry underneath it is repeating that defect, not resolving it.
- **A shot case that starts from a teleported ball proves nothing about whether the shot exists.** Every release point lives in one manifest, a single `driveCase(id)` entry point is the only way to drive one, and non-teleporting witness trajectories (originating at a plunge or at a bat, under the real physics pipeline) measure each release point's closest approach. An anti-vacuity floor makes a run that evaluates zero cases fail loudly. **Its measured baseline is a fact to preserve, not a number to edit:** `check:reachability` exits 0 over **52 driven cases — 32 proven reachable, 20 recorded unreachable** — from **644 swept releases**. 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6 and 2.7 all left that baseline unchanged with zero moved verdicts (2.6 re-exported, but the collision document came back byte-identical). The release count is the sweep's own recipe count and moves independently of the case manifest — re-measure both rather than deriving one from the other. **The single-origin limitation that once qualified this baseline is closed:** 2.1f added a **right-bat witness family** (which is why the Ramp could not previously be proved reachable at any corridor width); the dense sweep now drives both bat sides and asserts that it does, and the witness-corpus floor is a derived origin-family check — plunge-only, plunge-then-bat-l and plunge-then-bat-r must each be represented. The remaining unreachable set is a `DW-138` cohort the lead is harvesting. A case proved unreachable **fails, naming the case and the closest approach achieved**, and is marked against its owning ledger entry rather than deleted, weakened or quietly re-pointed at a reachable start. The same blind spot has a dimensional twin — an inlane feed guide can be shifted 20 mm or deleted with every routing case green, because the behavioural observable cannot distinguish "delivered onto the bat" from "still on the rail above it". A proximity record falsifies that one; the dimensional gates remain the primary catch.

- **Switch-edge semantics are settled, correct on both edges, and carry a device-ownership carve-out.** The tracker latches a make on the tick it observes the zone, and `settleTicks` gates the **break**, never the make; the residual break-side off-by-one was fixed in 2.1d across **both** trackers in one change — correcting either alone turns the tracker-agreement test red — with `settleTicks = 0` still a fixed point and no golden hash moved. **A switch owned end to end by a device module is excluded from the tracker entirely, so its `settleClass` no longer gates its break** *(AD-2 amended 2026-09-05 at Story 2.3)*. **The excluded set is derived from `TABLE`, never from name literals**, and covers every parking device's slots (`s_trough_1..4`, `s_lock_1..3`), the drop bank's six letters, and `s_spinner`. **The trap to carry forward:** `s_dragon_*` still declare `settleClass: 'drop_target'` (20 ms) in the registry, but nothing in production reads that class for them any more — the declaration is inert for the bank, and a story reading only the class would wrongly assume the settle window still applies.
- **Switches register at maximum ball speed or the geometry is wrong.** Zone tests use the ball's per-tick swept segment, never its end position, and a test asserts that the fastest ball a full-strength plunge and flipper hit can produce misses no pass through a rollover, the bank, the Lock lane or the drain — each switch closing exactly once per pass.
- **`bd_lock` behaves, and every later story consumes that delivered state rather than re-deriving it.** Boot occupancy is a **declared per-device property**, so the machine boots **4** balls, not seven; the four-ball invariant is checked *by name* at construction. `c_mouth` ejects exactly one ball that stays out. The Lock lane's slot zones sit in the Dragon-leg corridor, sealed from the north by `col_lock_ceiling` / `col_lock_ceiling_west_fill`. **Geometry work in the bottom-right must not assume these bodies are absent** — the west fill occupies a band a naive sweep will read as open field and mis-measure.
- **Physics device behaviour is delivered (Story 2.3).** The six drop targets fall on a genuine strike, go non-collidable, stay down through further contact and rise together on `pulse c_dragon_bank_reset` with their switches opening as edges and a `bank_reset` contact. The spinner free-runs and decays off a zone crossing, closing `s_spinner` once per revolution with strictly-increasing inter-closure gaps; a faster ball produces more closures. The Lock parks a precise shot in the lowest empty slot, ejects one ball per `c_mouth` pulse from the highest filled slot, and a slightly-off shot strikes `col_dragon_leg_l` / `col_dragon_leg_r` and closes `s_dragon_body` instead. **Overflow has a delivered physics shape whose rules obligation now belongs to Epic 3:** with three balls parked, a fourth entering the lane is not parked, stays in the simulated set at the lane, and produces **exactly one** `device_ball_entered` at a slot beyond capacity (measured at 315 events before the fix, corrected to 1). The AC's original "rests at the lane's entry" wording was **measured false and amended** — the ball sits in the slot band ~315 ticks, rolls back down the corridor and drains. AD-6's immediate-eject answer is `DW-174`, **routed to Story 3.2**.
- **Every shot's miss must come back playable (Lawlor's test), verified per shot.** A feel-test entry lands for each of the seven shots — Left Loop, Right Loop, Ramp, Dragon, Lock lane, bank, Top lanes — recording where the most common miss goes; none may be a centre drain, and any shot that fails is re-drawn before its story closes. Running the ritual is author-owned work at the Reference machine — the pipeline cannot close it, and the miss-destination judgement stands explicitly **`pending-author` and unwritten**, so no stage may record it as satisfied.
- **Per-player facts stay per player in Hot seat** — score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus and multiplier, extra balls, lit lanes. Delivered by 2.5 and populated for the lane set by 2.7; later stories consume it.
- **Ball save has a precise shape**: enabled at launch with the timer starting on the launch event, not on enable; a hurry-up display state before expiry; a grace window past the *displayed* expiry during which a drain still saves; saved balls auto-launch; multiple arming sources stack with the longest live window winning; Tilt disarms all.
- **Tilt keeps the score and forfeits the bonus.** Warnings are spaced by a semantic window (default count 1) and the bob is never reset by command — its physical decay plus a settle window is the settle. On Tilt, flippers, slings, pops and autolaunch are disabled together and the ball ends when the last ball in play drains. Slam tilt ends every player's game, returns the machine to Attract, and lives inside the replay.
- **Ball search never releases locked balls while a mode timer runs**, escalates through each device's declared order, and only at its final stage issues the one command that lets physics despawn balls outside a device.
- **Match draws a multiple of ten from the seeded rules PRNG** at the configured probability (default 8%) and is display-only under free play.
- **English literals live in the Backglass and nowhere else** in the simulation. Rules never format text. **Story 2.6 gave that rule its home and its gate:** a source-literal scan over `src/sim/` is one of 2.6's own acceptance assertions. **Its residual is live and worth knowing before trusting it:** the scan's comment-stripping was rewritten from a naive regex to a character scanner tracking quote state, but code review then found the replacement still **desyncs on regex literals** — `DW-195`'s own bug class one level down — recorded as an occurrence on that entry rather than re-filed, because the durable fix touches a CI gate.
- **Two distinct open questions are answered and recorded in the decisions document. Do not conflate them.** **OQ-6 is the playfield geometry itself** — flipper tip gap, outlane widths, post positions, loop entries, Ramp height and its return side (decided: right inlane, delivered by 2.1f), Dragon placement, and the spinner model. **OQ-5 is whether the Lock lane carries both lock and mode start.** If it does not, a separate `sw_scoop` and `bd_scoop` are added with the Mouth as eject only — a table-and-geometry change, not an architecture change, because the Lock arbiter decides from state.
- **A tunable's `source` *and* `confidence` strings are hashed into every golden replay's header** — the tuning resolver's entire serialized output is hashed, not just its numeric values, so correcting provenance prose *in place* invalidates all five goldens before tick 1, exactly as a numeric change would. Measured at 2.2: a prose-only fix took the suite from 93 files / 1488 passing to 91 / 1454 with 34 skipped, reproduced 3/3, with reverting the string restoring it every time. **A provenance correction is never free.** Budget a header-only five-golden re-record in the same change, or route it to a story already re-recording. A corrected bound can meanwhile be pinned *falsifiably* through the tuning resolver's override seam, which never mutates the production object and so perturbs no hash.
- **A golden is re-recorded only under an explicit author grant, never to make a suite green and never by switching a parity check off.** All five were re-recorded under grant with 2.1d's `bd_lock` boot fix, each traced correct *before* recording with the reasoning written into its own `notes`. 2.1f, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5 each carried one further re-record and **all five were header-only**. **2.6 and 2.7 carried none** — 2.6 re-exported the `.blend` and the collision document came back byte-identical, and 2.7 touched no asset or tunable. The five goldens currently share **`tableHash a2f90b52`** and **`assetHash ab163ff`**. **Any story that re-exports geometry or moves a tunable owes one re-record — budget it, and batch every such change into that single pass.** A tick-rate or solver-constant change is a physics-version bump that re-records every golden; since `DW-87`, that now fails as a **named** `StaleReplayHeaderError` rather than a bare hash mismatch.
- **Provenance is a hard gate** and the attribution entry lands before the file does, with the licence verified at its source.
- **A gate is only evidence if its red was observed for the reason the story claims. Assume your instruments are vacuous until you have watched them go red.** Every acceptance criterion this epic has found pinned by a test that could not fail was caught by a deliberate falsification attempt — **not one by a passing run.** The running count stood at **28** when Story 2.7's harvest filed `DW-203`, and 2.7's code review then proved **three more against a fully green 1697-test suite**: (1) the story's signature fix — the per-game seed — had no executing assertion, so replacing `rng: gameStart?.seed ?? 0` with `rng: 0` one hop downstream in `sim/loop/index.ts` left the entire suite green; (2) an insert-row assertion could not *see* the row it asserted on, because the builder placed it at dot row 800+ where the rasteriser drops it **silently** — the same shape as 2.6's left-margin column; and (3) AD-7 player scoping was never exercised, so replacing `active.player` with the literal `0` passed everything. Repeatedly the vacuity was *inside a test written specifically to prevent an earlier vacuity*. Other shapes worth knowing: a middle-count test that scripted its letters on separate ticks (making its own named mutation a no-op); an AC whose named mutation changed nothing because the runner re-groups edges by tick; a spinner test so symmetric in closed/open edges that it could not tell closes from opens; a tunable (`loopWindowMs`) entirely unenforceable with a green suite; a Hot-seat isolation test that never moved `currentPlayer` away from 0; an AC whose gate was **narrower than its own wording**; a terminal state inferred from a switch-make rather than read from an actual capture; a kick-direction test that dotted a physics-frame velocity against a table-frame offset so a full sign inversion made every case pass *more strongly*; and an assertion that compared a hand-built literal **against itself**. **The warning worth carrying: a test written to close a verification gap is not itself exempt from being verified, and neither is the fix for one.** Demonstrate the red *through the observable the criterion names*, in a single coordinate frame, asserting per axis so neither can mask the other. Prefer re-deriving a mutation at the **authored source constant**, through the real export and physics pipeline, in an isolated copy rather than in the worktree. A pre-existing observable that already passes before the story ships is a **pre-loaded vacuity**.
- **A mutation harness can itself be vacuous.** At 2.7's own gate the runner's extractor grepped `[0-9]+ failed` and took `head -1`, which matches vitest's **test-files** counter before its **tests** counter, under-reporting two mutations' red. It was caught only because the spec had *predicted* the expected red up front and the prediction and the measurement disagreed. **State the expected red before running the mutation.**
- **`toPhysics()` negates y** (`y → playfieldHeightMm - y`), so a table-frame vector and a physics-frame vector point opposite ways on that axis. **Frame-mixing is a live hazard with a proven history on this epic.** Assert in one frame, per axis.

The architecture spine is `final`, **updated 2026-09-06, holding 19 ADs** with `AD-20` the next claimable id. A story reports these as it found them and explains any movement, and **re-measures at its own tree rather than carrying a figure forward**: Story 2.2 alone needed three separate corrections to stale recorded figures, every one caused by reusing a baseline count instead of re-running the command.

## Technical Decisions

- **Story 2.8's substrate does not exist yet, and this is the same shape 2.6 found with the backbox quad — an acceptance criterion assuming a surface no earlier story ever built.** Verified at this tree: `TABLE.lamps` holds **exactly one** entry, `l_insert_left` (Epic 1's single placeholder insert, authored in `tools/make-placeholder-blend.py` under `lightgroup='lg_inserts'`); **`src/sim/rules/lamps.ts` does not exist**, so there is no `lampsOf()`; and **`src/presentation/lighting/` holds only a `.gitkeep`** — no `grammar.ts`, no `LampDriver`. `lg_inserts` is declared and otherwise unused. The lamp names 2.8's own AC 5 needs (a lit Top lane, a lit inlane/outlane, the dropped letters, the Lock) have no registry entries and no meshes. **Budget the golden consequence, which inverts 2.6's finding:** `tableHash()` hashes the **whole frozen `TABLE`** in canonical JSON, so *any* new `TABLE.lamps` key moves it and reddens all five goldens — a header-only five-golden re-record under explicit author grant. `assetHash` is the opposite case and stays put: it hashes the **parsed collision document only**, which `tools/export.py` builds from three prefix filters (`col_`, `sw_`, `ballDevices`) that an `l_` node matches none of — the identical reasoning 2.6 measured for `vis_`, twice, independently, getting a byte-identical `collision.json`. So authoring insert meshes and re-exporting is free; declaring their names is not.
- **`BLENDER` must be exported by any stage doing Blender work, or the toolchain will report Blender as absent.** The executable is a **portable extract** at `C:/Users/Josh/tools/blender-5.2.1-windows-x64/blender.exe` (5.2.1 LTS) — no registry entry, no conventional install location — which is exactly why `resolveBlender()` (`tools/blender.mjs`) cannot find it unless `BLENDER` names it. **Three separate agents on this epic have wrongly concluded "Blender is not installed" from this** (`DW-132`, again during 2.5, and again at 2.6, where the lead searched both Program Files trees, `LOCALAPPDATA/Programs` and both uninstall registry hives before escalating). `AGENTS.md` still contains **zero** occurrences of `BLENDER` — re-verified at this tree — which is `DW-193`'s whole complaint, so nothing a fresh agent reads will tell it. **The path is a host-local fact: export it in the environment, and never let it enter `src/`, `tools/` or `test/`** (`DW-46`'s manual check, and `DW-131`, which records that several tracked `_bmad-output` planning artifacts already carry it; both routed to Story 6.7). With `BLENDER` unset the Blender-gated cases skip — a skip, not a failure, so an unset variable looks like success rather than like a gap.
- **The Backglass and the backbox quad are DELIVERED, and the camera was NOT re-aimed.** `src/presentation/backglass/` now holds `backglass.ts`, `font.ts`, `frame.ts` and `raster.ts`, and `vis_backbox` is authored in the Blender source and exported. 2.6's predicted camera re-aim proved unnecessary — measured twice independently (a real Babylon `NullEngine` scene against the committed glb, and by hand from the `LookAtRH` basis, agreeing to five decimals): every corner of the quad projects inside NDC `[-1, 1]` with 0.2206 of headroom. `createFixedCamera()` (`src/presentation/camera/fixed-camera.ts`) and `test/scene-smoke.test.ts` were left untouched, and `assetHash ab163ff` / `tableHash a2f90b52` did not move.
- **The one-material-slot UV trap is the most transferable thing 2.6 found, and it is invisible to the whole test suite.** AD-11's export contract gives each mesh **exactly one** material slot, while Blender's `uv_layers.new()` gives **every face its own full `[0,1]` UV square** — so the emissive DMD texture mapped onto the player-facing face of a six-face box also lit the other five. Seen from the fixed camera's elevation the thin **top** face renders nearly edge-on and threw a ghost sliver directly above the top text row, which read as a *clipped first line*. Three plausible causes (the DMD-face UV extent, the RawTexture v-axis, the quad z-extent-vs-anchor) were each individually measured and **ruled out**. The fix was geometric — `BACKBOX_DMD_THICK_MM` 5.0 → 1.0 plus a re-export — because no material-level fix exists under the one-slot rule. **Two consequences to carry:** a mesh that must show different content per face cannot do it with one material slot, so make it thin or split it; and this was the **second** 2.6 defect that every one of ~1650 headless tests passed straight through (the first: `mat_backbox` never set `emissiveColor`, which would have rendered the panel fully black). **A presentation defect only a rendered frame can show needs the browser smoke gate — AD-15 forbids automated rendering assertions, so the suite structurally cannot see it.**

- **`playfield_switch_closed` exists because "the first playfield closure" was otherwise undecidable, and its membership is DERIVED, never hand-typed.** Slingshots, pops, the drain and a bare ramp switch emit no device event at all, while AD-19 forbids a mode reading a raw `SwitchEvent` — so a mode could not answer "has anything on the playfield closed yet?". The event fires on each `closed: true` edge of a set derived **by subtraction from `TABLE`**: every `TABLE.switches` key, minus the button, tilt-bob and slam classes, minus every parking device's slots, minus each non-parking device's entry switch — **28 switches at this tree**. Adding a switch to the registry therefore adds it to this set automatically; a hand-typed list would silently miss it.
- **The skill shot closes on the FIRST playfield closure of any kind at or after `ball_launched`, Top lane included, and an UNLIT Top lane is a MISS, not a skip** *(Story 2.7 AC 2, `[AMENDED 2026-09-06]`, author-decided; AD-6 amended to match in the same commit)*. Only the **lit** Top lane pays — the skill-shot value from `tuning.ts` plus one DRAGON letter — and any other first closure stops the mode with no award. **This resolved a real conflict, so do not re-derive the old reading from an older artifact:** AD-6's parenthetical previously read "closes on the next playfield closure that is *not* a Top lane", which would have let the ball rattle through unlit lanes and still pay on the lit one, with lane change live during flight. PRD FR-18 ("the Skill shot is only available until the first other switch closes") and the AC's own "before any other playfield switch closes" both said otherwise, and the author decided the FR-18 reading: **lane change matters before the plunge, not during.** The old wording is now read as shorthand for "not the Top lane you were aiming at". **The shipped code satisfies the operative Rule but not yet that rationale** — `base.ts` applies `lane_change_pressed` for the whole ball and the skill shot re-derives the lit lane at the resolving closure, so a flipper press after `ball_launched` still moves the paying lane. Two lead-commissioned tests currently encode the live behaviour.

Verified at this tree: `src/sim/rules/index.ts` produces `modeEvents` and the **only** reader anywhere is `test/util/switch-script.ts`; `src/sim/loop/index.ts` reads `rulesResult.state`, `.events`, `.commands` and `.coilCommands` and never `.modeEvents`. So `lanes_completed { set: 'top' }` — the event Story 2.7 produced and the event 2.10's bonus multiplier advances on — **cannot leave `rules.step()` in the shipped product**. The channel is deliberately separate from `events` and the event itself is correct where it is; what is missing is the loop-side half. 2.10 adds it.
- **The rules→physics coil channel exists and the ball controller owns it.** `RulesStepResult.coilCommands` is queued into `pendingCommands` and consumed on the next tick (AD-4). `FrameOutput.commands` deliberately stays presentation-only (`RulesStepResult.commands` is typed `readonly never[]`). This is the channel through which the ball controller pulses `c_trough_eject`, `c_autolaunch` and `c_mouth`. **AD-4's *N+1* delivery is why a rules-issued eject cannot fire before tick 2** — a constraint with downstream consequences (see `DW-175`).
- **Shots are declared data, not code.** `TABLE.shots` is declared: `shot_left_loop = [s_loop_l_in, s_loop_l_out]` and `shot_right_loop` within `loopWindowMs` (600), `shot_ramp = [s_ramp_enter, s_ramp_made]` within `rampWindowMs`. In order inside the window emits made; a broken sequence or an expired window emits broken; a Loop taken the wrong way emits nothing. `ShotName` is `keyof typeof TABLE.shots`. **`entryExclusive` records a MEASURED geometric fact, never a style choice** (`DW-133`): `false` when something other than the shot also closes its own first switch, `true` when nothing does. **Both Loops are `false`** — each `_in` zone spans its whole lane mouth, which also spans that side's outlane column, so **every outlane drain closes `s_loop_l_in` / `s_loop_r_in`, and so does a made Ramp** (measured `firstMakes` on a made Ramp: `s_ramp_enter, s_ramp_made, s_loop_r_in, s_inlane_r`). A sequence with `entryExclusive: false` is tracked to completion but never emits `_broken` on expiry, because emitting it would fire on both the outlane drain and the made Ramp. **The Ramp is `true`**, so a lapsed Ramp window is a real, falsifiable rejection. **Never treat a bare `s_loop_*_in` as a Loop entry.**
- **`DW-173` is an OPEN QUESTION awaiting the author's decision sheet, not work anyone should start.** Two consecutive same-side Loop orbits emit a spurious `shot_<other>_loop_made`: every made Loop ends by closing the opposite Loop's entry switch, arming it, and the next orbit's opposite `_out` completes that stale flight. The boundary is exactly `loopWindowTicks` (600) — measured spurious at 240 and 440 ticks of inter-orbit gap, clean at 640+. It is not reachable in play today (it needs a re-shot faster than a ball can return to the flipper), but **the inter-orbit margin was never measured**. Fix-risk is HIGH: distinguishing a stale arm from a real entry needs cross-shot state, which changes `DW-133`'s resolution that all of Epic 3 consumes. Affects FR-36 (Joust's Loop alternation). **Escalated; do not patch it blind.**
- **`lock_lane_entered` no longer over-reports, and the residual is Story 3.2's to decide.** `DW-166` is closed: the event fires only on a **resolved** lane closure, and the 550–600 mm/s band now produces silence (verified non-vacuous by deleting the gate, which reddens four tests including the real-physics 575 mm/s case, while the same test still asserts `s_lock_lane` *does* close). **`DW-166`'s own long-standing wording — "never reaches a slot" — was measured false.** The real discriminator is capture **latency**: at 575 mm/s `s_lock_lane` closes at tick 415 and `s_lock_1` at 696 (281 ticks, after rattling back down the corridor); at 800 mm/s it is 378 and 512 (134 ticks). `lockCaptureWindowMs` (180) sits inside that gap. **The surviving consequence is `DW-171`, routed to Story 3.2:** a shot slow enough to miss the window **still parks in `bd_lock`** — `s_drain` never closes, the ball is physically held, `device_ball_entered` fires and `lock_lane_entered` does not. AD-18 makes the arbiter the sole consumer of that event and the only pulser of `c_mouth`, so only Story 3.2 can set the policy. **A geometry change is the wrong answer** — it would undo the wedge fix 2.1c's bevel reversal exists for.
- **Hardware rules run inside the physics step.** Slingshots and pop bumpers join the flippers and plunger: switch closes at tick *t*, coil energises inside tick *t*'s own physics step with no rules round trip, gated only by coil enable/disable. Disabled, they act as passive rubber and emit no actuation. Every actuation emits a contact event to presentation so each mechanical sound has exactly one source; rules never receive a contact event. **They sit at different points in the tick — copy that shape rather than re-deriving it.** The slingshot *is* the ported model, registered per coil through a thin subclass that never edits the frozen port (`DW-79` — construct, subclass, inject), so its parabolic kick fires **inside the collision solve**. The pop bumper is an authored skirt device needing a switch *edge*, which exists only after the switch tracker steps, so it fires **after** that call, as an impulse directed radially away from the bumper's own footprint centroid. Three consequences worth inheriting: kick **magnitude alone cannot break a reflection symmetry** — a ball descending exactly onto an apex vertex needs a fixed, deterministic lateral floor, never randomness; reporting that a coil *branch ran* is not reporting that an impulse was *delivered*; and two `sw_pop_*` skirt zones overlap in committed geometry, so one ball can make two pop switches on a tick and fire two near-cancelling coils (`DW-161`, escalated rather than patched, because the root fix is `sw_` geometry and another five-golden re-record).
- **`popKickMmPerS` ships at 200 and is protected rather than safe.** There is **no floor near 180** — virtually any positive kick clears the stranding case, down to 0.5 mm/s — and the landscape is **chaotic in both directions**, with the nearest re-strand at **198 mm/s, two mm/s *below* the shipped value**, and a further cliff at 221. Measured neighbours: 198 → re-strand, 199 → 126.79, 200 → 126.84, 202 → 309.72. No nearby value is better. Sentinels sit on the clearing pocket's ends so a future physics change that lands on a re-strand reddens loudly; deliberately **not** a pin on 198's re-strand, which would go red if a future change *widened* the margin.
- **Physics owns ball bodies and mechanical state; rules own accounting.** The Lock is a parking device (capacity 3, filling the lowest empty slot, ejecting the highest filled slot at the Mouth pose, one ball per pulse). **A ball device's boot occupancy is a declared property of that device, never a global constant** — a registry contract every future ball device inherits. The shooter lane is non-parking with the served ball resting simulated on the plunger tip, and **the *opening* of `s_shooter_lane` is the one event that means "plunged"** — the devices layer emits it as `ball_launched`, on which the ball controller increments `ballsInPlay`, starts the ball-save timer and arms the skill shot. Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else. A dropped target is non-collidable until the bank reset coil raises it. **The spinner is a pass-through gate, not a collision body** — thirteen-plus rigid-body variants were measured across both candidate ball paths and every one that genuinely contacted the ball produced a permanent stall, because a static rigid body cannot graze a 13.495 mm-radius ball with the precision those paths allow; the analytic swept-segment zone test is the physically correct model here, not a workaround. A node nothing collides with is not `col_`, so the spinner's node is `vis_spinner_l` and is a presentation mesh.
- **`GameState` is one plain-data tree with fixed ownership scopes** — JSON-serializable, no class instances or closures, **mutated only inside the rules step, and that is now enforced by a source-level gate over `src/sim/loop/**`, not merely documented**. **Player-scoped** (only under `players[i]`): score, DRAGON letters, Lock credits, modes played, tilt warnings, bonus by category and multiplier, extra balls, lanes (owned by the base mode; the skill-shot mode writes the lit Top lane once on `ball_starting` from the PRNG and never again), Jackpot seed and Wars started. **Machine-scoped** (only under `machine`): device slot states and `ballsInPlay`, `hardwareEnabled`, `ballSave`, `tilt`, `multiball`, read-only high scores. **Mode-local**: the mode's own timers and counters, published to presentation only as its typed view. The mode stack is empty between balls: ball-will-start resets ball save, tilt and multiball; ball-starting enables hardware. **The skill shot reads `lanes.lit` live rather than caching its own draw** — that is the load-bearing design claim, and it is pinned by a composition test (draw, lane change, enter the *new* lit lane, award pays) plus a control proving the originally-drawn lane does **not** pay after rotation.
- **`hardwareEnabled` boots `false` while physics coils still default enabled** — so flippers, slings and pops are physically live through the machine's very **first** Attract, before any ball exists. This is `DW-177`, **`wontfix-accepted` with `reopen_if=DW-175 lands`**, and the disposition was reached by measurement, not preference: a boot-disable batch was applied and **diverged ball positions in two goldens**, because Attract-phase flipper actuation is the goldens' own premise. Every later Attract inherits a real disable from the prior game's game-over commands. **Do not "fix" this without the grant a trajectory re-record needs.**
- **Single arbiters for the overloaded seams.** The **Lock arbiter** in the ball controller is the only consumer of `lock_lane_entered` and emits exactly one of locked, mode-start with candidates, strike, or spit (device full of another player's balls; the credit still counts). It alone pulses `c_mouth` and serves from the trough after a lock entry; when lock and mode start both apply it credits the lock first; the mode-select window is its timer, the ball stays parked meanwhile, flipper edges move the selection and Start or either flipper confirms; every Mouth eject is preceded by a mouth-open show and follows it by a converted lead time. **Ball save** is one machine device the ball controller owns, armed and disarmed by source through a rules-internal interface, sources stacking with the longest live window winning, Tilt disarming all; only the ball controller pulses the trough-eject and autolaunch coils and mutates `ballsInPlay`. **"A multiball is running"** is a flag set in a mode's starting phase and cleared in its stopped phase, never derived from balls in play.
- **The command union is closed.** Rules to physics: coil pulse/enable/disable, plus the recover command for ball search alone. Rules to presentation: lamp, GI, flasher and show commands, nothing else.
- **Lamp state is a pure projection** recomputed from state every step; the loop emits only the diff, and no mode issues a lamp command. Roles are a closed union (`off · lit · hurryup · quickmb · joust · dragon · special`) and steps are 0 to 3; no RGB, hex or colour name may appear anywhere in the simulation, and one presentation-side table is the sole role-to-colour mapping. **This is Story 2.8's whole subject and none of it is built yet** — see the substrate bullet above.
- **One clock, no wall-clock or unseeded randomness in the simulation.** Timers are authored in milliseconds in the tuning file, converted to ticks once at load (`shotWindowTicks()` is the one sanctioned reader for the `…Ms` window tunables), and pace presentation by emitting step events presentation animates to — presentation never reports completion. All rules randomness draws from the seeded PRNG in game state, so the Skill-shot lane and the Match number replay identically. **Story 2.7 fixed the seed that fed it:** the gameplay path passed no `GameStart` at all, so `sim/loop`'s own `options.gameStart?.seed ?? 0` default supplied a literal zero to every real game on every machine — and seed 0's first three bound-3 draws are `0, 0, 0`, so the *same* Top lane lit on all three balls. `src/host/game-seed.ts` now derives a per-game seed via Web Crypto and `boot.ts` passes it through the existing `GameStart.seed` contract, so `sim/` still draws only from `GameState.rng` and replays stay reproducible because the header records the seed. **The dev replay recorder keeps its fixed seed 0, correctly** — a recorded replay must reproduce. Two residuals: `hostLoop.reset()` does not carry the seed forward, so a tuning-panel hot-apply or dev reset silently restores the zero (`DW-210`, dev-only paths, escalated); and uniform independent draws still repeat a lane across all three balls in ~11% of games, against the AC's "the lit lane differs across balls" (`DW-205`, decision-pending — the reviewer's recommendation is to accept uniform randomness and reword the AC).
- **Modes have unique numeric priorities** — base 100, skill shot 200 — and Epic 2 runs that minimal stack only. **The stack's own contract is unsettled and belongs to 3.1** (`DW-209`, routed): `createModeStack` holds `pendingStartPlayer` in a **closure outside `GameState`**, so two states that hash identically under `gameStateHash()` step differently and a caller handing a mid-game `initialState` to a fresh `createRules()` silently drops a pending arm; nothing cancels it if the ball ends inside the one-tick window; and the fan-out is **mode-major** (each mode gets the whole batch in turn) rather than event-major (each event to every mode, highest-first), which differ observably on a tick carrying both `lane_change_pressed` and the resolving closure. `ModeEvent` also has no exhaustiveness guard.
- **Rules tests run headless in Node with no physics or rendering loaded**, driven by the `SwitchName`-typed switch-script DSL at `test/util/switch-script.ts`. Every device and shot event needs at least one scripted test. **The headless claim is pinned by a transitive-closure import scan over resolved repo-relative paths, not by a comment** — its first two attempts were themselves vacuous. The scan's `ENTRY_FILES` list is now ratcheted: a `test/rules-*.test.ts` file is headless unless named `*-integration.test.ts` or is the gate itself, so the next omission reddens by name. Integration tests that reach `FrameOutput` through the loop's `advance` are a separate obligation.
- **Layering is law and is linted.** Device-name string literals outside the table file and the test tree are errors, so every new switch, coil, lamp and device name enters through the registry — rule (e) covers the `s_/c_/l_/f_/gi_/bd_/shot_/show_` prefixes, which **includes the `l_` names Story 2.8 introduces**. `tools/boundary-lint.mjs` also carries check **(g)** `rules-no-switch-event-outside-devices`, which is **textual, not a dependency-cruiser module rule** — with `parser: 'swc'` and `tsPreCompilationDeps: false` (pinned off by AD-16), a type-only edge is indistinguishable from a value import, so a module rule would fire on two innocent files *and still miss a real leak*. It catches **five** binding forms. Three complementary provenance gates exist and none may be retired in favour of another: a per-file **presence** check, a **structural** provenance test (upstream copyright block intact, provenance sets disjoint, the solver-constant pin, the port-body freeze), and the **import** linter. Story 2.0 is the structural gate's documented owner.
- **Blender owns placement, the registry owns wiring, the export script enforces the contract.** Node prefixes are load-bearing. The export validates the source against a dump of the registry and writes both the render asset and a collision file in table-frame millimetres; the simulation never parses the render asset, and both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property. New insert meshes enter through this pipeline like every other node.
- **Tunables carry a source and a confidence.** Material parameters are named entries with scatter at zero. Scoring values stay adjustable through Epic 2 and freeze only after Epic 3's first full playtest.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera, no camera controls.** Both flippers, the Dragon, both Loops, the Ramp and the DRAGON bank must all be legible from it, and **the backbox strip carrying the Backglass is now in that framing** — 2.6 authored the quad and measured that it projects inside the view, so the camera was not moved.
- **The Backglass is a low-resolution dot-matrix display** — a visible dot grid, no smoothing, **128×32 and 1-bit** — showing every player's score with the current player highlighted, the ball number, and the highest-priority mode's name and whichever fields it publishes (`timerTicks`, `value`, `charge`, `strikesRemaining`), converted to display units in presentation. **Its vertical budget is the live constraint, and the author has decided the fix:** with **four** players the BALL row drops; at **three or more** the whole mode block drops; at **two** the mode *name* survives and only the mode's optional fields drop (`DW-197`, whose own evidence line was off by one player and was corrected in place). Separately, `DmdRow.emphasis` is written and asserted but **no renderer reads it**, and the Attract screen emits bare unlabelled numbers, so the panel never identifies *which* player a score belongs to (`DW-198`). **Both are routed to Story 2.13 as one problem** with the author's decision recorded: give the ball number a shared or shortened line rather than its own, and implement `emphasis` rendering — with a **binding Rule 19 condition on 2.13's QA gate, that the pinning test must assert the RENDERED DOTS DIFFER between an emphasised and an unemphasised row, never merely that the field is set.** Asserting a field nothing reads is precisely the vacuity that produced `DW-198`.
- **The mode line shows a mode or nothing at all.** Once the skill shot resolves, the base mode is the highest-priority active mode for the rest of the ball, which rendered the literal word `BASE` on the panel (`DW-200`). The author decided: **blank the mode line when only the base mode is on the stack** — an internal identifier must not leak to the panel — and 2.7 delivered it, pinned on the rasterised dot buffer rather than on a helper's return value. **Its residual is live:** the fix generalises past the decision, so *any* unlabelled mode now suppresses its published fields too, and from Story 3.1 a higher-priority unlabelled mode would blank a labelled lower-priority one — which contradicts Story 2.6 AC 5, never amended (`DW-206`, escalated: it needs either that AC amendment or a fix narrowed to the base mode). Harmless at this tree, because base is the only unlabelled mode, publishes no fields, and is the lowest priority.
- **Presentation never joins a tick-*t* event to a later snapshot.** An end-of-ball screen names the player from the event payload, never from the snapshot that has already advanced.
- **The colour grammar is fixed and never varies**: white for a lit or qualifying shot, orange for DRAGON letters and the Lock, red for Hurry-up, green for Quick multiball, blue for Joust, purple for extra ball and special. Progression within a colour is a brightness and blink ladder expressed as the step value, with cadence timed by presentation.
- **Flipper buttons move the lit insert** one position per press across the Top lanes and across the inlane/outlane set, wrapping. **Per the author's 2026-09-06 decision, lane change matters before the plunge, not during** — see `DW-204`, still decision-pending against the shipped behaviour.
- **Attract in this epic is minimal**: the Backglass cycles the last game's scores with a start prompt and shows the flipper, plunge and Start keys once from the view configuration. The camera walk-up belongs to Epic 4.
