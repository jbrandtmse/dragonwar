# Epic 1 Context: First Flip — a ball and two flippers that feel right

<!-- Generated from planning artifacts. Regenerate with compile-epic-context if planning docs change. -->

## Goal

Epic 1 turns the repository into a playable vertical slice: the author opens a build in a browser and plunges, flips, taps, nudges and drains a ball on a bare playfield at real machine dimensions. Everything the rest of the project stands on is created here — the seam contracts, the `TABLE` registry, the ported time-of-impact physics core, the fixed-step loop, the Blender-to-glb export pipeline, a scene at the fixed authored view, keyboard input, a dev tuning panel, replay goldens, and CI with boundary lint. The pipeline and the feel are the deliverable, not the art: every mesh is a placeholder primitive. Two measurement spikes opened the epic and can still change its shape — a missed frame budget drops the tick rate and forces a solver re-tune. The feel ritual against the reference machine (a feel reference only; no art, audio or rules are taken from it) starts here and never stops.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **The CSP is pinned to exactly `default-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' blob:; img-src 'self' blob:`** — carried as an `index.html` meta tag, grepped in CI, with the build failing if the tag is absent. It is the enforcement of "local storage only, no network calls after load", and it constrains what the renderer may fetch (see WebGPU below). `blob:` is admitted because the engine serves images embedded in a `.glb` through `blob:` URLs, so a textured asset cannot boot without it; the no-network guarantee is unchanged because `blob:` is a same-document scheme that cannot reach the network, and the guarantee still rests entirely on `connect-src` admitting no remote origin. `data:` was deliberately **not** admitted and was proven unnecessary in a real browser — keep the grant minimal, and prove any future widening in a browser before pinning it. The exact string is duplicated across several enforcement points (the entry HTML, the dist checker, the spike harness page, and exact-string test pins); changing it means changing all of them together or the build fails.
- **A headless engine never decodes images, so the automated suite cannot see renderer-level breakage.** A per-story smoke in a real browser is what catches it — the CSP defect above passed 442 green tests. Do not read a green test run as proof the build renders.
- **A green suite is not proof an invariant is protected.** Epic 1 has one recorded case of the epic's central ordering invariant being genuinely broken by a deliberate mutation while the whole suite stayed green (590 passed, typecheck clean). Where a criterion names a mutation, the red must actually be observed and the observation recorded in the story spec; a mutation asserted but not run is not evidence.
- **Measure the production artifact, never the dev server.** Every performance and payload number in this epic comes from a real production build (`vite build` + `vite preview`, or the deployed static build). Treat this as standing practice, not a measured effect: the dev-vs-production delta it was originally justified by did not reproduce in a same-session A/B on identical code. The reason is that production is what ships.
- **The frame-budget outcome is `TICK_HZ = 1000`, provisional.** The bar — p95 sim cost of 4 ms or less per 60 Hz frame — gates on Chrome/Windows, Chrome/macOS and Safari/macOS; the two macOS legs are author-owned and still unmeasured, so a tick-rate change remains possible and later stories must not treat 1000 Hz as final. Edge/Windows is measured and recorded but is best-effort *for this gate only*; its number never decides the verdict. That is a frame-budget carve-out, not a support-tier demotion — Edge keeps its place in the boot message, the release matrix and the platform requirement. Safari was **not** demoted: it runs a different JavaScript engine, is still unmeasured, and is the real remaining performance risk.
- **Real dimensions or a load failure.** Playfield 514.4 x 1066.8 mm, ball 26.99 mm, flipper bat 3.125 in rubbered, default pitch 6.5 deg. The collision loader asserts playfield bounds and both flipper lengths against the reference block and throws a descriptive error on mismatch, so a wrong-size table is caught at load rather than becoming a feel problem discovered months later.
- **Feel is the acceptance criterion.** Cradle, live catch, light tap, dead bounce, post pass and backhand must all be possible on the finished machine. Rebounds stay lively at low speed and never pingy or airball-prone at high speed — elasticity falloff is the primary control. Occasional hops come from one explicit tunable: at zero there are none, at the default they occur on hard hits, and the mechanism adds no randomness.
- **This epic's placeholder table cannot hold a cradle, and that is geometry, not the flipper.** The committed collision document has the playfield, the glass, the outer and lane walls, a lane deflector and the two bats — and **nothing beside either flipper**. A real cradle is a pocket formed by the raised bat together with the inlane guide or post next to it, and that geometry does not exist until Epic 2. A ball resting on a raised bat therefore rolls along it under the 6.5 deg pitch and departs after roughly **1.2–1.9 simulated seconds**. This was proved not to be a defect in the ported flipper: the bat is provably static while held, the port matches its pinned upstream revision byte-for-byte, total mechanical energy is dissipated rather than injected, and a control ball that never touches a flipper runs away *faster*. So what this epic asserts is the coil half in full — under a 5 s hold the bat reaches its end-of-stroke angle and holds it, unmoving and without oscillating at the stop, for the whole 5 s — with the ball's rest-on-bat claim **bounded to the first 1 second**. Do not widen that bound here, do not alter the port to accommodate it, and do not build any test or golden in this epic that assumes a multi-second cradle.
- **The golden set is five replays, and a golden may not claim behaviour the table cannot produce.** Recorded and replayed to their hashes in Node in CI: roll-and-drain, **hold-and-release**, full plunge, nudge coupling, and a two-ball collision (momentum transferred, no overlap, no sticking, and separation never dropping below one diameter). The second was renamed from "cradle-and-release" for the reason in the bullet above: with no pocket geometry a golden named for a cradle would freeze roll-off *as* the cradle reference, and when Epic 2 authors the pocket the break would read as "re-record me" rather than "ask why". Its hold is therefore kept well inside the ~1 s window where behaviour is stable, so the golden survives Epic 2 intact. Browser parity is asserted on the game-state portion of the hash only; ball positions are asserted in Node.
- **Determinism is enforced, not assumed.** Identical inputs produce identical outcomes at 30, 60 and 120 Hz display rates. Owed simulated time beyond 200 ms is discarded — emitting a discard event as that frame's first event — rather than freezing or fast-forwarding. Scatter is 0 on every material and the physics PRNG is never drawn, both asserted by test. Nothing tunnels and nothing stalls at any display rate; every solver step terminates.
- **Nudge is cabinet motion, never a force on the ball.** An impulse goes to the damped-harmonic cabinet oscillator on a rising nudge edge; the ball keeps its inertia while the cabinet moves under it. This is the one place the port is deliberately *not* verbatim — the upstream nudge-as-force-on-the-ball is a known long-standing defect — so the re-derived coupling is pinned by a golden replay. The tilt bob is a real pendulum whose displacement crossing a threshold closes and re-opens its switch as single edges; it keeps swinging and decays physically, and no command ever resets it. The slam detector is a separate tick-windowed nudge count living beside the oscillator with its own threshold, never the bob's threshold or state. Both sensors are inside the replay — a host-side detector could not be reproduced, which is why they live in physics.
- **Flipper response lands within one 60 Hz display frame**, the loop entirely local to the physics step.
- **Provenance is a hard gate.** The attribution entry lands *before* the file, with licences verified at source rather than from package metadata. Ported files keep their original copyright header plus the port marker; every new file carries the project licence header; CI checks headers per file.
- **CI runs on every push and pull request** — typecheck, boundary lint, Vitest including replay goldens recorded in Node, build, the CSP grep and the size budget. The size budget is set from the measured production baseline plus authored headroom and fails with the measured and budgeted numbers in the message.
- **Deploying is a `main`-only rule with one temporary, self-cancelling exception.** The deploy workflow is triggered on the epic branch plus `workflow_dispatch` purely so the spike could measure a live deployment before Epic 1 merges. Removing that epic-branch trigger and narrowing the workflow back to `main` plus `workflow_dispatch` is itself an acceptance criterion that must be satisfied before Epic 1's merge gate. The spike record also states that the measured artifact was built from an unmerged branch and stays provisional until it reruns from `main`.
- **The epic closes with dated documents:** both spike results (numbers, machines, browsers, date, and which legs gate) and the feel-test log with its first entry per item — cradling, flipper snap, rejection/rebound — each closed as a tuning change or an accepted difference, run on both the forced-WebGL2 and the default renderer path.

## Technical Decisions

- **Layering is law and is linted, not merely documented.** `sim/**` is DOM-free, engine-free, wall-clock-free and unseeded-random-free. `presentation/**` imports only `sim/contracts` and `sim/table`. `host/**` never imports `sim/physics` or `sim/rules`. The engine's own physics plugin is banned everywhere. A device-name string literal outside the table file and `test/**` is a lint error. Dependency-cruiser enforces all of it in CI.
- **One clock behind one constant.** `TICK_HZ` lives in a single contracts file and `tick` is the only notion of time inside `sim/`. Durations are authored in ms in the tuning file and converted to ticks once at load; any other literal millisecond or tick arithmetic inside `sim/` fails a CI check. Changing the tick rate or any solver constant is a physics-version bump that re-records every golden.
- **Renderer selection happens once at boot, under the pinned CSP.** The engine is created once via the async engine factory, right-handed, after the press-to-begin gesture. WebGL2 is the floor and always renders. WebGPU is taken only where it can actually initialise under the pinned CSP — its transpiler must be served from our own origin, never a CDN — and otherwise falls back to WebGL2 **silently**. `?renderer=webgl2` forces WebGL2 in every case, and no feature may require WebGPU. A known open defect has the WebGPU path failing on the first alpha-blended material and falling back silently; it is routed to the later browser-matrix story, so WebGL2 is in practice the rendering path for this epic — the dual-path feel ritual still runs both.
- **Loop contract.** `advance(elapsedMs, transitions)` owes `elapsed x TICK_HZ` steps and carries the fractional remainder. Per step: physics consumes the input frame in force at that tick plus the commands issued at the previous tick, then the rules step runs — every step, even with no events. The frame output carries every event and command from all N steps in tick order, with empty arrays and an unchanged snapshot when N is 0. Presentation renders the latest snapshot without interpolation.
- **Closed seam contracts,** discriminated on `type`, every event and command carrying `tick`. Contracts are generic over the name unions; the name unions derive from the table type in a separate file; contracts never import the table.
- **One table registry.** A single frozen `TABLE` is the sole source of switches (each with a settle-ticks class), coils, ball devices (capacity, slot switches in fill order, eject coil, search order), reference dimensions and glb node names. It is imported directly — no table interface, loader API or plugin API. Tunables carry a unit-suffixed name, a source and a confidence; do-not-invent figures ship marked unverified.
- **Physics is ported, not derived — and the port outranks a criterion that contradicts it.** Solver constants are verbatim and never tunable. The flipper is the ported mover (strength, ramp-up, end-of-stroke torque and angle, return strength, inertia one-third m r squared); documented pulse/hold figures from elsewhere are calibration comments, never parameters. The cabinet oscillator is ported, but its ball coupling is re-derived as table-frame motion and pinned by a golden replay. Where an acceptance criterion turns out to be unreachable *because* of the verbatim port, the criterion is what is wrong: it gets amended with the measurement that shows why, and the port is left alone.
- **Hardware rules live inside the physics step,** each behind its coil and gated only by coil enable/disable — flippers and the manual plunger in this epic, slingshots and pops later. The precise meaning of "same tick" is: a button or switch closing at tick *t* **energises its coil inside tick *t*'s own physics step, with no rules round trip** — the rules step's command list stays empty. The *visible* mechanical response can lag by one tick and legitimately does for the flipper: the ported mover ramps torque from an at-rest return torque, so the net torque sign flips on the second step for any positive ramp-up, and the angle first moves at *t+1*. Angle is a lagging indicator of torque; assert the energisation and the ordering, not an instantaneous angle change. **This ordering is the epic's central invariant and must be pinned by a test that fails when the hardware rules are moved to run after the physics step** — the mutation is part of the criterion, not of anyone's diligence.
- **Ball devices.** Four balls, asserted at boot. The trough parks entering balls unconditionally into the lowest empty slot and ejects the highest filled slot, one per pulse, at the authored pose; the shooter lane is non-parking, the served ball resting simulated on the plunger tip. Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else. The *opening* of the shooter-lane switch is the one thing that means "plunged" and drives the launch event and the in-play count.
- **Switch discipline.** Physics emits playfield and cabinet-mechanism switches — the tilt bob and the slam sensor included — as edges only, with per-switch hysteresis and settle classes (rollover 0, standup 8, drop target 20, bumper skirt 2, tilt bob 0). The loop emits the button switches from input-frame transitions; rules never debounce, and the only rules-side windows are semantic. Switch zones test the ball's per-tick swept segment, never its end position.
- **Units and frames.** The table frame (playfield-local mm, right-handed, origin bottom-left nearest the player, X right, Y up the playfield, Z toward the glass) is canonical; physics keeps its ported internal units. Exactly one file exports the three conversions and no other file converts units or axes. Geometry is authored unpitched; pitch is applied by physics as the gravity vector and by presentation as a rotation of the playfield root about a pitch pivot, with the cabinet staying level.
- **Blender owns placement; the export script is the contract's enforcer.** One source file owns every position, mesh and switch zone. The export tool validates node names, uniqueness, one-material-per-mesh and every custom property against a dump of the table, then writes the glb for presentation and a collision file (mm, table frame) for physics, exiting non-zero and naming the offending node on any violation. `sim/` never parses glb; both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property. Node prefixes cover collision scaffolding that reduces to the ported primitive set, switch zones, visuals, inserts with lens and cup geometry, and mechanisms named as their device; exactly two top-level nodes plus the pitch pivot; walls and floor have real thickness; playfield collision is one compound body; static meshes carry a second UV set and a light-group property.
- **Replays are first-class — and in this epic each golden also carries a declared coil prologue.** A replay is a header (the whole game-start bundle, physics seed, tick rate, table hash, asset hash, physics version) plus the ordered input transitions, and it must reproduce the state hash. But nothing in that body can put a ball in play here, and the reason is **type-level, not a gap in today's behaviour**: the rules step result types its command list as an empty-never array, so the rules layer *cannot* issue a coil command; the input action union is closed at eight members and none of them reaches a coil; and the loop's dev-only `pulseCoil()` escape hatch is the only path by which a ball reaches the playfield, already documented in-file as Story 2.5's to replace. So each golden declares its prologue — the coil-pulse sequence that serves the ball — **as data in the golden file** beside the header and body, and re-asserts it on replay. Do not resolve this by pre-building Story 2.5's ball lifecycle inside a determinism story: **Story 2.5** owns removing the prologue once Start serves through the rules layer, and re-recording the goldens then. The state hash itself is FNV-1a over canonical JSON of the game state plus ball positions quantised to 0.01 mm; a recording whose tunables were hot-applied mid-record is marked invalid and cannot be saved as a golden.
- **Testing shape.** Rules are tested headless through a switch-script DSL typed by the switch names; physics is tested by replaying goldens and asserting the hash; the only presentation test is a headless-engine load smoke test — which, being headless, proves the scene graph loads and nothing about what the browser actually draws.
- **Error policy.** Load-time paths throw and boot reports them in the host error panel; step paths never throw — invariant violations are dev-mode assertions and device failure is an event. Only `host/` logs; no telemetry; nothing reads env at runtime.
- **Naming.** Files kebab-case, types PascalCase, events snake_case on a four-phase lifecycle, entities identified by table name and balls by id rather than index.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera,** no camera controls; this epic renders from it directly — the walk-up and attract show are later work.
- **Default keys:** left and right Shift for the flippers, Enter to plunge with hold-for-strength, Space and the arrow keys for the three nudge actions and nothing else, 1 for Start. The key-to-action map lives only in host input and key codes never enter `sim/`; each transition is tick-stamped from the DOM event timestamp against the accumulator origin.
- **Boot is a press-to-begin gesture** before the engine is created and before any asset loads. The renderer choice behind it is invisible to the player — a WebGPU attempt that cannot initialise or cannot render degrades to WebGL2 with nothing surfaced — and the forced-WebGL2 query parameter exists for the feel ritual's second pass.
- **Dev tuning panel** lists every table tunable with its value, source and confidence, hot-applies an edit to the running sim, and exports the current set back to the tuning file in that file's own format. A hot-apply during a recording invalidates the recording so it cannot be saved as a golden.

## Cross-Story Dependencies

- **The two spikes gated the epic and are closed.** The tick rate is set to 1000 Hz and marked **provisional** — the two macOS gating legs are author-owned and still unmeasured, so later stories should treat a tick-rate change as still possible. The CI workflow, the deploy, the pinned CSP and the size budget all exist and are extended from here rather than created.
- **Story 1.2 leaves one debt that Epic 1 must clear before merging:** the epic-branch deploy trigger it added has to be narrowed back to `main` plus `workflow_dispatch`. Whichever story or gate closes the epic must confirm it, and no later story may rely on the epic branch deploying.
- **The scaffold (contracts, table registry, tuning, boundary lint) exists** and precedes every later story; it owns the tick-rate constant's home and the single load-time ms-to-ticks conversion, and it extended the workflow into the full check set. Build on those contracts rather than widening them.
- **The asset pipeline exists:** the frames module, the collision file and the glb are in place, and the physics loader, the scene and the serve-roll-drain story consume them. The placeholder renders at real dimensions, pitched, from the fixed camera, on WebGL2.
- **Every behaviour the goldens capture is already built.** The fixed-step conductor, the trough serve and the drain, the input mapping and tick-stamped transitions, the hardware-rule seam inside the physics step, the ported flipper mover, the hold-to-charge manual plunge, and the cabinet oscillator, tilt bob and slam counter beside the other hardware rules — all exist. Story 1.8 records against them rather than adding behaviour, and any change to the tick rate or to a solver constant re-records every golden.
- **A test-vacuity and invariant sweep runs before any golden is recorded** — a user-approved, binding ordering. A golden freezes current behaviour as the reference, so recording one while a vacuous assertion is masking a defect promotes that defect to specification, and the next person to see the golden break re-records it instead of questioning it. The sweep was originally slated for the burn-down and was moved ahead of the goldens for that reason; artifacts describing it as living in "Story 1.9" predate the 1.9 / 1.10 numbering above.
- **The goldens must fit inside what this epic's geometry supports.** A ball resting on a raised bat departs on its own after roughly 1.2–1.9 simulated seconds, so both the nudge-coupling golden and the **hold-and-release** golden must act well inside that window and must not be written to depend on a stable multi-second cradle. That bound is why the golden is named for the hold and not for a cradle.
- **One piece of Epic 1's cradle criterion is deliberately owed to Epic 2,** tracked in the ledger as `DW-72`: the full 5 s ball-stays-cradled assertion is re-asserted in Story 2.1 against the real playfield once the inlane guides and posts that form the pocket exist, and the 1 s ball bound in the flipper collision test is widened back to 5 s there. Epic 1 must leave that test's shape and its ball placement intact — re-placing the ball would invalidate both the amended tolerances and the measured evidence the amendment rests on.
- **The feel-ritual story depends on replay record/play** and on the tuning file's provenance fields, so it follows the goldens.
- **Downstream:** **Story 2.5** owns the real ball lifecycle — it replaces the dev-only coil escape hatch, removes each golden's declared prologue, and re-records the goldens; nothing here should anticipate that work. **Story 2.1** draws the real playfield geometry and replaces the placeholder behind the same node contract and the same table registry, so nothing here may hard-code placeholder-specific geometry. Epic 6 re-asserts the same pinned CSP and the `main`-only deploy rule, so leaving either loosened here breaks a later gate; the open WebGPU defect is routed to its browser-matrix story. This epic must not pre-build modes, the tilt warning and Tilt rules, the lighting colour grammar, audio, persistence or the settings panel — the bob and the slam sensor here are sensors closing switches inside physics, and the rules that consume them are Epic 2's; the rules layer in this epic is only the minimal step that runs after every physics step.
