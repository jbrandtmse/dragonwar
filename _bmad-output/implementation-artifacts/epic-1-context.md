# Epic 1 Context: First Flip — a ball and two flippers that feel right

## Goal

Epic 1 turns the repository into a playable vertical slice: the author opens a build in a browser and plunges, flips, taps, nudges and drains a ball on a bare playfield at real machine dimensions. Everything the rest of the project stands on is created here — the seam contracts, the `TABLE` registry, the ported time-of-impact physics core, the fixed-step loop, the Blender-to-glb export pipeline, a scene at the fixed authored view, keyboard input, a dev tuning panel, replay goldens, and CI with boundary lint. The pipeline and the feel are the deliverable, not the art: every mesh is a placeholder primitive. The build work is finished — Stories 1.1 through 1.9 are all done — and the epic's only remaining story is **1.10**, a bounded burn-down that pays down twelve of the items Epic 1 chose to defer so they do not become Epic 2's inheritance.

## Requirements & Constraints

- **A green suite is not proof an invariant is protected.** Epic 1 has a recorded case of its central ordering invariant being genuinely broken by a deliberate mutation while the whole suite stayed green (590 passed, typecheck clean). Where a criterion names a mutation, the red must actually be observed, the mutation reverted, the tree confirmed byte-identical, and the observation recorded in the story spec. A mutation asserted but not run is not evidence.
- **A headless engine never decodes images, so the automated suite cannot see renderer-level breakage.** A smoke in a real browser is what catches it — the CSP defect below passed 442 green tests. Never read a green run as proof the build renders.
- **Measure the production artifact, never the dev server.** Every performance and payload number comes from a real production build (`vite build` + `vite preview`, or the deployed static build), because production is what ships.
- **The CSP is pinned to exactly `default-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' blob:; img-src 'self' blob:`** — an `index.html` meta tag, grepped in CI, build failing if absent. It enforces "local storage only, no network calls after load". `blob:` is admitted because the engine serves `.glb`-embedded images through `blob:` URLs; it is a same-document scheme that cannot reach the network, so the guarantee still rests entirely on `connect-src`. `data:` was deliberately not admitted and proven unnecessary in a real browser. The exact string is duplicated across the entry HTML, the dist checker, the spike harness page and exact-string test pins — change them together or the build fails.
- **`TICK_HZ = 1000` is provisional.** The bar (p95 sim cost of 4 ms or less per 60 Hz frame) gates on Chrome/Windows, Chrome/macOS and Safari/macOS; the two macOS legs are author-owned and still unmeasured, so a tick-rate change remains possible. Edge/Windows is measured but best-effort *for this gate only* — a frame-budget carve-out, not a support-tier demotion; Edge keeps its place in the boot message, release matrix and platform requirement. Safari was not demoted and is the real remaining performance risk.
- **Real dimensions or a load failure.** Playfield 514.4 x 1066.8 mm, ball 26.99 mm, flipper bat 3.125 in rubbered, default pitch 6.5 deg. The collision loader asserts playfield bounds and both flipper lengths against the reference block and throws on mismatch.
- **Feel is the acceptance criterion.** Cradle, live catch, light tap, dead bounce, post pass and backhand must all be possible on the finished machine. Rebounds stay lively at low speed, never pingy or airball-prone at high speed — elasticity falloff is the primary control. Hops come from one explicit tunable that adds no randomness.
- **This epic's placeholder table cannot hold a cradle, and that is geometry, not the flipper.** There is nothing beside either flipper until Epic 2, so a ball on a raised bat rolls along it under pitch and departs after roughly **1.2–1.9 simulated seconds**. Proved not to be a port defect: the bat is static while held, the port matches its pinned upstream revision byte-for-byte, energy is dissipated not injected, and a control ball that never touches a flipper runs away faster. The coil half is asserted in full (5 s hold, end-of-stroke angle held without oscillating); the ball's rest-on-bat claim is bounded to the first 1 second. Do not widen that bound, do not alter the port to accommodate it, and build nothing here that assumes a multi-second cradle.
- **Determinism is enforced, not assumed.** Identical inputs produce identical outcomes at 30, 60 and 120 Hz display rates. Owed simulated time beyond 200 ms is discarded, emitting a discard event as that frame's first event, rather than freezing or fast-forwarding. Scatter is 0 on every material and the physics PRNG is never drawn, both asserted by test. Nothing tunnels, nothing stalls, every solver step terminates.
- **Nudge is cabinet motion, never a force on the ball.** An impulse goes to the damped-harmonic cabinet oscillator on a rising nudge edge; the ball keeps its inertia while the cabinet moves under it. This is the one place the port is deliberately *not* verbatim (upstream's nudge-as-force is a known defect), so the re-derived coupling is pinned by a golden. The tilt bob is a real pendulum whose displacement crossing a threshold closes and re-opens its switch as single edges; it decays physically and no command resets it. The slam detector is a separate tick-windowed nudge count with its own threshold. Both live in physics so they are inside the replay.
- **Flipper response lands within one 60 Hz display frame**, the loop entirely local to the physics step.
- **Provenance is a hard gate.** The attribution entry lands *before* the file, with licences verified at source rather than from package metadata. Ported files keep their original copyright header plus the port marker; every new file carries the project licence header; CI checks headers per file.
- **CI runs on every push and pull request** — typecheck, boundary lint, Vitest including replay goldens recorded in Node, build, the CSP grep and the size budget (measured baseline plus authored headroom, failing with both numbers in the message).
- **Deploying is a `main`-only rule with one temporary, self-cancelling exception.** The epic-branch deploy trigger exists only so the spike could measure a live deployment; narrowing the workflow back to `main` plus `workflow_dispatch` is itself an acceptance criterion to satisfy before Epic 1 merges. The spike's measured artifact stays provisional until it reruns from `main`.
- **The epic closes with dated documents:** both spike results (numbers, machines, browsers, date, which legs gate) and the feel-test log with its first entry per item — cradling, flipper snap, rejection/rebound — each closed as a tuning change or an accepted difference, run on both the forced-WebGL2 and default renderer paths.

## Technical Decisions

- **Layering is law and is linted, not merely documented.** `sim/**` is DOM-free, engine-free, wall-clock-free and unseeded-random-free. `presentation/**` imports only `sim/contracts` and `sim/table`. `host/**` never imports `sim/physics` or `sim/rules`. The engine's own physics plugin is banned everywhere. A device-name string literal outside the table file and `test/**` is a lint error. Dependency-cruiser enforces all of it in CI, and the lint's fixtures are expected to prove each rule's *exemptions* hold, not merely that the rule fires.
- **One clock behind one constant.** `TICK_HZ` lives in a single contracts file and `tick` is the only notion of time inside `sim/`. Durations are authored in ms in the tuning file and converted to ticks once at load; any other literal millisecond or tick arithmetic inside `sim/` fails a CI check. Changing the tick rate or any solver constant is a physics-version bump that re-records every golden.
- **Loop contract.** `advance(elapsedMs, transitions)` owes `elapsed x TICK_HZ` steps and carries the fractional remainder. Per step: physics consumes the input frame in force at that tick plus the commands issued at the previous tick, then the rules step runs — every step, even with no events. The frame output carries every event and command from all N steps in tick order, with empty arrays and an unchanged snapshot when N is 0. Presentation renders the latest snapshot without interpolation.
- **Hardware rules live inside the physics step,** each behind its coil and gated only by coil enable/disable — flippers and the manual plunger in this epic. "Same tick" means: a button or switch closing at tick *t* energises its coil inside tick *t*'s own physics step, with no rules round trip; the rules step's command list stays empty. The *visible* response may lag one tick and legitimately does for the flipper — the ported mover ramps torque from an at-rest return torque, so angle first moves at *t+1*. Assert energisation and ordering, not an instantaneous angle. **This ordering is the epic's central invariant and must stay pinned by a test that fails when the hardware rules are moved to run after the physics step.**
- **Physics is ported, not derived — and the port outranks a criterion that contradicts it.** Solver constants are verbatim and never tunable. The flipper is the ported mover (strength, ramp-up, end-of-stroke torque and angle, return strength, inertia one-third m r squared); documented pulse/hold figures from elsewhere are calibration comments, never parameters. Where a criterion is unreachable *because* of the verbatim port, the criterion is what is wrong: amend it with the measurement that shows why and leave the port alone.
- **Closed seam contracts,** discriminated on `type`, every event and command carrying `tick`. Contracts are generic over the name unions; the name unions derive from the table type in a separate file; contracts never import the table, and `sim/table` never imports `sim/physics`.
- **One table registry.** A single frozen `TABLE` is the sole source of switches (each with a settle-ticks class), coils, ball devices (capacity, slot switches in fill order, eject coil, search order), reference dimensions and glb node names. Imported directly — no table interface, loader API or plugin API. Tunables carry a unit-suffixed name, a source and a confidence; do-not-invent figures ship marked unverified.
- **Ball devices.** Four balls, asserted at boot. The trough parks entering balls unconditionally into the lowest empty slot and ejects the highest filled slot, one per pulse, at the authored pose; the shooter lane is non-parking, the served ball resting simulated on the plunger tip. Device counts are the number of closed slot switches and nothing else. The *opening* of the shooter-lane switch is the one thing that means "plunged".
- **Switch discipline.** Physics emits playfield and cabinet-mechanism switches — tilt bob and slam sensor included — as edges only, with per-switch hysteresis and settle classes (rollover 0, standup 8, drop target 20, bumper skirt 2, tilt bob 0). The loop emits button switches from input-frame transitions; rules never debounce, and the only rules-side windows are semantic. Switch zones test the ball's per-tick swept segment, never its end position.
- **Units and frames.** The table frame (playfield-local mm, right-handed, origin bottom-left nearest the player, X right, Y up the playfield, Z toward the glass) is canonical; physics keeps its ported internal units. Exactly one file exports the three conversions. Geometry is authored unpitched; pitch is applied by physics as the gravity vector and by presentation as a rotation of the playfield root about a pitch pivot, cabinet staying level.
- **Blender owns placement; the export script is the contract's enforcer.** One source file owns every position, mesh and switch zone. The export tool validates node names, uniqueness, one-material-per-mesh and every custom property against a dump of the table, then writes the glb for presentation and a collision file (mm, table frame) for physics, exiting non-zero and naming the offending node on violation. `sim/` never parses glb; both loaders fail fast on a missing node or unknown property.
- **Renderer selection happens once at boot, under the pinned CSP.** The engine is created once via the async factory, right-handed, after the press-to-begin gesture. WebGL2 is the floor and always renders. WebGPU is taken only where it can initialise under the pinned CSP — its transpiler served from our own origin, never a CDN — and otherwise falls back silently. `?renderer=webgl2` forces WebGL2, and no feature may require WebGPU. A known open defect fails the WebGPU path on the first alpha-blended material; it is routed to the later browser-matrix story, so WebGL2 is in practice this epic's rendering path.
- **Replays are first-class, and each golden also carries a declared coil prologue.** A replay is a header (game-start bundle, physics seed, tick rate, table hash, asset hash, physics version) plus ordered input transitions, and must reproduce the state hash. Nothing in that body can put a ball in play here, for a **type-level** reason: the rules step types its command list as an empty-never array, the input action union is closed at eight members and none reaches a coil, and the loop's dev-only `pulseCoil()` escape hatch is the only path to the playfield. So each golden declares its coil-pulse prologue **as data in the golden file** and re-asserts it on replay. The state hash is FNV-1a over canonical JSON of game state plus ball positions quantised to 0.01 mm; a recording whose tunables were hot-applied mid-record is invalid and cannot be saved as a golden. **The golden set is five replays** — roll-and-drain, hold-and-release, full plunge, nudge coupling, two-ball collision — replayed to their hashes in Node in CI, with browser parity asserted on the game-state portion of the hash only. "hold-and-release" is deliberately not named for a cradle, and its hold stays well inside the ~1 s stable window so it survives Epic 2 intact.
- **Testing shape.** Rules are tested headless through a switch-script DSL typed by the switch names; physics by replaying goldens and asserting the hash; the only presentation test is a headless-engine load smoke, which proves the scene graph loads and nothing about what a browser draws. Tests must not assert wall-clock ratios between timing runs.
- **Error policy.** Load-time paths throw and boot reports them in the host error panel; step paths never throw — invariant violations are dev-mode assertions and device failure is an event. Only `host/` logs; no telemetry; nothing reads env at runtime.
- **Naming and encoding.** Files kebab-case, types PascalCase, events snake_case on a four-phase lifecycle, entities identified by table name and balls by id rather than index. Non-ASCII in authored source is written as escape sequences, not literal bytes.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- **One fixed authored camera,** no camera controls; the walk-up and attract show are later work.
- **Default keys:** left and right Shift for the flippers, Enter to plunge with hold-for-strength, Space and the arrow keys for the three nudge actions and nothing else, 1 for Start. The key-to-action map lives only in host input, key codes never enter `sim/`, and each transition is tick-stamped from the DOM event timestamp against the accumulator origin.
- **Boot is a press-to-begin gesture** before the engine is created and before any asset loads. The renderer choice behind it is invisible to the player; the forced-WebGL2 query parameter exists for the feel ritual's second pass.
- **Dev tuning panel** lists every table tunable with value, source and confidence, hot-applies an edit to the running sim, and exports the current set back to the tuning file in that file's own format. A hot-apply during a recording invalidates the recording.

## Cross-Story Dependencies

- **Everything Epic 1 builds now exists.** Contracts, `TABLE`, tuning and boundary lint; the asset pipeline (frames module, collision file, glb) rendering at real dimensions, pitched, from the fixed camera on WebGL2; the fixed-step conductor, trough serve and drain; tick-stamped input; the hardware-rule seam inside the physics step with the ported flipper mover, hold-to-charge plunge, cabinet oscillator, tilt bob and slam counter; the five goldens and CI parity; the tuning panel and `docs/feel-test.md`. Story 1.10 changes these rather than adding behaviour — and any change to the tick rate or a solver constant re-records every golden.
- **Story 1.10 draws its scope from the deferred-work ledger, and its charter is closed.** Epic 1 finished with thirty-five `burndown`-owned entries against a cap of 8, so the drain rule required a bounded burn-down. Twelve were chartered by severity, then occurrence count, then lowest fix-risk; ten were re-owned to named stories in Epics 2, 4, 5 and 6; thirteen were set aside for the author's decision sheet at the merge gate. **A burn-down story makes no product calls** — it cannot ratify `TICK_HZ`, run a playtest or the feel ritual, or supply macOS measurement legs. Do not pull a decision-pending entry into it.
- **Two debts Epic 1 must clear before merging:** the epic-branch deploy trigger has to be narrowed back to `main` plus `workflow_dispatch`, and no later story may rely on the epic branch deploying.
- **One piece of Epic 1's cradle criterion is deliberately owed to Epic 2** (`DW-72`): the full 5 s ball-stays-cradled assertion is re-asserted in Story 2.1 against the real playfield once the inlane guides and posts that form the pocket exist, and the 1 s ball bound in the flipper collision test is widened back to 5 s there. Leave that test's shape and its ball placement intact — re-placing the ball would invalidate both the amended tolerances and the measured evidence they rest on.
- **Downstream:** **Story 2.5** owns the real ball lifecycle — it replaces the dev-only coil escape hatch, removes each golden's declared prologue, and re-records the goldens. **Story 2.1** draws the real playfield geometry behind the same node contract and table registry, so nothing here may hard-code placeholder-specific geometry. Epic 6 re-asserts the same pinned CSP and the `main`-only deploy rule; the open WebGPU defect is routed to its browser-matrix story. This epic must not pre-build modes, tilt-warning rules, the lighting colour grammar, audio, persistence or the settings panel — the bob and slam sensor here are sensors closing switches inside physics, and the rules that consume them are Epic 2's.
