- (assumption) TENSION TO FLAG IN BRIEF: user wants the ball to 'sometimes hop' (airballs). The research found that VPX's elasticity-falloff parameter exists specifically to SUPPRESS pingy rebounds and airballs, and that community tuning drives scatter to zero. Hops are therefore a deliberate tuning target to dial IN, not a free by-product - and too many hops is the classic 'too fast / pingy' complaint. Worth one explicit tuning knob rather than an accident.
- (decision) FOUR ASSUMPTIONS RESOLVED BY USER: (1) ART = STYLIZED / LOW-POLY, chosen over the parts-library route and over full original modelling; (2) PLAYERS = 1-4 hot seat, matching a real machine, which fixes per-player state in the rules layer from day one; (3) DISTRIBUTION = BROWSER-FIRST, overriding the research's native-first recommendation because that recommendation was reasoning about a COMMERCIAL product and this is open source, where click-and-play is the whole share story; (4) NUDGE = full nudge plus tilt warnings plus slam tilt, in scope for v1.

Proposed reconciliation to be offered - STYLIZED REALISM: correct dimensions, correct materials, correct lighting behaviour and correct ball behaviour, but simplified geometry and hand-painted textures rather than photoreal scans. Justified by the research: geometry is NOT the bottleneck (single LOD, small fixed near-field scene, 500-2000 tris for standard playfield objects), LIGHTING AND TEXTURE MEMORY are. A low-poly playfield with correct insert glow, correct GI and correct ball physics will read as more authentically pinball than a high-poly one with wrong lighting. Drafted into the brief under this reading, tagged as an assumption for the user to correct.

- (decision) LICENCE RESOLVED TO GPL-3.0, and the verification was load-bearing. Verified this session: (a) vpx-js source file headers (lib/physics/hit-object.ts) read 'either version 2 of the License, or (at your option) any later version' = GPL-2.0-OR-LATER, even though package.json declares only 'GPL-2.0' and the LICENSE file carries the GPLv2 text; (b) Babylon.js is Apache-2.0 per the GitHub API (spdx_id Apache-2.0). Apache-2.0 is INCOMPATIBLE with GPL-2.0-only but COMPATIBLE with GPL-3.0. The or-later clause therefore permits taking the ported physics core to GPL-3.0, where Babylon fits legally. HAD vpx-js BEEN GPL-2.0-ONLY, the chosen renderer and the chosen physics core could not have shipped together. Final stack licensing: DragonWar GPL-3.0, Babylon.js Apache-2.0, MPF ontology MIT - all compatible. User's requirement (free to distribute) is satisfied by GPL-3.0 by design.

- (decision) JOUST MECHANIC RESOLVED: LOOPING SHOTS. Thematically exact - a joust is repeated passes, riders charge, pass, wheel around and charge again, which is mechanically what an orbit/loop shot is. Also PROVEN IN REAL CODE: the research found Stern's Godzilla builds loop combos up to 10x for consecutive shots, so 'each successful loop raises the stakes, a miss breaks the charge' is an established rule pattern rather than an invention. Suggested to the author but not yet decided: a SPINNER on the loop, which is classic orbit furniture and whose rip is the sound of a charge. - (decision) SHOT LAYOUT TAKING SHAPE (author + analyst, not yet written to addendum - holding until layout settles): (1) SPINNER ON THE LOOP confirmed by author. (2) TWO LOOPS, left and right, plus a RAMP. Analyst proposal: the joust ALTERNATES loops (left-right-left), same loop twice breaks the charge - physically what a joust looks like and harder than hammering one shot; spinner on ONE loop only so the two orbits are not the same shot. Ramp maps to the researched skeleton's QUALIFIER role. Shot mix is healthy per Lawlor: loops and ramp are flow, targets and mouth are stop-and-go. Layout rules from research to carry: orbit exits feed the flippers, guides end at rubber posts, a missed loop must come back playable.
- (decision) DRAGON PLACEMENT: OFF-CENTRE BASH TOY (author). Design reasoning to record: off-centre is the right call for three researched reasons - (a) Lawlor's miss test: a DEAD-CENTRE bash toy rejects balls straight down the middle, an off-centre one deflects rejections toward a flipper; (b) it creates a dominant flipper and a BACKHAND from the other, and the research found flipper friction 0.8-0.9 is precisely what makes backhands possible - so the geometry rewards the physics fidelity being built; (c) bash toys are the modern standard (CGC Pulp Fiction briefcase, Cactus Canyon Bart, Godzilla). CONSEQUENCE FOR THE DEVICE MODEL: since balls FIRE FROM THE MOUTH, the mouth is an EJECT device in MPF terms (kickout/VUK), not just a target - it needs a ball_device behind it, and the physical-vs-virtual lock question from the research applies. Open: which side, and whether locked balls physically live behind the dragon or the mouth is fed from the trough via a subway.
- (decision) DRAGON RESOLVED. Side: right flipper takes the dragon straight, left flipper backhands it (author confirmed). Lock: PHYSICAL, balls shot BETWEEN THE DRAGON'S LEGS. Design consequences: the dragon now carries TWO shots in one lane - a slightly-off shot hits the body (bash), a precise shot goes under (lock) - a skill gradient by precision rather than by separate geometry. Balls enter the lock below, are held physically (MPF ball_hold/multiball_lock, ball_device), and on multiball start a VUK lifts and fires them out the MOUTH as dragon fire - the research's VUK definition ('launches the ball vertically') is exactly this device. Lock must be DISABLED during multiball so under-leg shots count as bash hits, not re-locks. Ball-count grammar: lock 2 + ball in play = 3-ball war; 4-ball reserved for a later finale.
