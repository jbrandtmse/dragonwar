- (decision) STAKES READ: passion project, not commercial. Goal is a playable game for the user himself, shared as OPEN SOURCE. Calibrate brief accordingly - no investor-grade market rigor, no fabricated moat. Success is defined by the builder's own satisfaction plus whatever the open-source release earns.
- (decision) SEED IMAGE / creative origin: user recently played a table where lighting D-R-A-G-O-N targets triggered a multiball in which the dragon FIRES THE BALLS FROM ITS MOUTH as dragon fire. He wants that moment in his game. This is the emotional center of the concept - a specific feeling being chased, not a feature list. Treat as the brief's anchor.
- (event) MATERIAL CONSEQUENCE OF OPEN-SOURCE INTENT: the technical research's single biggest risk was licensing - vpinball defaults to non-commercial for unmarked files and vpx-js is GPL-2.0, which blocked a closed-source commercial build. An open-source DragonWar can be GPL-compatible, which UNBLOCKS porting vpx-js's proven analytic-TOI physics core. This removes the largest single work item's largest risk. Must be surfaced to the user and reflected in the brief's scope.
- (decision) SCOPE LOCKED: ONE table only, DragonWar. Explicitly NOT an engine or platform hosting many tables. This is the single most important scope decision in the brief and it is what makes the project achievable solo.
- (decision) USER'S DEFINITION OF REALISTIC, in his own words - this is the acceptance criterion, replacing the undefined industry term: (1) it should FEEL like real pinball and stay playable; (2) PRE-GAME PRESENTATION - you walk up to a real machine, see the backbox/backboard, then the camera zooms down onto the playfield; (3) lights and sounds of a real machine; (4) correct ball physics - bouncing, rebounding, SOMETIMES HOPPING. Note the walk-up-and-zoom moment is a specific, cheap, high-impact piece of choreography and should be treated as a first-class feature, not polish.
- (decision) RULES CORE: a multiball 'WAR' - scoring against the dragon enough times WINS THE WAR and collects the jackpot. Plus additional feature events so the table is not a single-facet game. Combined with the earlier seed (D-R-A-G-O-N targets light the multiball, balls fired from the dragon's mouth as fire), the rules spine is: spell DRAGON -> dragon-fire multiball -> damage the dragon -> win the war -> jackpot, with other modes layered alongside. Maps cleanly onto the researched Stern skeleton (qualifier -> selector -> tiered modes -> wizard).
- (decision) WORKING MODE: fast path, with assumptions surfaced for resolution rather than silently tagged. User asked to be asked.
- (assumption) TENSION TO FLAG IN BRIEF: user wants the ball to 'sometimes hop' (airballs). The research found that VPX's elasticity-falloff parameter exists specifically to SUPPRESS pingy rebounds and airballs, and that community tuning drives scatter to zero. Hops are therefore a deliberate tuning target to dial IN, not a free by-product - and too many hops is the classic 'too fast / pingy' complaint. Worth one explicit tuning knob rather than an accident.
- (decision) FOUR ASSUMPTIONS RESOLVED BY USER: (1) ART = STYLIZED / LOW-POLY, chosen over the parts-library route and over full original modelling; (2) PLAYERS = 1-4 hot seat, matching a real machine, which fixes per-player state in the rules layer from day one; (3) DISTRIBUTION = BROWSER-FIRST, overriding the research's native-first recommendation because that recommendation was reasoning about a COMMERCIAL product and this is open source, where click-and-play is the whole share story; (4) NUDGE = full nudge plus tilt warnings plus slam tilt, in scope for v1.
- (assumption) TENSION THE USER SHOULD SEE: the stylized/low-poly art choice works against his own stated goal that it should feel like walking up to a REAL machine. Proposed reconciliation to be offered - STYLIZED REALISM: correct dimensions, correct materials, correct lighting behaviour and correct ball behaviour, but simplified geometry and hand-painted textures rather than photoreal scans. Justified by the research: geometry is NOT the bottleneck (single LOD, small fixed near-field scene, 500-2000 tris for standard playfield objects), LIGHTING AND TEXTURE MEMORY are. A low-poly playfield with correct insert glow, correct GI and correct ball physics will read as more authentically pinball than a high-poly one with wrong lighting. Drafted into the brief under this reading, tagged as an assumption for the user to correct.
- (assumption) UNANSWERED AFTER TWO ASKS: whether the user plays real pinball or has physical access to a machine to tune against. Not pressed further - flagged as an open assumption in the brief instead, because it determines whether feel can be validated against a reference or only against memory.
- (decision) THEME RESOLVED, and it is strong: THE PLAYER IS A KNIGHT, THE WAR IS THE MULTIBALL, AND THE FLIPPERS ARE THE WEAPON. This is thematically coherent and mechanically literal at the same time - every flip during dragon-fire multiball is a strike against the dragon, so the core verb of pinball IS the core verb of the fiction. It also explains why the war lives in multiball specifically rather than being a separate mode. Fold into the brief as the theme spine; it drives art, callouts and mode naming, which were previously blank.
- (decision) LICENCE: user accepts GPL-2.0 provided the result is free to distribute - which GPL-2.0 guarantees by design. POTENTIAL PROBLEM TO VERIFY BEFORE THIS IS SETTLED: Babylon.js is Apache-2.0, and Apache-2.0 is INCOMPATIBLE with GPL-2.0-ONLY (though compatible with GPL-3.0). If vpx-js is GPL-2.0-only rather than GPL-2.0-or-later, porting its physics into a Babylon.js game is a licence conflict. Checking the actual vpx-js licence declaration and Babylon's licence now.
- (decision) LICENCE RESOLVED TO GPL-3.0, and the verification was load-bearing. Verified this session: (a) vpx-js source file headers (lib/physics/hit-object.ts) read 'either version 2 of the License, or (at your option) any later version' = GPL-2.0-OR-LATER, even though package.json declares only 'GPL-2.0' and the LICENSE file carries the GPLv2 text; (b) Babylon.js is Apache-2.0 per the GitHub API (spdx_id Apache-2.0). Apache-2.0 is INCOMPATIBLE with GPL-2.0-only but COMPATIBLE with GPL-3.0. The or-later clause therefore permits taking the ported physics core to GPL-3.0, where Babylon fits legally. HAD vpx-js BEEN GPL-2.0-ONLY, the chosen renderer and the chosen physics core could not have shipped together. Final stack licensing: DragonWar GPL-3.0, Babylon.js Apache-2.0, MPF ontology MIT - all compatible. User's requirement (free to distribute) is satisfied by GPL-3.0 by design.

- (event) OPEN QUESTION RAISED BY THE RESOLUTION: which machine is at the bar, and from which era? The research established that flipper strength is era-banded (EM 500-1000, late70s-mid80s 1400-1600, mid80s-early90s 2000-2600, mid90s+ 3200-3300 in VPX units) and that Marco publishes a five-tier coil ordering. Calibrating DragonWar against a specific reference machine means inheriting that machine's era feel - which is fine, but should be a deliberate choice rather than an accident. Worth capturing in the PRD.
